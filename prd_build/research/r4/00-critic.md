# [r4] critic

## assessment

OVERALL: This is unusually good research for a PRD input — it has been through adversarial passes (7, 8, 11 and 7 retractions across the four dimensions), it corrects several errors that are circulating widely in Indian practitioner commentary, and it flags a foreign-law citation landmine (the Turkish KVKK decision) that would have been genuinely embarrassing in front of a customer's lawyer. The single most valuable output is the commencement analysis: almost nothing in DPDP binds this product today, and the live obligations are CERT-In and the SPDI Rules 2011. Most competitor PRDs get this backwards.

But the four dimensions were clearly run independently and they have NOT been reconciled. There are at least four places where one dimension states something at [high] confidence that another dimension flags as an open counsel question, and two of those have direct architectural consequences (rule 8(3) retention floor; processor penalty exposure). If these are copied into a PRD as written, the document will contradict itself, and a lawyer reading it will find the contradiction before finding anything else.

WHERE THE RESEARCH IS GENUINELY THIN — say this plainly rather than papering over it:
(1) Indian anti-discrimination law as applied to AI-assisted hiring was NOT RESEARCHED IN EITHER ROUND. Dimension 4 admits this. For an AI-first HR product doing shortlisting and performance scoring, this is the largest unexamined substantive legal risk in the whole set — larger than most of what was researched — because it is the one most likely to produce litigation and a headline. The RPwD Act 2016 imposes duties on private establishments; nobody looked.
(2) The SEBI cloud circular was never retrieved. If the MeitY-empanelment claim in the secondary sources is right, it is a harder constraint on a US-hosted LLM than anything in DPDP, for any capital-markets customer.
(3) IRDAI: not researched at all.
(4) The labour/tax retention schedule is flagged as needed in three separate dimensions and never produced. A P1 requirement (retention-policy engine) depends on it. The engine can be built; the periods cannot be stated.
(5) Several load-bearing findings rest on single unverifiable sources: the EPFO 30.07.2025 UMANG/Face-Auth circular (an entire P0 onboarding redesign hangs off it, and nobody could retrieve post-November-2025 EPFO guidance); the MeitY 2011 press note (one dimension characterises it, another could not retrieve it at all); the OSH Rules 2026 rule 72 retention period (aggregator-only); the Income-tax Act 2025 section number (four secondary sources, no primary).
(6) Nobody could prove the central negative — that no amendment was gazetted between November 2025 and September 2026 — because meity.gov.in and indiacode.nic.in return 403 to programmatic fetch. Every date in this PRD sits on that unproven negative.

A structural warning the research does not make: the interregnum creates a MISREPRESENTATION risk that is separate from any compliance risk. Between now and ~May 2027, marketing and DPAs will want to say "DPDP compliant." Given that DPDP is not in force, that biometrics are sensitive under a different instrument, and that several classifications here are contested, a PRD statement that overstates the law becomes a contractual representation the company has to stand behind. Treat customer-facing legal claims as product surface with an owner, not as collateral.

## safe_to_state_in_prd

- COMMENCEMENT: The substantive DPDP obligations (ss.3-17 and the penalty provisions ss.28-34) are not in force. They commence at the eighteen-month mark, on or about 13 May 2027. Cite DPDP Act 2023 s.1(2) read with G.S.R. 843(E) dated 13.11.2025; DPDP Rules 2025 r.1(2)-(4) per G.S.R. 846(E). WRITE 'on or about 13 May 2027' — do not pin a single date. Dimension 1 correctly flags that 13 vs 14 May is unresolved and dimensions 3 and 4 both assert '13 May 2027' as certain. Do not inherit that false precision.
- The twelve-month tranche (November 2026) commences only Consent Manager registration and Consent-Manager-specific enforcement. It creates no obligation for an HR SaaS or its employer customers. Cite G.S.R. 843(E) tranche (b); DPDP Act s.6(9), s.27(1)(d); DPDP Rules r.4. This is worth stating affirmatively because the contrary claim — that the Board can inquire and penalise from 13 November 2026 — is circulating widely and a customer's lawyer may arrive believing it.
- DPDP creates NO special or sensitive category of personal data. Aadhaar numbers, PAN, bank details, salary and biometric face templates attract identical obligations to a work email address. Cite s.2(t) and the verified absence of any sensitive-category provision across the Act and Rules. MANDATORY PAIRING: state in the same paragraph that this does not mean biometrics are unregulated, because the SPDI Rules 2011 are in force and do classify them as sensitive. Stating either half alone misleads.
- The SPDI Rules 2011 remain in force and classify biometric information and financial information as sensitive personal data. Cite IT (Reasonable Security Practices...) Rules 2011, G.S.R. 313(E) dated 11.04.2011, rr.2(1)(b), 3(ii), 3(vi), read with IT Act 2000 s.43A. Note that DPDP s.44(2), which omits s.43A, is itself in the eighteen-month tranche. Flag the provenance caveat: dimension 4 could not locate a live .gov.in copy of G.S.R. 313(E). Re-verify before quoting the text verbatim to counsel.
- CERT-In Directions No. 20(3)/2022-CERT-In dated 28.04.2022 are IN FORCE NOW: incident reporting within 6 HOURS of becoming aware (direction (ii)), and 180 days of ICT system logs maintained within Indian jurisdiction (direction (iv)). Annexure I expressly includes IoT device incidents (xiii), cloud systems (xviii) and attacks on AI/ML systems (xx). Issued under IT Act s.70B(6). This is the best-verified item in the entire research set — retrieved from the CERT-In domain — and it is the only live breach clock.
- IT Act s.43A compensation is live today and carries NO statutory cap. State this alongside the Rs 250 crore DPDP figure, because the intuition is inverted: today's actual exposure for a biometric or financial-data breach is uncapped compensation under a live provision, while the headline DPDP number is not yet enforceable. Cite IT Act 2000 ss.43A, 45, 46(1A).
- DPDP contains no right against automated decision-making, no right to an explanation, and no right to human review of an algorithmic decision. Chapter III contains exactly four rights: access (s.11), correction and erasure (s.12), grievance redressal (s.13), nomination (s.14). Cite Chapter III ss.11-15. This is a verified negative and the largest India-versus-GDPR divergence for an AI-first HR product.
- DPDP imposes no data-localisation mandate. s.16 uses a negative list — transfer is permitted by default unless a country is notified — and s.16 is not in force in any event. There is no adequacy regime, no SCC equivalent and no transfer-impact-assessment requirement in the Act. Cite s.16(1)-(2) and DPDP Rules r.15. PAIR THIS with the CERT-In 180-day in-India log requirement, which IS a live residency constraint, or the statement misleads in the other direction.
- Aadhaar Act s.57 was omitted entirely by the 2019 Amendment. A private entity cannot perform Aadhaar authentication without going through the permission-gated route (parliamentary law or prescribed purpose) or the Good Governance (SWIK) Rules application route. Cite s.57 as omitted by Act 14 of 2019 s.25 w.e.f. 25.07.2019; ss.4(3), 4(4).
- A registered Offline Verification Seeking Entity may not collect, use or store an Aadhaar number for any purpose, with no consent exception. Cite Aadhaar Act s.8A(4)(b). This is the sharpest single finding in the research and it has a corporate-structure consequence nobody stated: storing Aadhaar numbers and holding OVSE registration cannot lawfully coexist in one legal entity. If the roadmap wants both, that is a subsidiary decision made now, not a feature flag later.
- An entity may not perform Aadhaar offline verification on behalf of another entity, and an e-KYC licence key may not be shared (only Yes/No authentication may be extended to a third party). Cite AOVR 2021 regs. 16A(2), 15(1), 15(2), 15(5). This kills any 'we verify Aadhaar for your employees' feature outright — a scope decision, not a policy document.
- Virtual ID may not be stored by any entity. Cite AOVR 2021 reg. 4A(4). VID is therefore not a lawful substitute store for the Aadhaar number.
- Aadhaar (Sharing of Information) Regulations 2016 reg. 6 imposes four directly implementable duties on ANY entity holding Aadhaar numbers, including a plain employer and its SaaS: security and confidentiality of the database (6(2)); redaction before any database is made public (6(3)); encrypted transmission over the Internet (6(4)); and a retention ceiling tied to the consented purpose (6(5)). Reg. 7 deems contravention of regs 3-6 a violation of Act s.29(2). Cite regs 6(1)-(5) and 7.
- Regulation 5 of the Sharing of Information Regulations is the affirmative basis on which a non-requesting-entity employer may collect and store an employee's Aadhaar number, subject to three cumulative duties: lawful purpose; a three-part disclosure (purpose; whether mandatory or voluntary and if mandatory the mandating provision; alternatives); and consent. Cite reg. 5.
- There is no subsisting UIDAI prohibition on private entities holding Aadhaar photocopies. The widely-cited May 2022 advisory was withdrawn within 48 hours and must never be cited as law. Cite UIDAI Bengaluru RO press release 27.05.2022, withdrawn by PIB/MeitY release 29.05.2022. Worth stating affirmatively because customers will raise it.
- PAN-Aadhaar linkage is an obligation of the individual taxpayer, not of the employer. There is no employer-facing duty to collect Aadhaar for income-tax purposes and no employer-facing penalty; the sanction is that the individual's PAN becomes inoperative. STATE THE PROPOSITION, OMIT THE SECTION NUMBER — the Income-tax Act 2025 s.262(5)-(7) citation could not be verified at primary source in two attempts.
- Aadhaar Act s.29 restrictions bite on information 'collected or created under this Act'. A face template captured by an employer's own attendance terminal is neither collected under the Act nor 'core biometric information' as defined. Cite ss.2(g), 2(j), 29(1). PRECISION NOTE: a photograph IS 'biometric information' under s.2(g) — it is merely outside the narrower 'core biometric information' in s.2(j). Do not write 'a face template is not biometric information under the Aadhaar Act'; write that it is not core biometric information and in any event was not collected under the Act.
- No Indian law requires a private employer to use biometric attendance. Labour law requires an attendance RECORD and is expressly device-neutral, so the biometric template is a means of authentication and not the legally required record. Cite OSH Code 2020 s.33(a). DO NOT state the retention period for that record — see the must-not list.
- The DPDP Rules Third Schedule three-year inactivity erasure applies only to specified large e-commerce, online gaming and social media intermediaries. It reaches neither this product nor a 20-200 employee customer. Cite r.8(1)-(2) with the Third Schedule. This is a safe de-scoping statement and building the feature would actively delete records employers must keep.
- Significant Data Fiduciary status arises only by affirmative Central Government notification under s.10(1). There is no automatic threshold, and s.10 is not in force. Cite s.10(1); DPDP Rules r.13(1)-(5).
- DPDP breach notification, when it commences, has no materiality threshold and no de minimis filter — every personal data breach is notifiable to both the Board and every affected data principal. Cite DPDP Rules r.7(1)-(2); Act s.8(6) and s.2(u). Safe as a statement about the text, with the not-in-force caveat.
- DPDP Rules r.6(1)(a) names encryption, obfuscation, masking and virtual tokens as examples of the required minimum safeguards, and r.6(1)(c) requires logging with the capability to DETECT unauthorised access, not merely to record it. Cite r.6(1)(a) and (c), with the not-in-force caveat. This is the most build-relevant text in the framework.
- The India AI Governance Guidelines (MeitY, November 2025) are expressly voluntary and non-statutory, and expressly recommend against a separate AI law. There is no binding Indian regulation governing AI in hiring or performance management.
- The IT Amendment Rules 2026 synthetically-generated-information regime covers only audio, visual or audio-visual information, with a proviso carving out routine document, presentation and training-material generation. Cite IT Rules 2021 r.2(1)(wa) and r.3(3) as amended by G.S.R. 120(E) dated 10.02.2026. Safe as a statement of scope; whether the product is an 'intermediary' is a separate counsel question.
- SAFE INTERSECTION ON SPDI: whichever way the 2011 press-note carve-out resolves, SPDI r.7 (restricted cross-border transfer of sensitive personal information) and r.8 (reasonable security practices) bind the SaaS today. State that floor. It is true on both readings and it avoids the contested r.5/r.6 allocation entirely.

## must_not_be_stated_without_counsel

- #1 — WHETHER EMPLOYEES HAVE DPDP ACCESS, CORRECTION OR ERASURE RIGHTS AT ALL against an employer relying on s.7(i). Both ss.11(1) and 12(1) are textually limited to processing for which consent was given 'including consent as referred to in clause (a) of section 7', and r.14(2) repeats the qualifier — so the drafting is consistent across both instruments, not a slip. All four dimensions independently reach this and all four flag it as their top counsel question. BOTH DIRECTIONS ARE DANGEROUS: asserting the rights exist creates an obligation the law may not impose; asserting they do not exist tells a customer they can refuse an employee request, which is a catastrophic thing to be wrong about. PRD language: build the machinery, describe it as a product capability and an enterprise-buyer requirement, and say the statutory basis is under review by counsel.
- THAT BIOMETRIC ATTENDANCE CAPTURE IS AUTHORISED BY s.7(i) 'for the purposes of employment'. There is no Indian case law, no Board decision and no MeitY guidance. The unresolved question is not whether attendance is an employment purpose — it plainly is — but whether choosing the MOST INTRUSIVE available means requires consent or a proportionality justification under Puttaswamy. Point counsel at the Turkish KVKK Principle Decision 2026/921 as a worked example of the adverse argument, clearly labelled as persuasive-only foreign law. This is the central legal risk of the attendance module and there is nothing to hide behind.
- THAT THE SaaS BEARS NO DIRECT STATUTORY PENALTY EXPOSURE AS A PROCESSOR. Dimension 4 states this at [high]; dimension 1 flags it as genuinely unresolved and is right to. s.33(1) permits penalty where 'a person' commits a significant breach, Schedule item 7 is a residual catch-all, and the SaaS may be a Data Fiduciary in its own right for processing it self-determines (choosing to route data to an LLM, selecting the vendor, using aggregate customer data for model improvement). This claim will go straight into DPA negotiation, indemnity scope and the liability cap. Being wrong here is directly and immediately expensive.
- THAT THE MeitY PRESS NOTE OF 24 AUGUST 2011 EXEMPTS THE HR SaaS FROM SPDI RULES 5 AND 6. A press note is an executive clarification, not a statutory instrument, and its legal weight is itself contestable. Worse, the provenance is broken: dimension 4 characterises the note's content while dimension 3 states it could not retrieve it at primary source (PIB returns 403). Do not build a compliance posture on a document the research could not read.
- WHO OWES THE WRITTEN-CONSENT DUTY FOR BIOMETRIC ENROLMENT TODAY — the employer or the SaaS. Same root as the press-note question. Dimensions 1 and 3 treat SPDI r.5(1) written consent as a live obligation driving P0 build; dimension 4 says the SaaS is carved out. Note the practical resolution: the product must provide the consent-capture machinery either way, because if the SaaS is carved out the duty simply relocates to the customer. So the ARCHITECTURE is unaffected and only the LEGAL REPRESENTATION changes. Write the PRD requirement; do not write who the law obliges.
- THAT MASKING AN AADHAAR NUMBER IS A STATUTORY OBLIGATION ON A PLAIN EMPLOYER, OR THAT REG 6(1) PROHIBITS INTERNAL DISPLAY. Dimension 2 correctly says the 8-digit masking rule in AOVR r.14(1)(mb) binds requesting entities only — but its own requirement then hooks 'never render a full Aadhaar in any UI, export, log or support tool' onto Sharing Reg 6(1). Reg 6(1) prohibits Aadhaar being 'published, displayed or posted PUBLICLY'. It does not on its face govern showing the number to an authorised HR admin inside the application. The build requirement is right; the citation offered does not carry it. Ground it in reg 6(2) general security and in risk mitigation, never as a statutory display prohibition. A customer's counsel will catch this and it will discredit the rest of the document.
- THAT THE AADHAAR DATA VAULT / HSM REGIME DOES NOT BIND A NON-REQUESTING ENTITY. This rests on UIDAI Circular 14 of 2025 and an FAQ, not on a notified regulation, and UIDAI's position has already flipped once (the 2017 position extended ADV to 'other entities' storing Aadhaar numbers). The cost delta if it flips back is large — on-premises or MeitY-empanelled GCC hosting, HSM, annual SOC 2 Type II by a CERT-In empanelled auditor. Get a written opinion before freezing the architecture.
- THAT AN EMPLOYER TYPING AN AADHAAR NUMBER INTO THE EPFO OR ESIC PORTAL IS NOT A 'REQUESTING ENTITY'. s.2(u) reads 'an agency or person that submits the Aadhaar number... to the Central Identities Data Repository for authentication'. The submission goes to a government portal which then hits the CIDR. Dimension 2's reading that this does not make the employer an RE is probably right, but the wording is uncomfortably broad and the entire RE stack (reg 14, ADV, HSM, audits, Rs 1 crore civil penalty regime) hangs off it. Several P0 requirements assume the answer.
- WHETHER THE CHAIN [breach of Sharing Regs 5-6] → [deemed s.29(2) violation via reg 7] → [criminal liability under s.38(g)] HOLDS. s.38 is headed 'Penalty for unauthorised access to the CIDR', opens 'Whoever, not being authorised by the Authority', and every other limb is CIDR-centric — which may contextually confine limb (g). If the chain holds, a mishandled employer Aadhaar database carries imprisonment up to TEN YEARS and a minimum Rs 10 lakh fine, and s.43 reaches company officers personally. Do not characterise this either way in the PRD. Build as though it holds; say nothing about whether it does.
- THAT DPDP RULE 8(3) IMPOSES A UNIVERSAL ONE-YEAR MINIMUM RETENTION ON EVERY DATA FIDUCIARY. Dimension 1 asserts this at [high] and builds a P0 soft-delete requirement on it. Dimension 3 says the scope is genuinely ambiguous at [medium] — the operative words point to Seventh Schedule State-access and SDF-assessment purposes while only the illustration suggests general application. Dimension 3's reading is the more careful one. This is not a drafting quibble: if the floor is universal, erase-on-exit for biometrics is unlawful for a year; if it is Schedule-scoped, immediate purge is fine and arguably required by purpose limitation. Do not state either reading, and do not hard-code either behaviour.
- THAT AN EMPLOYER MAY MAKE AADHAAR SUBMISSION OR BIOMETRIC ENROLMENT A CONDITION OF EMPLOYMENT, PAYROLL ENROLMENT OR ANY BENEFIT. The picture is genuinely mixed: CoSS s.142 is drafted as an obligation on the EMPLOYEE; MoLE clarified on notification that no benefit would be denied for want of Aadhaar; s.4(6) and AOVR r.5(3) forbid denial of service but are scoped to REs and OVSEs; SPDI r.5(7) expressly preserves the body corporate's option to withhold the service; and EPFO's ECR seeding instruction is administrative, not statutory. Beyond not stating it — DO NOT SHIP THE HARD-BLOCK CONFIGURATION. If the product provides a 'no Aadhaar, no payroll' or 'biometric only, no alternative' switch, the vendor has built the instrument of every customer's violation.
- ANY STATUTORY RETENTION PERIOD for payroll records, statutory registers, PF/ESI records, Form 16 data or attendance registers. This was flagged as needed in three dimensions and produced in none. The 'five calendar years from date of last entry' figure for OSH (Central) Rules 2026 r.72 comes from professional aggregators only — the gazette (G.S.R. 345(E), 08.05.2026) could not be reached. Build the retention-policy engine; leave the periods as configuration populated by counsel.
- THE INCOME-TAX ACT 2025 SECTION NUMBER for PAN-Aadhaar linkage. Four secondary sources agree on s.262(5)-(7) and the substantive conclusion is robust, but incometaxindia.gov.in returns 403, indiacode bitstream paths 404, and there is no Wayback snapshot. State the proposition without the citation.
- WHETHER THE SPDI RULES 2011 SURVIVE THE OMISSION OF IT ACT s.43A on the eighteen-month date. They are not expressly repealed by any instrument. Whether they fall with their parent provision or are saved (e.g. by General Clauses Act s.6A) is a live construction question that determines whether biometrics remain legally 'sensitive' in India after that date — and therefore whether the product's whole SPDI-regime behaviour set has an end date or not. Related: Aadhaar Act s.30 defines sensitive personal data by express cross-reference to 'clause (iii) of the Explanation to section 43A', which will point at a repealed provision.
- WHETHER AN HR/PAYROLL SaaS IS AN 'INTERMEDIARY' UNDER IT ACT s.2(1)(w). If it is, the IT Rules 2021 due-diligence obligations attach and the 2026 SGI amendments become live the moment any audio-visual generation feature ships.
- WHETHER HR/PAYROLL SaaS IS 'MATERIAL' IT OUTSOURCING FOR A GIVEN RBI-REGULATED ENTITY. The para 16(r) sub-contractor-consent duty and the para 16(o) regulator-inspection duty only bite if the arrangement is material, and that is an RE-by-RE determination that will recur in every BFSI sales cycle. Also note the RBI citations are mirror-sourced — rbidocs.rbi.org.in refused direct access.
- ANYTHING ABOUT SEBI CLOUD REQUIREMENTS. The circular (SEBI/HO/ITD/ITD_VAPT/P/CIR/2023/033, 06.03.2023) was never retrieved; the NSDL mirror 404'd. The reported MeitY-empanelment and STQC-audit requirement, if true, is a harder constraint on a US-hosted LLM than anything in DPDP. State nothing until the circular is read. IRDAI was not researched at all.
- ANYTHING ABOUT AI, HIRING AND DISCRIMINATION. Not researched in either round, and the research says so. Constitutional Arts. 14/15/16 bind the State, not private employers, but the RPwD Act 2016 and other statutes may create private-sector duties. For a product that will do AI-assisted shortlisting and performance scoring this is the biggest unexamined risk in the set. Do not write a reassuring sentence about it; write that it is an open workstream.
- WHETHER THE MeitY COMPRESSION PROPOSAL (18 months to 12) APPLIES TO ALL DATA FIDUCIARIES OR ONLY TO SDFs. Dimension 1 says the reporting is genuinely split and the primary source itself says it remains to be seen. Dimensions 3 and 4 resolve it as SDF-only and dimension 3 builds a requirement on that resolution ('do not build to a November 2026 date'). Dimension 1's caution is correct. If it applies across the board the deadline moves to ~13 November 2026 and the entire build sequence changes.
- THAT HOSTING AADHAAR NUMBERS ON NON-INDIAN INFRASTRUCTURE, OR EXPOSING THEM TO A FOREIGN SUB-PROCESSOR, IS LAWFUL FOR A NON-REQUESTING ENTITY. No express localisation rule was found for non-REs, but absence of a rule is not permission, Aadhaar s.44 has extraterritorial reach, and this interacts with SPDI r.7 which IS live and IS restrictive for sensitive data.
- ALL UNPROVABLE NEGATIVES — that no country is notified under s.16(1), that no SDF has been designated under s.10(1), that no s.17(3) startup exemption has issued, that no amendment was gazetted between November 2025 and September 2026, that UIDAI has never imposed a s.33A penalty on a private entity, that the Data Protection Board is unstaffed. Every one of these was inferred from failed searches against government sites returning 403/404. Phrase each as 'we are not aware of any...' with a stated verification date and a re-check owner. Never as 'there is no...'.

## contradictions

- ACROSS DIMENSIONS — RULE 8(3) RETENTION FLOOR. Dimension 1: 'Rule 8(3) imposes a universal one-year MINIMUM retention on every Data Fiduciary' [high, law_requires], and makes it a P0 requiring soft-delete-plus-suppression for a year. Dimension 3: scope is 'genuinely ambiguous' [medium], operative words point only to Seventh Schedule purposes while the illustration suggests general application, and makes it P1 with 'do not hard-code immediate purge until settled'. This is the most architecturally consequential disagreement in the set: it determines whether a departing employee's biometric template can be destroyed on exit day or must be suppressed for twelve months. Dimension 3's reading is the more careful one.
- ACROSS DIMENSIONS — PROCESSOR PENALTY EXPOSURE. Dimension 4: 'All penalties fall on the Data Fiduciary — the employer — not on the SaaS' [high]. Dimension 1: flags as an open counsel question, noting s.33(1) says 'a person', Schedule item 7 is residual, and s.8(1) makes the fiduciary responsible 'irrespective of any agreement to the contrary' without thereby immunising the processor. Dimension 1 is right to be cautious. This one determines the company's own risk posture, insurance and liability cap.
- ACROSS DIMENSIONS — WHO OWES SPDI RULE 5 DUTIES. Dimension 3 makes 'capture explicit WRITTEN consent for biometric enrolment, required TODAY under SPDI r.5(1)' a P0 obligation of the product. Dimension 4 states as a CORRECTION that rr.5 and 6 bind the employer and not the SaaS, via the 2011 MeitY press note. Dimension 1 independently treats SPDI r.5(1) written consent as a hard architectural constraint. Compounding it: dimension 4 characterises the press note's content while dimension 3 says it could not retrieve the press note at all. One dimension is summarising a document another dimension could not open.
- ACROSS DIMENSIONS — COMPRESSION-PROPOSAL SCOPE. Dimension 1 treats it as genuinely unresolved and materially affecting the roadmap. Dimensions 3 and 4 resolve it as SDF-targeted and dimension 3 issues a build instruction premised on that resolution. Three-against-one is not a reason to prefer the majority here; dimension 1 is the only one that examined whether the reporting agrees, and found it does not.
- ACROSS DIMENSIONS — COMMENCEMENT DATE PRECISION. Dimension 1 flags 13 vs 14 May 2027 as unresolved and says explicitly 'do not state a single date as certain in a customer-facing document'. Dimensions 2, 3 and 4 all state '13 May 2027' flatly, repeatedly. Immaterial for planning, material for a document a lawyer reads.
- ACROSS DIMENSIONS — AADHAAR MASKING ON NON-RE HOLDERS. Dimension 2 researches this and answers it (no statutory masking duty on a plain employer; masking is risk mitigation). Dimension 4 leaves it as an unresolved open question bearing on its redaction gateway's default-deny list. Dimension 2's answer is the better-researched one, but dimension 2 then over-cites its own display requirement to reg 6(1), which is a PUBLIC-display prohibition and does not support a ban on internal display. So the same finding is simultaneously under-resolved in one dimension and over-stated in another.
- AGAINST READER PRIOR — 'DPDP HAS SENSITIVE CATEGORIES LIKE GDPR'. False, and all four dimensions agree. But the correction is a trap: it does NOT follow that biometrics are unregulated, because SPDI 2011 is live and does make them sensitive. The PRD must state both halves in the same breath or it will mislead a lawyer into relaxing controls that are currently required.
- AGAINST READER PRIOR — 'BIOMETRICS REQUIRE EXPLICIT CONSENT'. The truth is time-inverted from what everyone expects. TODAY consent in writing is required under SPDI r.5(1). FROM ~MAY 2027 s.7(i) may remove the consent requirement entirely — but whether it stretches to biometrics specifically is untested. So the belief is correct now and possibly wrong later, which is the opposite of the usual 'the law is getting stricter' assumption a reader brings.
- AGAINST READER PRIOR — 'INDIA MANDATES DATA LOCALISATION'. False for DPDP (negative list, empty, not in force). True in practical effect for CERT-In (180 days of ICT logs within Indian jurisdiction, live today), arguably true under SPDI r.7 for biometric and financial data, and possibly true under SEBI/RBI sectoral rules that were not verified. A PRD that says 'India has no localisation requirement' is wrong; one that says 'India requires localisation' is also wrong. Both halves, precisely scoped.
- AGAINST READER PRIOR — 'THE 72-HOUR BREACH CLOCK'. Everyone imports this from GDPR. The live Indian clock is SIX HOURS under CERT-In. DPDP's 72 hours is not in force, and even when it commences it is a detailed follow-up report — the initial intimation to the Board and to every affected employee is 'without delay'. A runbook built to 72 hours is non-compliant today.
- AGAINST READER PRIOR — 'THE BOARD CAN FINE FROM NOVEMBER 2026'. Widely repeated and false; dimension 1 corrects it explicitly. Only Consent Manager registration and Consent-Manager-specific penalties commence then.
- AGAINST READER PRIOR — 'PRIVATE ENTITIES CANNOT HOLD AADHAAR PHOTOCOPIES'. The May 2022 advisory was withdrawn within 48 hours. Still cited as live by many Indian practitioners and will come up in a customer security review.
- AGAINST READER PRIOR — 'THE SUPREME COURT UPHELD MANDATORY BIOMETRIC ATTENDANCE IN OCTOBER 2025'. The order in Dillip Kumar Rout turned on the employees not opposing it and decided nothing about privacy or data protection. Anyone who has read the trade press will arrive believing otherwise.
- CITATION LANDMINE — the 2026 'Principle Decision on the Processing of Biometric Data for Employee Attendance Tracking', which holds biometric attendance unlawful even with explicit consent, is TURKISH (KVKK 2026/921), not Indian. Dimension 3 notes that searches for Indian biometric-attendance guidance surface this decision instead. It has no force in India. This one is worth a standing rule in the PRD, because the failure mode is a well-meaning person pasting it in as authority.

## what_changes_the_architecture

- AADHAAR AS A SEPARATE, SEPARATELY-ENCRYPTED, SEPARATELY-ACCESS-CONTROLLED STORE, referenced everywhere by an opaque internal token — never the number, never a hash of it, in any business table. Schema-level and irreversible-in-practice: retrofitting means a data migration across every module plus an audit of every query. The research marks this P1; that is wrong on engineering grounds even if right on current legal grounds. Promote it. The ADV mandate has already flipped once and if it flips back this design turns a rewrite into a migration.
- BIOMETRIC TEMPLATE MODELLED AS A SEPARATE ENTITY FROM THE ATTENDANCE EVENT. An attendance record must reference employee id plus timestamp only, and must remain valid and readable after the template is destroyed. This is the architectural precondition for erase-on-exit without breaching labour-law record retention, and it is a schema decision that cannot be made later.
- TWO-PHASE DEVICE-SIDE TEMPLATE ERASURE WITH PER-DEVICE ACKNOWLEDGEMENT AND A RETRY QUEUE. Templates live in the flash of eSSL/Matrix-class terminals, not just on the server, so deletion is a distributed-systems problem: device registry, per-employee-per-device enrolment ledger, command queue, reconciliation job. NOBODY IN THE RESEARCH COSTED THIS AGAINST REALITY — these boxes sit on flaky office networks, behind NAT, powered off after hours. 'Do not report erasure complete until every device acknowledges' guarantees permanently-pending erasures and a compliance dashboard that is always red, which users then learn to ignore. The design needs a documented exception state (admin attestation of physical decommission or device loss) or the control is theatre.
- A MANDATORY REDACTION/TOKENISATION CHOKEPOINT ON EVERY OUTBOUND MODEL CALL, default-denying Aadhaar, PAN, bank account and IFSC, and raw biometric templates, with reversible tokens mapped server-side in India. This must be a structural constraint in the call graph, not a library developers are asked to use. If any AI feature can reach a model provider directly, you can never add this later without auditing every call site — and the failure mode is silent.
- LOG RESIDENCY SPLIT. CERT-In requires 180 days of ICT logs within Indian jurisdiction, live today. LLM prompt/completion logs are the component most likely to end up in a US region by default. This constrains cloud region topology and observability vendor choice (US-region Datadog/Sentry/logging pipelines become a problem) and it is a day-one infrastructure decision, not a later configuration.
- CONSENT AS A FIRST-CLASS VERSIONED ENTITY, capturing purpose, notice-text version, timestamp, actor, device, and — critically — WHOSE consent artefact it is (employer's or vendor's), because whether the SaaS is itself bound by Aadhaar Sharing Reg 5 is unresolved. Consent cannot be backfilled. For a 20-200 employee customer you cannot re-paper 200 employees retroactively, so getting the record shape wrong is unrecoverable. Add: the system must run TWO CONSENT REGIMES SIMULTANEOUSLY across the ~May 2027 boundary (SPDI written-consent behaviour and DPDP behaviour), which is more than the 'compliance-date switch' the research describes.
- SOFT-DELETE PLUS SUPPRESSION AND A RETENTION-POLICY ENGINE that can cite the legal ground on which it refuses an erasure request. Requires deleted_at/suppression/legal-hold in the schema and a policy layer keyed to statutory grounds, because s.8(7) and s.12(3) both yield to 'compliance with any law'. Build the engine; the actual periods are missing from the research and must come from counsel.
- SEPARATE KEYSPACE FOR BIOMETRIC TEMPLATES so template access is independently revocable and independently logged from ordinary HR data. Key-management architecture, not a config flag.
- DATA-ACCESS-LAYER ACCESS LOGGING WITH DETECTION, NOT JUST RECORDING. r.6(1)(c) requires the capability to detect unauthorised access, which implies alerting and periodic review, not a write-only audit table. Must sit at the data-access layer to be complete; sprinkled through controllers it will always have holes. Also doubles as the evidence for the Aadhaar s.43 officer due-diligence defence.
- NO IMAGE COLUMN, NO IMAGE BUCKET FOR BIOMETRICS. Enrolment images deleted immediately after template extraction, asserted as an architectural negative in the data model rather than as a retention policy someone can change.
- ATTENDANCE METHOD AS A PER-EMPLOYEE ATTRIBUTE with a genuine non-biometric path (card, PIN, geo, web punch, supervisor entry) selectable without manager override or penalty flagging. This is a data-model and product-surface requirement, not a policy statement, and it is the thing that makes the whole biometric module defensible.
- PER-EMPLOYEE AI DISCLOSURE RECORD written at every model call site — vendor, model, timestamp, purpose, categories of fields sent, exportable per employee. The research correctly downgrades its LEGAL justification (it is not compelled by s.11(1)(b) if that right is consent-gated) but the ENGINEERING conclusion is unchanged: instrument the call sites from day one, because retrofitting per-call provenance across a mature AI layer is brutal.
- PER-TENANT AI KILL SWITCH AND PER-FEATURE OPT-OUT, defaulting OFF for tenants flagged as RBI/SEBI/IRDAI-regulated. Requires tenant-tier awareness in the feature-flag system and a sub-processor-change gate wired to per-tenant consent state, because RBI para 16(r) makes adding an LLM vendor a consent event for those customers.
- PER-TENANT DATA RESIDENCY CONFIGURABILITY AND A SWAPPABLE INFERENCE PATH (in-region, in-VPC, or self-hosted). Deployment topology. Needed for SPDI r.7 today, for RBI para 16(o) inspection rights, for the unverified SEBI empanelment position, and as the hedge against r.13(4) localisation orders later.
- SIX-HOUR INCIDENT PIPELINE. Architectural, not procedural — a manual process cannot meet six hours. Requires detection tooling, pre-staged reporting fields, a registered CERT-In point of contact, and an AI/ML incident class (prompt injection, model or data poisoning, model extraction, unauthorised access to the inference pipeline or vector store) distinct from the general security path.
- CORPORATE STRUCTURE, NOT SOFTWARE, BUT DECIDED NOW: because s.8A(4)(b) bars a registered OVSE from storing Aadhaar numbers, offering Aadhaar offline verification and storing Aadhaar numbers cannot coexist in one legal entity. If both are on the roadmap that is a subsidiary decision made at design time. The research states the rule but never draws this consequence.

## highest_impact_product_requirements

- NOTE ON THE FRAMING: the items below create EXPOSURE — prosecution, uncapped damages, personal officer liability, or a false representation the company must stand behind — as distinct from audit findings. The critical and counterintuitive point is that MOST OF THE REAL EXPOSURE IS LIVE TODAY AND SITS IN THE AADHAAR AND CERT-IN REGIMES, NOT IN DPDP. The Rs 250 crore DPDP number that will dominate every stakeholder conversation is not enforceable until ~May 2027.
- AADHAAR HANDLING — CRIMINAL AND PERSONAL. Aadhaar s.38 carries imprisonment up to TEN YEARS and a fine of not less than Rs 10 lakh, and s.43 reaches company officers personally with only a due-diligence defence. Whether the reg 5/6 → reg 7 → s.38(g) chain actually holds is unresolved, but even at medium confidence, criminal exposure reaching named individuals is categorically different from a civil penalty and it is live now. Concretely: separate encrypted Aadhaar store with token references; encrypted transmission (reg 6(4) is a legal requirement, not a practice); purpose-bound automatic deletion (reg 6(5) is a hard ceiling); role-limited read access with actor-and-reason logging that EVIDENCES the s.43 due-diligence defence; and Aadhaar excluded by default from support tooling, analytics, warehouse, LLM and sub-processor flows. This is the highest-exposure cluster in the PRD and it is in the dimension most likely to be skimmed.
- NEVER SEND AADHAAR NUMBERS OR BIOMETRIC TEMPLATES TO A THIRD-PARTY MODEL PROVIDER. Under SPDI r.7 (live) this is a restricted transfer of sensitive personal information; under Aadhaar reg 6 and s.29(2) it is potentially the criminal chain above. Two live regimes, either of which is sufficient. This is a hard architectural constraint, enforced by the redaction gateway, not a guideline.
- DO NOT SHIP A HARD-BLOCK ON AADHAAR OR ON BIOMETRIC ENROLMENT. If the product offers 'no Aadhaar, no payroll' or 'biometric only, no alternative' as a configuration, the vendor has manufactured the instrument of every customer's violation, and has done so against a picture where MoLE has publicly said no benefit may be denied for want of Aadhaar and where no Indian authority permits imposing biometric attendance on an unwilling employee. Make Aadhaar optional everywhere including the EPF/ESI flow; ship a first-class non-biometric attendance path. This is a product-shape decision that cannot be walked back once customers have configured around it.
- THE SIX-HOUR CERT-IN CLOCK. Live today, under IT Act s.70B(6), with statutory consequences for non-compliance. Attendance terminals are IoT devices (Annexure I xiii), cloud systems are (xviii), and attacks on AI/ML systems are (xx) — so this product has three separate reportable surfaces. Build one pipeline to six hours that pre-stages the DPDP fields for 2027. A runbook built to 72 hours is non-compliant on day one, and this is the single most common miss in Indian SaaS.
- BIOMETRIC WRITTEN CONSENT AND THE ERASE-ON-EXIT CHAIN. Today's exposure for a biometric breach runs through IT Act s.43A compensation, which is UNCAPPED — larger in practical terms than the not-yet-enforceable DPDP figure. The requirement set is: written consent captured separately from the employment contract and exportable as evidence; templates only, never raw images; template separated from the attendance event so records survive deletion; device-side erasure with acknowledgement. Consent cannot be backfilled and the schema separation cannot be retrofitted, so both are one-way doors.
- THE MULTI-TENANT AADHAAR VERIFICATION FEATURE MUST NOT BE BUILT. AOVR reg 16A(2) is an express, unqualified bar on performing offline verification on behalf of another entity, and reg 15(2) prohibits sharing an e-KYC licence key. This is a scope decision worth stating loudly in the PRD because it is exactly the feature a competitive-analysis exercise will demand, and the lawful alternatives (plain QR-code / e-Aadhaar verification, which UIDAI says needs no registration) look superficially similar.
- THE EPF ONBOARDING REDESIGN — HIGH IMPACT, THIN EVIDENCE. Since 1 August 2025, UAN allotment and activation is employee-driven via Aadhaar Face Authentication in UMANG, with employer-side generation surviving only for International Workers and Nepal/Bhutan citizens. If correct, this is the single largest reduction in the product's Aadhaar surface area and it changes the onboarding flow fundamentally. BUT the entire P0 rests on one EPFO circular, and the research could not retrieve any post-November-2025 EPFO guidance. Verify before building the flow; if the circular has been superseded the onboarding design is wrong.
- REPRESENTATION CONTROL — TREAT CUSTOMER-FACING LEGAL CLAIMS AS PRODUCT SURFACE WITH AN OWNER. The exposure here is contractual, not regulatory: a PRD, DPA or security whitepaper that says 'biometric data is sensitive personal data under the DPDP Act', or warrants compliance with obligations that are not in force, or asserts that employees hold statutory access rights, is a misrepresentation a customer may rely on. Concrete rules: never describe anything as sensitive 'under DPDP'; cite SPDI r.3(vi) instead and note its scheduled lapse; and adopt the citation-hygiene rule that no foreign decision may be cited without naming its jurisdiction in the same sentence (the Turkish KVKK decision is the live hazard).
- RBI SUB-PROCESSOR CONSENT FOR REGULATED CUSTOMERS. Para 16(r) requires the regulated entity's prior consent before a service provider uses a sub-contractor, and para 16(o) requires RBI access to sub-contractor-held data. Silently adding or swapping an LLM vendor puts a bank or NBFC customer in breach of its own outsourcing obligations, and that returns as contractual liability. The gate must be technical (per-tenant sub-processor state blocking the feature) rather than a promise in a policy document, because the failure mode is an ordinary infrastructure change nobody flags.
- THE UNRESEARCHED ONE THAT BELONGS ON THIS LIST ANYWAY: AI-assisted shortlisting and performance scoring against Indian anti-discrimination law. Not researched in either round. There is no Indian right to explanation or human review, which removes the compliance driver but not the litigation risk. Human-in-the-loop with a recorded human decision-maker for any AI output feeding hiring, appraisal, promotion, PIP or termination should be built — as a defensibility control, explicitly NOT represented to customers as a legal requirement — and the legal question should be opened as a workstream rather than answered with a reassuring sentence.
