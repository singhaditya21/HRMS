## Key findings (24)

### 1. [high|law_requires] Section 57 of the Aadhaar Act was OMITTED entirely by the 2019 Amendment Act, not restored. The replacement is a narrower permission-gated route: s.4(4) authentication with UIDAI satisfaction plus either a parliamentary-law permission or a Central Government prescribed purpose, and s.8A offline verification.

**Provision:** Aadhaar Act 2016 s.57 (omitted by Act 14 of 2019 s.25 w.e.f. 25-7-2019); ss.4(3),(4),(6),(7)

> 57. Act not to prevent use of Aadhaar number for other purposes under law.—Omitted by the Aadhaar and Other Laws (Amendment) Act 2019 (Act 14 of 2019), s. 25 (w.e.f. 25-07-2019)

VERIFIED against UIDAI's consolidated bare Act. Arrangement of sections at p.4 reads '57. [Omitted.].'; the body at p.25 carries the omission note. s.4(3),(4),(5),(6),(7) were all inserted by Act 14 of 2019 s.7 w.e.f. 25-7-2019 and read as quoted. No change on this point since.

Source: https://uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_Act_2016_as_amended.pdf

### 2. [high|law_requires] An ordinary private employer that is NOT a Requesting Entity and NOT an Offline Verification Seeking Entity MAY lawfully collect, store and use an employee's Aadhaar number. Regulation 5 of the Aadhaar (Sharing of Information) Regulations 2016 is the affirmative legal basis, subject to three cumulative duties: (a) lawful purpose; (b) a three-part disclosure (purpose; whether mandatory or voluntary and if mandatory the legal provision mandating it; alternatives); and (c) consent.

**Provision:** Aadhaar (Sharing of Information) Regulations 2016, reg. 5 [consolidated as on 21.08.2025]

> 5.(1) Any individual, agency or entity which collects Aadhaar number or any document containing the Aadhaar number, shall: (a) collect, store and use the Aadhaar number for a lawful purpose; (b) inform the Aadhaar number holder the following details:- i. the purpose for which the information is collected; ii. whether submission of Aadhaar number or proof of Aadhaar for such purpose is mandatory or voluntary, and if mandatory, the legal provision mandating it; iii. alternatives to submission of Aadhaar number or the document containing Aadhaar number, if any; (c) obtain consent of the Aadhaar number holder to the collection, storage and use of his Aadhaar number for the specified purposes.

VERIFIED verbatim against UIDAI's current consolidated text [Updated as on 21.08.2025], which is byte-identical to the copy UIDAI serves today from its Updated Regulations page. Regulation heading is 'Responsibility of any agency or entity other than requesting entity with respect to Aadhaar number' — its existence presupposes a non-RE may hold Aadhaar numbers. reg 5(2): no use beyond the stated purposes. reg 5(3): no sharing without consent. CORRECTION to the prior draft: the conditions are three heads, not four; the earlier text said 'four' and then listed five items.

Source: https://uidai.gov.in/images/The_Aadhaar_Sharing_of_information_regulation_2016.pdf

### 3. [high|law_requires] Counterintuitive and load-bearing: becoming a registered Offline Verification Seeking Entity makes an HR product's position WORSE, not better, for storing Aadhaar numbers. s.8A(4)(b) bars an OVSE from collecting, using or storing an Aadhaar number for any purpose, with no consent exception. A non-registered entity under Sharing Regulation 5 faces no such bar.

**Provision:** Aadhaar Act 2016 s.8A(4)(b) (ins. by Act 14 of 2019 s.9 w.e.f. 25-7-2019); AOVR 2021 reg.14A(1)(b)

> s.8A(4): 'No offline verification-seeking entity shall— (a) subject an Aadhaar number holder to authentication; (b) collect, use, or store an Aadhaar number or biometric information of any individual for any purpose; (c) take any action contrary to any obligation on it as may be specified by regulations.'

VERIFIED verbatim. s.8A was inserted by Act 14 of 2019 s.9 w.e.f. 25-7-2019. Regulation 14A(1)(b) of the 2021 Regulations restates it as: 'shall not collect, use or store Aadhaar number or biometric information of any individual for any purpose or share offline Aadhaar data with any other entity except in accordance with the Act and Regulations framed thereunder'. TWO readings of that 'except' clause are open: it may qualify the whole sub-clause (creating divergence from the statute) or, on the more natural grammatical reading, attach only to the 'share offline Aadhaar data' limb (creating no divergence). The statute prevails either way. UIDAI's own May 2026 OVSE Handbook confirms the design intent: 'As offline verification does not involve the transmission or storage of the Aadhaar number, OVSEs face less compliance burden.'

Source: https://uidai.gov.in/images/Aadhaar_Act_2016_as_amended.pdf

### 4. [high|law_requires] A multi-tenant HR SaaS CANNOT perform Aadhaar offline verification on behalf of its employer customers. Regulation 16A(2) is an express, unqualified bar. Nor can e-KYC be resold: reg 15(2) permits licence-key sharing only for Yes/No authentication and expressly prohibits it for e-KYC. Only Yes/No authentication can be extended to a third party, and then the requesting entity is jointly and severally liable.

**Provision:** Aadhaar (Authentication and Offline Verification) Regulations 2021, regs. 16A(2), 15(1), 15(2), 15(4), 15(5)

> reg 16A(2): 'No entity shall perform Offline Verification on behalf of another entity or person.' reg 15(2): '...such sharing of license key is only permissible for performing Yes/ No authentication, and is prohibited in case of e-KYC authentication.' reg 15(5): 'The requesting entity shall be jointly and severally liable, along with the entity or agency with which it has shared a license key, for non-compliance with the regulations, processes, standards, guidelines and protocols of the Authority.'

VERIFIED verbatim against the current UIDAI consolidated AOVR 2021 [Updated as on 5.12.2024] — the version UIDAI still serves as its consolidated text, obtained both from UIDAI's live document store and a January 2026 Wayback capture of the /images path (byte-identical, 323,609 bytes). The December 2025 amendment does not touch regs 15, 16A or 14A. Note reg 15(1) does permit a requesting entity to use Yes/No authentication 'for its own use or on behalf of other agencies' — so a Yes/No-only SaaS RE model is not foreclosed, unlike offline verification and e-KYC.

Source: https://uidai.gov.in/images/The_Aadhaar_Authentication_and_Offline_Verifications_Regulations_2021.pdf

### 6. [medium|common_practice] CORRECTION TO EARLIER RESEARCH — do not treat s.142 as making Aadhaar mandatory in the product. When s.142 was notified, the Ministry of Labour and Employment publicly clarified that it was notified only for collection of worker data and that no benefit would be denied for want of Aadhaar. The earlier draft's inference that 'Aadhaar can be treated as mandatory for the EPF/ESI enrolment flow' is withdrawn.

**Provision:** Code on Social Security 2020 s.142 as notified by S.O. 1730(E); MoLE public clarification, May 2021

The clarification (attributed to the then Labour Minister, May 2021) is reported consistently across multiple independent secondary sources: s.142 'has been notified only for collection of data of workers including migrant workers. No benefit will be denied to workers for want of Aadhaar.' I could not retrieve the underlying PIB release itself, so this is corroborated secondary reporting, not a primary instrument — but it is directionally unambiguous and it cuts against the product treating Aadhaar as a hard gate. It also sits consistently with s.4(6) of the Aadhaar Act (no denial of service) and reg 5(3) of the AOVR 2021, both of which are drafted against requesting entities and OVSEs rather than plain employers.

### 11. [high|law_requires] The general prohibition on publicly displaying an Aadhaar number that actually binds a plain employer is Regulation 6(1) of the Sharing Regulations, not s.29(4) of the Act. Reg 6(1) is drafted against 'any person or entity or agency' with no qualifier. s.29(4) carries the qualifier 'collected or created under this Act', which introduces an interpretive step for an employer's own record.

**Provision:** Aadhaar (Sharing of Information) Regulations 2016 reg.6(1); Aadhaar Act 2016 ss.29(1)–(4), 2(g), 2(j)

> reg 6(1): 'The Aadhaar number of an individual shall not be published, displayed or posted publicly by any person or entity or agency.' s.29(4): 'No Aadhaar number, demographic information or photograph collected or created under this Act in respect of an Aadhaar number holder shall be published, displayed or posted publicly, except for the purposes as may be specified by regulations.'

CORRECTION OF EMPHASIS from the earlier draft, which put the weight on s.29(4) and quietly dropped its qualifier while relying on the same qualifier elsewhere (for face templates). Both provisions verified verbatim. s.29(1) bars sharing of CORE biometric information (finger print, Iris scan — s.2(j)) 'collected or created under this Act'; a photograph is 'biometric information' under s.2(g) but is NOT core biometric. s.29(3) was substituted by Act 14 of 2019 s.13 and requires purposes to be informed IN WRITING and 'in clear and precise language understandable to the individual', but is confined to requesting entities and OVSEs. Only s.29(1), (2) and (4) are general on their face. The practical product conclusion (never display a full Aadhaar number) is unaffected and rests most cleanly on reg 6(1).

Source: https://uidai.gov.in/images/The_Aadhaar_Sharing_of_information_regulation_2016.pdf

### 12. [high|law_requires] Regulation 6 of the Sharing of Information Regulations imposes four directly implementable engineering obligations on ANY entity holding Aadhaar numbers, including a plain employer and its HR SaaS: security and confidentiality of the database, redaction before any database is made public, encrypted transmission over the Internet, and a hard retention ceiling tied to the consented purpose. Regulation 7 then deems any contravention of regs 3–6 to be a violation of s.29(2) of the Act.

**Provision:** Aadhaar (Sharing of Information) Regulations 2016, regs. 6(1)–(5), 7

> 6.(2) 'Any individual, entity or agency, which is in possession of Aadhaar number(s) of Aadhaar number holders, shall ensure security and confidentiality of the Aadhaar numbers and of any record or database containing the Aadhaar numbers.' 6.(4) 'No entity, including a requesting entity, shall require an individual to transmit his Aadhaar number over the Internet unless such transmission is secure and the Aadhaar number is transmitted in encrypted form except where transmission is required for correction of errors or redressal of grievances.' 6.(5) 'No entity, including a requesting entity, shall retain Aadhaar numbers or any document or database containing Aadhaar numbers for longer than is necessary for the purpose specified to the Aadhaar number holder at the time of obtaining consent.' 7. 'Without prejudice to any action that may be taken under the Act, any contravention of regulations 3, 4, 5 and 6 of these regulations shall constitute a violation of sub-section (2) of Section 29 of the Act.'

## Product requirements

- Do NOT register the product, or advise customers to register, as an Offline Verification Seeking Entity while the product also stores Aadhaar numbers. s.8A(4)(b) bars a registered OVSE from storing an Aadhaar number for any purpose, so the two functions cannot lawfully coexist in one registered legal entity. Note the escape hatch: per UIDAI's own May 2026 handbook, plain Aadhaar secure QR-code and e-Aadhaar verification do NOT require registration, so a QR-based identity check can sit alongside Aadhaar storage in the same entity.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: Aadhaar Act s.8A(4)(b); AOVR 2021 regs.13A, 14A(1)(b); UIDAI OVSE Handbook (May 2026)
- Rely on Regulation 5 of the Sharing of Information Regulations as the storage basis, and implement its disclosures as a hard-gated consent screen before any Aadhaar field accepts input: (a) purpose, (b) whether mandatory or voluntary, (c) if mandatory, the citation of the mandating provision, (d) available alternatives. Record consent with timestamp, notice-text version and actor. Consent must be per-purpose, not blanket.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: Aadhaar (Sharing of Information) Regulations 2016 reg.5(1)(b)–(c)
- REVISED — make Aadhaar OPTIONAL everywhere in the product, including the EPF/ESI flow, and do not present it to users as legally mandatory. The earlier recommendation to treat Aadhaar as mandatory for social-security enrolment on the strength of s.142 is withdrawn: MoLE clarified on notification that s.142 was notified for data collection and that no benefit would be denied for want of Aadhaar. Cite 'Section 142, Code on Social Security, 2020' in the notice as the statutory context and as the reason it is requested, not as a gate that blocks employment or enrolment.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: Code on Social Security 2020 s.142 and the MoLE clarification; Sharing Regulations reg.5(1)(b)(ii)–(iii); Aadhaar Act s.4(7)
- NEW — model EPF onboarding around the fact that, since 1 August 2025, the EMPLOYEE generates and activates their own UAN via Aadhaar Face Authentication in the UMANG app. Make employee self-service the default and primary path; the product should prompt, track and chase, not collect. Retain an employer-side Aadhaar capture path only for the exceptional cases EPFO still allows: International Workers and citizens of Nepal and Bhutan. This is the single largest reduction in the product's Aadhaar surface area.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: EPFO HO Circular No. WSU/MemberProfilePt1/E-965649/2025-26/22 dated 30.07.2025
- Never render a full Aadhaar number in any UI, export, report, log, payslip, PDF, email, error message or support tool. Display last-4 only. The cleanest legal hook for a plain employer is reg 6(1) of the Sharing Regulations ('shall not be published, displayed or posted publicly by any person or entity or agency'), which carries no 'collected under this Act' qualifier, together with reg 6(3) on redaction before any database is made public.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: Sharing Regulations regs.6(1), 6(3); Aadhaar Act s.29(4)
- Encrypt Aadhaar numbers at rest and enforce TLS plus application-layer encryption for any transmission over the Internet. Reg 6(4) makes encrypted transmission a legal requirement, subject only to a narrow carve-out for transmission required for correction of errors or grievance redressal — do not build to that carve-out.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: Sharing Regulations regs.6(2), 6(4)
- Implement automatic deletion of Aadhaar numbers and any uploaded Aadhaar document once the consented purpose is spent (UAN seeding confirmed, IP number generated), with a configurable statutory-retention override and an audit trail of deletions. Reg 6(5) is a hard retention ceiling tied to the consented purpose.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: Sharing Regulations reg.6(5)
- Do NOT store Virtual ID anywhere in the system. Reg 4A(4) is a general prohibition binding every entity, not just requesting entities. VID may be entered into a government portal but is not a lawful substitute store.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: AOVR 2021 reg.4A(4)
- Architect the Aadhaar number as a separate, separately-encrypted, separately-access-controlled store referenced by an internal token, with the token — never the number — used in all business tables. The formal Aadhaar Data Vault mandate currently binds only requesting entities, but this design satisfies the general security duty in reg 6(2), makes a future move to RE status or a notified vault requirement a migration rather than a rewrite, and limits criminal exposure. Do not use a hash of the Aadhaar number as the token.
  - priority: P1 | driven_by: Sharing Regulations reg.6(2); UIDAI Circular 14 of 2025; UIDAI ADV FAQ 03.11.2025 Q23
- Do not build any feature that performs Aadhaar offline verification on behalf of customer organisations. Reg 16A(2) forbids it outright. If Aadhaar-based identity verification is a product requirement, either each employer registers as its own OVSE and the product provides only the interface (and that employer then cannot store the Aadhaar number), or the product confines itself to QR-code / e-Aadhaar verification, which UIDAI says needs no registration.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: AOVR 2021 reg.16A(2); Aadhaar Act s.8A(4)(b); UIDAI OVSE Handbook (May 2026)
- Do not resell or share an e-KYC licence key. Reg 15(2) permits licence-key sharing only for Yes/No authentication and expressly prohibits it for e-KYC; under reg 15(5) the requesting entity is jointly and severally liable for the recipient's non-compliance.
  - priority: P1 | driven_by: AOVR 2021 regs.15(2), 15(4), 15(5)
- Model EPFO and ESIC integration as 'employer enters Aadhaar into a government portal which itself performs the authentication'. Do not design toward AUA/KUA status for statutory filing — it is unnecessary and would import the ADV/HSM, GCC-hosting, SOC 2 Type II audit and Rs 1 crore civil-penalty regime. CAVEAT: the ESIC portal manual offers OTP or biometric (fingerprint/iris) authentication; never build or endorse the biometric path at the employer's premises, since that would require a UIDAI-certified Registered Device and undermines the data-entry-channel characterisation.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: ESIC HQ Aadhaar seeding manual; Aadhaar Act ss.33A, 2(aa); AOVR 2021 reg.7(1a)
- Surface a remediation task, not a hard block, when an employee's UAN is not Aadhaar-seeded. EPFO's instruction that ECR filing requires Aadhaar-seeded UANs is an administrative circular rather than a statute, and the MoLE clarification cuts against denying anything for want of Aadhaar. Route the employee to the UMANG self-service path so the employer never handles the number where avoidable.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: EPFO Circular BKG-27/5/2021-BKG dated 11.09.2021; EPFO Circular dated 30.07.2025; MoLE clarification on s.142
- Do NOT collect Aadhaar for income-tax, TDS or Form-16 purposes. PAN–Aadhaar linkage is an obligation of the individual; the sanction is PAN inoperability and there is no employer-facing duty or penalty. Surface a non-blocking nudge if a PAN is reported inoperative; collect nothing. Do not put the section number in customer-facing material until it is gazette-verified.
  - priority: P1 | driven_by: Income-tax Act 2025 s.262(5)–(7) (citation not yet gazette-verified)
- Treat attendance face templates as a DPDP-governed biometric, NOT as Aadhaar data, and keep the two subsystems and their consent flows strictly separate. Aadhaar criminal provisions do not reach a face template that never touches UIDAI. Conversely, any Aadhaar biometric authentication would require Requesting Entity status and UIDAI-certified Registered Devices.
  - priority: P1 | driven_by: Aadhaar Act ss.2(g), 2(j), 29(1); AOVR 2021 reg.7(1a)
- Prefer rejecting Aadhaar document uploads entirely in favour of a typed, validated number field; where an image is unavoidable, mask the first 8 digits at ingestion. ACCURACY NOTE for customer-facing material: the 8-digit masking rule in reg 14(1)(mb) formally binds requesting entities only. Present it to customers as risk mitigation grounded in the general security duty in reg 6(2), never as a statutory duty on them.
  - priority: P1 | driven_by: AOVR 2021 reg.14(1)(mb) (by analogy); Sharing Regulations reg.6(2)
- Restrict Aadhaar read access by role, log every read with actor and reason, and exclude Aadhaar from all support-tooling, analytics, data-warehouse, LLM/AI feature and third-party sub-processor flows by default. s.43 makes officers personally liable with only a due-diligence defence, so access logging is a governance control that evidences that defence, not merely a security one.
  - priority: P0 | driven_by: Aadhaar Act ss.38, 43; Sharing Regulations reg.6(2)
- Design the identity-verification module so that Aadhaar Verifiable Credential and QR verification — which yield only last-4 plus demographics and photograph, never the full number — can be swapped in as the default onboarding check. This is the cleanest lawful design today, the direction UIDAI has publicly signalled, and the route competitors in employment screening and gig workforce verification have already taken via OVSE registration.
  - priority: P2 | driven_by: AOVR Amendment Regulations 2025 regs.2(be), 3A, 13A; UIDAI List of Registered OVSEs as on 30.06.2026
- If Aadhaar-based attendance authentication is ever pursued, budget for the Good Governance (SWIK) Rules route: a per-customer, per-use-case proposal sponsored by a line Ministry, examined by UIDAI, authorised by MeitY, and notified in the Gazette. 'Staff attendance' is a listed candidate use case for several sectors, but the approval attaches to the employer, not the software vendor, and rule 3(2) requires it to be voluntary. Treat as a long-lead enterprise programme, not a feature.
  - priority: P2 | driven_by: Good Governance (SWIK) Rules 2020 rr.3, 4(2), 5 as amended by G.S.R. 88(E) dated 31.1.2025; MeitY SOP Annexures 1 and 2

## Needs counsel

- Whether the chain [breach of Sharing Regulations regs 5–6] → [deemed violation of s.29(2) via reg 7] → [criminal liability under s.38(g)] actually holds. s.38 is headed 'Penalty for unauthorised access to the Central Identities Data Repository', opens 'Whoever, not being authorised by the Authority, intentionally,—' and its other limbs (a)–(f), (h)–(i) are all CIDR-centric, which may contextually confine (g). If the chain holds, a mishandled employer Aadhaar database carries up to ten years' imprisonment and a minimum Rs 10 lakh fine. This remains the single largest unresolved criminal-exposure question and cannot be settled by desk research.
- Whether an HR SaaS vendor that stores Aadhaar numbers on behalf of employer customers is itself an 'entity' bound by Sharing Regulation 5 in its own right, or is a processor acting for the employer. Reg 5 says 'Any individual, agency or entity which collects Aadhaar number', arguably catching the SaaS directly and requiring it to have its own lawful purpose and consent. This determines whether consent must be captured in the vendor's name, the customer's name, or both, and it interacts with the DPDP data-fiduciary / data-processor split.
- Whether the Aadhaar Data Vault / HSM regime binds a non-requesting entity. UIDAI's 2017 position extended ADV to 'AUAs/KUAs/Sub-AUAs and other entities' storing Aadhaar numbers; Circular 14 of 2025 and the 03.11.2025 FAQ scope it to REs. Because this rests on a circular and an FAQ rather than a notified regulation and has already flipped once, and because the compliance-cost delta is large (on-premises or MeitY-empanelled GCC hosting, HSM, annual SOC 2 Type II audit by a CERT-In empanelled auditor), a written opinion is warranted before the architecture is frozen.
- Whether an employer's act of typing an employee's Aadhaar number into the EPFO or ESIC portal makes the employer a 'requesting entity' within s.2(u) ('an agency or person that submits the Aadhaar number, and demographic information or biometric information, of an individual to the Central Identities Data Repository for authentication'). The submission is to a government portal which then hits the CIDR. If an employer were held to be an RE, the entire RE stack (reg 14, ADV, HSM, audits, reg 14(o) permission to store for non-authentication purposes) would attach. My reading is that it does not, but the wording is uncomfortably broad and this underpins several P0 requirements. The 2025 shift of UAN generation to employee self-service reduces but does not eliminate the exposure.
- Whether an employer may lawfully make Aadhaar submission a condition of employment, or a hard precondition to payroll enrolment. This is now sharper than the earlier draft suggested: s.142 is drafted as an obligation on the employee; MoLE clarified on notification that no benefit would be denied for want of Aadhaar; s.4(6) and AOVR reg 5(3) forbid denial of service but are scoped to REs and OVSEs; and EPFO's ECR instruction is administrative. There is existing constitutional-law criticism of employer-side enforcement of s.142. Counsel should settle whether the product may ever hard-block.
- Verification of the Income-tax Act 2025 section number for PAN–Aadhaar linkage (believed to be s.262(5)–(7)). Re-tested this round and still could not obtain a primary text: incometaxindia.gov.in returns HTTP 403 to both the Act PDF and the section browser even with full browser headers, upload.indiacode.nic.in refuses connections, www.indiacode.nic.in 404s on every bitstream path, and there is no Wayback snapshot. Four independent secondary sources now agree on the text. The substantive conclusion is robust; the citation must be gazette-verified before it appears in customer-facing material.
- Cross-border and sub-processor implications: whether hosting Aadhaar numbers on non-Indian cloud infrastructure, or exposing them to a foreign sub-processor, is lawful for a non-requesting entity. s.44 gives the Act extraterritorial reach where CIDR data is involved, and Circular 14 of 2025 confines RE vaults to on-premises, MeitY-empanelled GCC, or ADV-as-a-service. No equivalent express localisation rule was found for non-REs, but absence of a rule is not permission, and this interacts with the DPDP transfer regime.
- Whether the 'except in accordance with the Act and Regulations framed thereunder' qualifier in AOVR reg 14A(1)(b) attaches to the whole sub-clause (creating a divergence from the absolute bar in s.8A(4)(b)) or only to the final 'share offline Aadhaar data with any other entity' limb. On the natural grammatical reading it is the latter and there is no divergence, but the point is worth a line in any opinion because it is the only textual argument a customer might raise for an OVSE storing an Aadhaar number.

## Retracted

- RETRACTED: the inference that 'because s.142 of the Code on Social Security IS a law made by Parliament, Aadhaar can be treated as mandatory for the EPF/ESI enrolment flow specifically'. On notification of s.142 the Ministry of Labour and Employment publicly clarified that it was notified only for collection of worker data and that no benefit would be denied for want of Aadhaar. The product must treat Aadhaar as voluntary throughout, including in the social-security flow. The corresponding P0 product requirement has been rewritten.
- RETRACTED as a driver: the use of Aadhaar Act s.4(6) ('shall not deny any service') to ground a product requirement binding a plain employer. s.4(6) is expressly scoped to 'Every requesting entity to whom an authentication request is made by an Aadhaar number holder under sub-section (3)', and AOVR reg 5(3) is scoped to 'A requesting entity or OVSE'. Neither reaches an employer that is neither. The correct driver for a plain employer is Sharing Regulation 5(1)(b)(ii)–(iii).
- CORRECTED CITATION: the EPFO circular underlying the 01.06.2021 ECR seeding instruction is No. BKG-27/7/2020-G/Pt.V/, not 'BKG-27/7/2020-G/Pt.file' as previously stated. Read off the scanned original.
- CORRECTED SCOPE: the ESIC Aadhaar seeding circular No. 32/P/11/11/85/Cov/24 dated 28.04.2023 is a GOA REGIONAL OFFICE circular addressed to employers in the Goa Region, not a national ESIC instrument. The attached user manual is an ESIC HQ document. The extension of its logic to the EPFO portal was an inference and was never verified against an EPFO document.
- CORRECTED EMPHASIS: s.29(4) was previously asserted to 'bind an ordinary employer' while its qualifier 'collected or created under this Act' was passed over — even though the same qualifier was relied on elsewhere in the report to exclude attendance face templates. The general public-display prohibition that plainly binds an employer is Sharing Regulation 6(1), which is drafted against 'any person or entity or agency' with no such qualifier. The product conclusion is unchanged; the legal hook is corrected.
- CORRECTED LABELS: several findings previously marked 'law_requires' rest on regulator circulars, FAQs, handbooks or portal manuals rather than on a statute or notified regulation. Relabelled to common_practice: the EPFO ECR seeding requirement, the ESIC portal characterisation, the Aadhaar Data Vault scoping (Circular 14 of 2025 plus the 03.11.2025 FAQ), and the MoLE s.142 clarification. The obligations may still be practically binding, but they are not notified law and should not be described to a customer's counsel as such.
- CORRECTED ARITHMETIC: Sharing Regulation 5 imposes THREE cumulative heads — (a) lawful purpose, (b) a three-part disclosure, (c) consent. The earlier text said 'four cumulative conditions' and then listed five items.
- STANDING CORRECTIONS to widely-held beliefs that may have entered the PRD by osmosis, all re-verified this round: (1) 'The 2019 amendment restored private-sector Aadhaar use under s.57' — FALSE; s.57 was omitted outright and replaced by a narrower permission-gated route. (2) 'Private entities are not permitted to collect or keep Aadhaar copies (per the UIDAI advisory)' — FALSE as current law; that May 2022 advisory was withdrawn within 48 hours (now verified against the withdrawal release itself), and Sharing Regulation 5 expressly contemplates non-requesting entities collecting and storing Aadhaar numbers. (3) 'Everyone storing Aadhaar must implement an Aadhaar Data Vault' — not the current position; Circular 14 of 2025 and the 03.11.2025 FAQ scope the duty to requesting entities.
