## assessment

HEADLINE: round three did not deliver the gap it was commissioned for. Four gaps were commissioned; five dimensions came back — parity, hr-ops/personas, verticals, integration surface, GTM. There is NO dimension covering system architecture and data model. It was silently substituted with an integration-surface sweep, which is the *boundary* of the architecture, not the architecture. Do not let the volume of output disguise this: gap (2) is unfilled.

SECOND: the retraction counts are the most important number in this report and nobody has commented on them. 12, 13, 14 and 15 retractions across the four dimensions that report a count — roughly 54 findings killed on adversarial re-verification of a prior sweep. That is not a round-three quality problem; it is a *process* error-rate measurement, and it applies retroactively. Rounds one and two have not been adversarially re-verified. If ~13 findings per dimension die when someone actually opens the competitor's module pages instead of its index page, then every round-one and round-two finding that has not been re-checked must be treated as ~70-75% reliable, not as settled. Three specific round-one/two conclusions were already killed inside round three (the Frappe PT/LWF state coverage claims, the greytHR whitespace claim, the ECR irreversibility line). Assume there are more.

THIRD: round three is internally inconsistent in a way that will produce a broken PRD if merged naively. Two dimensions disagree on a P0 spec. hr-ops states as a P0 requirement "the ECR as a plain-text UAN-based file with 11 #~#-delimited fields per member line and no header row"; the integration dimension states as a P0 requirement "Do not code against the 11-field '#~#' layout as received." Both are marked P0. Only one can be built. The integration dimension is right — hr-ops' own confidence on that claim is [medium] and its own open questions admit no post-revamp primary spec was read.

The same split runs through the form vocabulary. hr-ops establishes at [high] that the Income-tax Act 2025 is in force from 1 April 2026 and renumbered everything (24Q→138, Form 16→130, 12BB→124, 80C→123, 80D→126). The integration dimension and the entire Frappe/Tally parity table are written exclusively in the old vocabulary and never acknowledge the transition. The parity table's single widest claimed gap — "Form 24Q, Form 16, Form 12BA, Form 27A all absent from Frappe, all present in Tally" — is stated in form numbers that, per this same report, no longer exist. Either the renumbering claim is wrong or the parity table needs relabelling. This must be resolved before either goes in the PRD, and the renumbering must be verified against notified Income-tax Rules, not a secondary source, because it determines every form label, every payslip footer, every export filename and every search synonym in the product.

FOURTH: the largest substantive finding in round three is the central-sphere vs state-sphere correction, and it is correctly identified as such by the researcher. It invalidates most of the *operative detail* in the vertical section for the target market. Every form number (Form I/IV/IX), the 8-hour day, the 48-hour week, the twice-yearly VDA cadence, the daily÷8 / daily×26 conversions, Rule 51(4) retention — all central-sphere. A 20-200 employee manufacturer in Pune or Coimbatore is state-sphere. No state's notified rules were obtained for any target state. So the vertical section can describe the *shape* of the obligation but cannot state a single operative rule for a single state the product will actually sell into.

FIFTH: what is entirely missing across all of round three, and needs commissioning before the PRD can be written:
- **DPDP Act 2023, Aadhaar handling, and biometric data.** Zero coverage in any dimension. The product holds Aadhaar, PAN, bank accounts, salary, and — per the manufacturing/CLMS requirements — biometric face templates and gate photos. Consent, purpose limitation, data-principal rights, breach notification, data-fiduciary obligations, cross-border transfer, and the Aadhaar Act's storage restrictions are all P0 architecture and legal constraints. Three rounds and not one mention. This alone can make the product unsellable to any buyer with a competent lawyer, and it directly constrains the AI layer.
- **The AI layer itself.** The product is "AI-native" and round three contains one relevant sentence ("treat AI extraction output as a draft requiring human sign-off"). There is nothing on where AI creates value in this workflow, what the human-in-the-loop boundary is, PII redaction before model calls, grounding and citation for compliance answers, or how an AI action appears in the audit trail that BFSI and every payroll auditor will demand. Round two did AI unit economics; nobody has done AI product design or AI risk.
- **Implementation and migration time.** Not researched. In Indian SMB SaaS this is what kills deals, and round three proves the constraint exists (Keka admits a setup fee; migration from Tally/Frappe/incumbent HRMS with YTD tie-out is listed as a P1 requirement with no evidence of how hard it is).
- **Gratuity provisioning, Payment of Bonus annual workflow, and Maternity Benefit.** All flagged as open questions in round three; none scoped.

SIXTH, and this is a positioning problem rather than an evidence problem: round three quietly relocates the competitive centre of gravity. The parity dimension is a Frappe-vs-Tally bake-off. The vertical dimension calls greytHR "the volume incumbent at this band" and the GTM dimension prices greytHR at Rs 2,495/month including 50 employees with a bank-alliance distribution channel nobody else has. Those are different fights. Round three does not reconcile them and the PRD must.

Net: the parity table is ready. The problem statement is ready in shape, not in magnitude. GTM is ready on structure, empty on economics. Verticals must be rewritten around statutory-threshold segmentation because the vertical-whitespace argument collapsed. Architecture needs another round.

## contradictions_with_earlier_rounds

- 'FILING IS THE UNIT OF DELIVERY' — this is the most consequential contradiction and it is a change of KIND, not degree. Round three confirms filing is where the value is (Frappe has literally zero filing artefacts; Tally has all of them; the parity dimension calls it 'the single widest statutory gap'). But it simultaneously establishes that filing CANNOT be delivered as a unit. Every filing surface is an attended portal: EPFO requires interactive login with a CAPTCHA (explicit hard blocker for unattended automation); the TDS chain is not an API at all — the payroll system emits a text file, the EMPLOYER runs a Java desktop FVU utility against it plus a CSI file pulled from the income tax portal, and the EMPLOYER personally uploads the .fvu; ESIC is an Excel template upload behind a portal serving an incomplete TLS chain; state PT is N separate manual portals. So the deliverable is 'portal-accepted artefact plus attended assisted filing with a human in the loop and a credential vault,' not 'we file for you.' If rounds one or two priced, positioned or scoped on 'filing as the unit of delivery' meaning automated filing, that must be restated — it changes the value proposition, the marketing claim, the support model, the COGS (a supervised human step per customer per month), and creates a legal exposure (holding employer credentials and acting on government portals on their behalf) that has never been scoped.
- 'FILING IS THE UNIT OF DELIVERY' — second, narrower contradiction: round three states outright that round two drew the irreversibility line in the wrong place. EPFO's re-engineered ECR (effective wage month September 2025) separates return submission from payment generation and introduces Revised and Supplementary returns, so a filed PF return is now correctable. The real asymmetry is 'downward revision only before PAYMENT INITIATION, upward revision at any time.' The verification gate therefore belongs immediately before payment initiation, not before return submission. Any round-two design that hardened the pre-submission gate is optimising the wrong moment. Additionally ReECR's mandatory sequential chronological filing is a new hard architectural constraint round two did not have: a skipped or failed month blocks every subsequent month, so the product needs an unbroken per-establishment filing ledger that refuses to let a month be silently abandoned.
- 'TALLY IS THE TRUE INCUMBENT' — half-confirmed, half-broken, and the broken half was load-bearing. CONFIRMED: Tally owns the statutory outputs Frappe entirely lacks (PF computation + ECR file, ESI, Form 24Q, Form 16, Form 12BA, Form 27A, gratuity computation) and has the deepest SMB distribution in India (four partner grades, 1,257 partners displayed, plus a distinct CA Community). BROKEN: two of Tally's assumed statutory advantages do not exist — TallyPrime does NOT ship state PT slabs and has NO LWF engine; both incumbents require hand-entered slabs, and Tally's own content tells employers to go ask their State Labour Welfare Board. Tally also has no leave management module (admitted in its own FAQ), cannot calculate leave encashment (also its own FAQ), has no shift/roster/OT/F&F/recruitment/performance, requires manual Attendance Vouchers with no device ingestion, and Silver is single-PC with multi-user gated behind Gold at 3x price. THE REAL PROBLEM: round three's own vertical dimension calls greytHR 'the volume incumbent at this band,' and the GTM dimension shows greytHR at Rs 2,495/month including 50 employees with a bank-alliance distribution channel (ICICI, HSBC, IDFC First, AU SFB, BoB, Kotak, Visa, Mastercard) that no other vendor has. Tally is the incumbent for the accounting/GL and statutory-filing job. greytHR is the incumbent for the HRMS job. These require different displacement arguments, different migration tooling and different battlecards. Round three does not reconcile them and rounds one/two apparently did not either. Unresolved and material: nobody has evidence on Tally payroll module ADOPTION as opposed to capability — if Tally's payroll module is capable but largely unused, and the true incumbent at 20-200 is Excel plus a CA, the entire displacement strategy changes.
- 'BEACHHEAD IS 20-200' — the segment holds on statutory grounds and is in fact strengthened (ESI at 10, EPF at 20, GRC at 20 workers, OSHWC factory at >20 with power — the entire band is in full statutory scope, unlike BFSI or IT/ITES). - WHETHER ANY INDIAN SMB/MID-MARKET HRMS SHIPS TRUE MULTI-CLIENT (BUREAU) PAYROLL — one tenant running many client entities with per-client statutory segregation and billing. Unverified. Multi-ENTITY is now confirmed widely available, so the question narrows to the bureau case — and the entire CA-channel product thesis rests on Kredily alone.
- ALL VERTICAL SIZING AND ANY TAM/SAM/SOM BY SEGMENT. Zero data exists in round three. Any number in the PRD's sizing section for a vertical is invented.
- PIECE-RATE AND PRODUCTION-INCENTIVE PAYROLL for manufacturing — no product page or government source examined describes it, and HROne's manufacturing page omits it. Whether Indian 20-200 manufacturers run piece-rate inside payroll or as a separate spreadsheet is unsettled.
- WHICH IDENTITY PROVIDER INDIAN 20-200 COMPANIES ACTUALLY USE (Google Workspace vs Microsoft 365). Round three says explicitly: do not assert a share. It affects SSO/SCIM sequencing.
- WHETHER THE FORTUNA/SMART FACE DEVICE API CONTRACT GENERALISES. The GET/POST attendance ingest plus POST negative-ID push with 20-character device reason strings is a real, buildable spec — but it belongs to one vendor ecosystem, not an industry standard. Budget per-vendor adapters, not one integration.

## highest_value_requirements

- PORTAL-ACCEPTED STATUTORY OUTPUT FILES, BYTE-EXACT, WITH PRE-SUBMISSION VALIDATION. EPFO ECR text file, ESIC monthly contribution file, Form 24Q/138 text file that passes the FVU, Form 16/130 Parts A and B, Form 12BA, Form 27A, and state PT and LWF challans — each validated against its own schema BEFORE the operator opens a government portal, with an error list covering the known failure classes (employee in payroll but missing from the return, UAN missing or malformed, wage-month mismatch, arrear wages in the regular column, ineligible EPS contributions, headcount variance vs prior month). SALE-KILLER because this is the single reason to buy: Frappe has none of it, Tally has all of it, and both freemium competitors put their paywall on exactly this boundary. A file rejected at the portal makes the product worse than the spreadsheet it replaced.
- BANK SALARY DISBURSEMENT FILE GENERATION AS THE PRIMARY RAIL, with per-bank versioned templates (HDFC ENet, ICICI, Axis, SBI, Kotak plus a generic NEFT/RTGS CSV), configurable filename and extension, the generated file persisted as an artefact tied to the run, and returned bank status reconciled back per employee. SALE-KILLER because without it the money does not move, and round three proves this is weak in BOTH incumbents (Frappe emits only on-screen reports; Tally's connected banking is partner-gated and payments-capable at only three banks). Equally important is the negative requirement: do NOT architect disbursement on a direct bank integration. Those are BD-gated, require the employer to already hold a current account at that specific bank, carry 30-day session expiry, 5-attempt OTP lockout and same-day cut-offs, and Zoho's YES Bank rail is already closed to new customers. File is the floor, integration is the upsell, and bank-side maker-checker must be modelled as an explicit tracked stage of the run rather than hidden — an API 200 is never settlement.
- STATE AS A FIRST-CLASS DIMENSION ON THE WORK LOCATION, NOT ON THE EMPLOYEE — carrying PT, LWF, minimum wages, shops-and-establishments, leave entitlement, AND the applicable Labour Code rules regime with its own commencement date, so one company runs multiple states in a single cycle. SALE-KILLER and the one decision that is genuinely impossible to retrofit. Frappe has multi-company but no state dimension anywhere in its payroll model — that is the structural gap, not a feature gap. The rolling per-state Code commencement makes this permanent rather than transitional: a group with plants in Gujarat, Karnataka and UP is running three regimes concurrently and must produce correct registers for each. Round three's own conclusion is right — this should be the architectural spine of the PRD.
- STATUTORY RATES, THRESHOLDS, FORM NUMBERS, SECTION NUMBERS, DEADLINES, FILING AUTHORITIES AND FORM LAYOUTS AS VERSIONED, EFFECTIVE-DATED DATA WITH PER-ENTRY PROVENANCE — never code constants. Proven necessary from four independent directions in round three: retro replay requires dated rate records; the Income-tax Act 2025 renumbered every form and section; the 50% wage add-back is expressly 'or another percentage notified by the Central Government'; VDA revises twice yearly; states commence on their own dates. Add the dual-vocabulary requirement — accept 24Q and 138, Form 16 and 130, 80C and 123 interchangeably in search, imports, labels, help and support, because historical periods legitimately keep the old forms and practitioners were still using them in August 2026. SALE-KILLER indirectly: this is the difference between shipping a compliance change as a config push and shipping it as a release, and round three demonstrates the incumbents' own published data is wrong (a live Zoho page with the wrong Section 126 limit, a greytHR calendar with both the wrong due date and the wrong authority for Karnataka PT). If compliance correctness is the differentiator, the compliance data set IS the product.
- THE PAYROLL MONTH AS AN AUDITABLE STATE MACHINE WITH AN EXPLICIT LOCK, built for iterate-and-diff rather than one clean run: OPEN → INPUTS CLOSED → PROCESSED → APPROVED → LOCKED → DISBURSED → FILED, reversible before LOCK and immutable after, with post-processing attendance/leave changes detected and forcing an explicit reprocess-or-defer decision showing exactly whose net pay moves and by how much. SALE-KILLER because designing for a single correct run is the classic way to lose this market — the documented model is process, inspect, fix inputs, reprocess, repeat until locked. It also produces the founder/CFO approval artefact (total payout, headcount, variance vs prior month with a per-employee explanation of every delta, statutory liability with due dates, approve or return-for-correction), which is the second buyer's entire job to be done.
- ARREARS AND RETROSPECTIVE RECOMPUTATION AS A CORE PRIMITIVE, NOT AN EDGE CASE — arrears as batches keyed to an effective retro date, recomputing all affected components across the retro period, showing the statutory delta (PF/ESI/PT/TDS) separately from the gross delta, generating a per-employee arrear statement, recomputing TDS across the remaining year, with a separate new-joiner arrears path and a first-class LOP reversal object with a configurable 1-12 month lookback. Plus the non-obvious rule that decides real money: PF liability on arrears dates from the DISBURSAL date, not the wage month the arrears relate to, which determines interest and damages exposure and must be surfaced when the batch is approved. SALE-KILLER because retro is routine in Indian payroll (salary revisions, backdated joining, promotions, LWP reversal, structure revision) and a product that cannot replay a month cleanly forces the operator back to Excel — at which point the product is decoration.
- ATTENDANCE-TO-PAYROLL AS A RECONCILIATION SURFACE, NOT A FEED. Ingest from biometric/face/turnstile devices, roster and ERP exports, client MIS files and manual entry; show per-employee source-of-record, conflicts and unexplained gaps; require an operations owner to clear exceptions before inputs can close; support attendance period (26th-25th) as an object distinct from pay period (calendar month), and at least three LOP day-rate conventions printed on the payslip. SALE-KILLER because this IS the problem statement — the verified job description spends most of its lines on inputs, reconciliation, deposits and filings and almost none on gross-to-net. Note the non-calendar wage period is not cosmetic: it measurably breaks arrear calculation, income-tax accrual at the FY boundary, and PF/ESI which compute on calendar months.
- MIGRATION FROM TALLY AND FROM THE INCUMBENT HRMS WITH A YTD RECONCILIATION REPORT THAT PROVABLY TIES OUT — employee master, pay heads to salary components, YTD figures, PF/ESI/PT/UAN identifiers, opening balances, and a discrepancy report before go-live. UNDER-RATED SALE-KILLER: without provable YTD tie-out on tax and statutory totals, mid-year switching is impossible and every deal must wait for an April go-live — a one-in-twelve sales window that alone can sink the business plan. Round three lists this at P1; it belongs at P0 for commercial reasons, not technical ones.
- FULL-AND-FINAL SETTLEMENT AS A SEPARATE, LOCKABLE WORKFLOW meeting the statutory SLAs — all due wages within TWO WORKING DAYS of resignation, dismissal or termination, gratuity within 30 days of becoming payable at 15 days' wages per completed year, with fixed-term employees earning gratuity at ONE year against five for permanent staff, and nomination captured from every employee at one year of service. Capturing resignation date, last working day, settlement date, leaving reason, notice required vs served with auto-computed shortfall and recovery, days worked, leave encashment by type, loan and advance recovery, and IT-declaration treatment. SALE-KILLER for any high-churn buyer, and note Tally cannot even calculate leave encashment by its own admission.
- EXCEL/CSV IMPORT AND EXPORT AT EVERY STAGE, NON-LOSSY ROUND-TRIP — inputs, register, variance, bank file, statutory returns. Cheap to build, fatal to omit. Advanced Excel is a stated hiring requirement alongside the payroll system in the verified job posting, not a legacy fallback. A product that cannot be reconciled against a spreadsheet gets worked around rather than adopted, and the workaround becomes the system of record.
- CREDENTIAL VAULT, EXPLICIT CUSTOMER AUTHORITY-TO-ACT, AND A FULL 'WHAT WE DID ON YOUR BEHALF' AUDIT LOG FOR ATTENDED PORTAL AUTOMATION — with legal review before shipping. This gates the entire filing value proposition. You are holding employer credentials for EPFO, ESIC, TRACES and N state PT portals and acting on government systems as the employer. Getting this wrong is not a feature failure, it is an existential and possibly criminal one. It is also completely unscoped against DPDP obligations, which no round has researched.
- THE SHARED, EXPLAINABLE, EFFECTIVE-DATED WAGE-BASE SERVICE implementing the Code on Wages s.2(y) 50% add-back once, feeding PF, gratuity, bonus, retrenchment compensation and leave encashment — with the threshold percentage as configurable data (the Code says 'or another percentage notified'), the separate 15% cap on remuneration in kind, and the recomputation exposed as a readable before/after diff. Handled by NEITHER incumbent — genuine greenfield, cross-cutting, and impossible to retrofit if duplicated per module. This is the strongest surviving differentiator in the vertical dimension and the one place where being new is an actual advantage.
