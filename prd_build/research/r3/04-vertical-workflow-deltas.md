METHOD. WHAT I CHANGED, IN ORDER OF SEVERITY.

1. The report is BUILT ON CENTRAL-SPHERE MATERIAL BUT TARGETS A STATE-SPHERE MARKET. The handbook says so in its own title and Chapter 1, and PRS confirms the Central Rules apply to 'central sector establishments... railways, mines, oilfields, and banking companies'. Typical 20-200 manufacturers, retailers, IT firms, hospitals and staffing agencies are not those. The original report quoted the handbook's title, including the parenthetical, and never drew the inference. This affects every form number and prescribed procedure in it.

2. THE HEADLINE FINDING WAS SEVEN MONTHS STALE. The report asserts the Central Rules are 'still draft' and formats 'not yet frozen'. They were notified 8 May 2026 (G.S.R. 343(E)), four months before today. Nobody checked for supersession of a February document in September. The architectural conclusion (schema-driven registers) happens to survive, but for a different and stronger reason: rolling state-by-state commencement, not draft status.

3. THE LARGEST FEATURE ERROR: the greytHR negative claim is false, and it was load-bearing for the wedge recommendation's whitespace leg. It was inferred from a marketing index page; the actual attendance module page markets shift management, rotation, rostering, auto shift detection, overtime, and muster roll / overtime register / attendance register maintenance. The payroll page markets FBP. Multi-entity also exists. This is exactly the failure mode flagged in the brief, in its negative form - asserting absence from a marketing page. HROne additionally markets contract labour, which the report's 'no mainstream vendor covers it' framing also missed. I rewrote the whitespace implication and re-derived a much shorter list of genuine gaps.

4. FOUR FINDINGS LOST THEIR EVIDENCE ENTIRELY. All Indeed citations use to.indeed.com short links that now 302 to unresolvable tracking URLs. Two carried 'high' confidence. Buyer persona, pricing and the IT/ITES bench argument all rested on these; all are downgraded to low and flagged as needing customer interviews.

WHAT I ADDED, AND WHY IT IS NOT PADDING. I resolved two of the report's own open questions with primary-adjacent sources, because the brief asked for one genuine attempt before leaving anything unknown: working-hour limits are now prescribed (8-hour day, 48-hour week, one weekly rest day, and a 10-consecutive-day ceiling), and minimum-wage arithmetic is now concrete (hourly = daily/8, monthly = daily x 26, VDA revised twice yearly on CPI-IW). The 10-consecutive-day statutory rule independently corroborates the FaceIT spec's N parameter, which is a genuinely useful convergence between an industry integration contract and the new law.

DID THE DIMENSION ANSWER ITS QUESTION? Partly, and less well than its length suggests. On MANUFACTURING and CONTRACT LABOUR it is strong, specific and now largely verified. On IT/ITES, HEALTHCARE and RETAIL the deltas rest almost entirely on job postings that no longer resolve and on vendor marketing pages - thin evidence dressed as findings. On BFSI it verified real regulatory detail for a segment it then correctly advises against targeting. And the report never asked the question that most determines vertical workflow deltas in India: which appropriate-government sphere and which state an establishment sits in. That single omission does more damage than any individual error, because it means the compliance detail is right about the wrong employers.

HOW MUCH TO TRUST THIS. The statutory spine: high confidence, verified verbatim, but read every claim as central-sphere unless a state rule says otherwise. The CLMS/contract-labour findings: high confidence, genuinely well sourced, the best part of this research. Competitive positioning and market structure: LOW confidence - one major claim was false, several negative claims were partly wrong, and the job-posting evidence has evaporated. Do not let a competitive-whitespace claim from this report reach a PRD or a pitch deck without opening the competitor's actual module page first.

### 3. [medium] NEW. The real compliance situation for the target market is a rolling, state-by-state DUAL REGIME through 2026-27: the Codes are in force nationally, but each state's rules (carrying the operative forms, registers and procedures) commence on its own date, and until then employers continue under the pre-existing state Act rules and forms. Roughly six states had notified final rules across all four codes - Gujarat and Arunachal Pradesh effective 21 Nov 2025, Haryana and Madhya Pradesh 1 Jan 2026, Karnataka 1 Feb 2026, Maharashtra 1 Mar 2026 - while Uttar Pradesh, Tamil Nadu and Telangana were still at draft stage and West Bengal, Kerala, Bihar and Chhattisgarh were stalled.

### 6. [high] NEW - ANSWERS THE REPORT'S #2 OPEN QUESTION. Working-time limits are now prescribed, not unknown: an eight-hour normal working day where the wage period is daily; a 48-hour maximum working week where the wage period is other than daily (Rule 5); at least one weekly rest day, normally Sunday in a six-day week, with the employer free to fix another day (Rule 6); AND an employee cannot work more than TEN consecutive days without a full rest day. Working on a rest day requires both a substituted rest day and overtime wages.

PRS review of the Code on Wages (Central) Rules, 2026: 'The Rules provide for an eight-hour work day for an employee whose wage period is on a daily basis... a 48-hour work week.' 'The Rules also state that an employee cannot work for more than 10 consecutive days without a full rest day. If an employee works on a rest day, the employer is required to provide a substituted rest day and pay overtime wages.'

### 7. [high] NEW. Concrete minimum-wage and DA arithmetic now exists and is directly implementable: the minimum wage rate is fixed on a DAY basis; the hourly rate = daily rate divided by 8; the monthly rate = daily rate multiplied by 26. Variable dearness allowance is revised TWICE YEARLY - once before 1 April and once before 1 October - based on the Average Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers. Floor wage may be revised every five years. Note the Rules deliberately do NOT specify the criteria for calculating minimum wage (the detailed 2025 draft criteria - three consumption units, 2,700 calories, etc. - were dropped from the final Rules); those come by separate special or general order.

PRS review: 'To fix the hourly rate, the daily rate will be divided by eight, and for the monthly rate it will be multiplied by 26.' 'The Rules provide that the variable dearness allowance will be revised twice yearly (once before April 1 and once before October 1). This revision will be based on the Average Consumer Price Index for Industrial Workers.' 'The 2026 Rules do not specify such details.'

Source: https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2026/Wages_Rules_2026.pdf

### 8. [high] PARTIALLY CORRECTED. The 'wages' definition carries a 50% add-back: the aggregate of exclusions (a)-(i) is tested against 50% of total pay and any excess is deemed wages. Gratuity (j) and retrenchment compensation/ex-gratia (k) are excluded from the add-back test. Remuneration in kind is capped at 15% of total wages.

Handbook Annexure 1 s.2(y), read directly from extracted PDF: 'If the total of items (a) to (i) exceeds 50% of the total pay (or another percentage notified by the Central Government), the excess amount will be treated as wages.' ## Implications

- THE SINGLE MOST IMPORTANT CORRECTION: the entire compliance corpus this research rests on is CENTRAL-SPHERE material being applied to a STATE-SPHERE market. The Ministry handbook says so in its own title and first chapter, and PRS confirms the 8 May 2026 Central Rules apply to 'central sector establishments... railways, mines, oilfields, and banking companies, and establishments carried on by or under the authority of the central government'. A 20-200 employee manufacturer, retailer, IT firm, hospital or staffing agency is almost never one of those. The Codes bind them; these Rules do not. Before any PRD text is written, someone must obtain the notified rules for the three or four states the wedge will actually sell into. Every form number, every prescribed procedure and several thresholds in this report are central-sphere artefacts and must not be shipped as national defaults.
- THE TIMING ARGUMENT SURVIVES BUT CHANGES SHAPE. The original framing - 'Central Rules are still draft, creating a forced re-implementation event during the build window' - is stale; the Central Rules were notified 8 May 2026, four months before today. The accurate and better framing: the Codes commenced 21 November 2025, and states are now commencing their own rules on a rolling basis (Gujarat 21 Nov 2025; Haryana and MP 1 Jan 2026; Karnataka 1 Feb 2026; Maharashtra 1 Mar 2026; UP, Tamil Nadu and Telangana still draft; West Bengal, Kerala, Bihar, Chhattisgarh stalled). The forced migration is not a single event - it is a rolling per-state wave running through 2026-27, and every multi-state employer sits in several regimes at once. That is a better wedge story than the original, because it is durable rather than a one-off cutover.
- THE WHITESPACE ARGUMENT IS THE WEAKEST PART OF THE ORIGINAL REPORT AND MUST BE REBUILT. Its claim that greytHR - the volume incumbent at this band - 'names no shift, roster, overtime, register, contract-labour or multi-entity capability' was inferred from a marketing index page and is false. greytHR's attendance product explicitly markets shift management, shift rotation, rostering, auto shift detection, overtime policies and muster roll / overtime register / attendance register maintenance; its payroll product explicitly markets FBP; and it markets a multi-entity module. HROne markets contract labour outright. Scheduling, overtime, registers, FBP and multi-entity are all TABLE STAKES, not whitespace. Do not build a positioning deck on competitor absence without opening the competitor's module pages.
- WHAT GENUINE WHITESPACE ACTUALLY SURVIVED VERIFICATION - a much shorter list, and the PRD should be built on exactly this: (1) the s.2(y) 50% wage add-back as a shared, explainable, configurable wage-base service; (2) the Grievance Redressal Committee obligation at 20+ workers, surfaced by no examined product; (3) women's night-work consent as a roster-blocking artefact; (4) welfare-threshold monitoring as headcount crosses 50/100/250/300/500; (5) ISMW journey allowance as a payroll component; (6) canteen and transport recoveries as governed amenity deductions under the 50% cap, confirmed absent from the one manufacturing-specific incumbent page; (7) per-site jurisdiction binding so a multi-state group runs several rule regimes simultaneously; and (8) the rolling state-rule change pipeline. Note that most of these are compliance obligations rather than software categories - which is the correct place for a new entrant to attack.
- RECOMMENDED WEDGE - MANUFACTURING AT 20-200 - STILL STANDS, BUT ON THREE LEGS RATHER THAN FIVE. Surviving: (1) threshold coverage - OSHWC treats a 20-worker powered plant as a factory, the GRC obligation starts at 20 workers, ESI at 10 and EPF at 20, so the entire band is in full statutory scope, unlike BFSI or IT/ITES; (2) budget release - OSHWC s.55 makes the principal employer liable for contract workers' wages on contractor default, which is a liability line rather than an HR-efficiency line; (3) AI fit - extracting rules from newly-notified state rules, generating rosters under six simultaneous working-time constraints, and reconciling contractor challans against wage registers are genuinely AI-shaped problems. COLLAPSED: the whitespace leg (greytHR and HROne both cover far more of manufacturing than claimed) and the timing leg as originally worded. ALSO SIZE THIS FIRST: the contract-labour threshold ROSE from 20 to 50 workers, so a plant with 30 contract workers is now outside ss.45/47 entirely - which may have removed a meaningful slice of the small end of the band from the differentiating obligation.
- ARCHITECTURE: jurisdiction is a first-class dimension, not a setting. Every establishment carries its own appropriate-government sphere and state, and therefore its own rule set, form layouts, commencement date and legacy-regime fallback. A group with plants in Gujarat, Karnataka and Uttar Pradesh is running three regimes with three commencement dates concurrently, and must produce correct registers for each. This single requirement is the strongest reason a new entrant can beat an incumbent whose forms are hardcoded, and it should be the architectural spine of the PRD.
- ARCHITECTURE: the s.2(y) add-back is a cross-cutting calculation that simultaneously changes the PF, gratuity, bonus, retrenchment compensation and leave-encashment bases. It must live in one shared, effective-dated, explainable wage-base service - never duplicated per module - and its 50% threshold must be configurable data, because the Code expressly permits the Central Government to notify a different percentage.
- ARCHITECTURE: contract labour requires physical-world write-back (blocking a worker at a turnstile), not just reporting. But budget for per-vendor device adapters rather than a single integration: the one published API contract found (GET/POST attendance ingest plus a POST negative-ID push, incremental, with 20-character device reason strings) belongs to the Fortuna / Smart Face ecosystem specifically, not to an industry standard. Validate against a design-partner plant's actual device fleet before committing to the manufacturing wedge.
- EVIDENCE QUALITY WARNING FOR THE PRD AUTHOR: four of the original report's market-structure findings rested on Indeed short links that can no longer be resolved, and two of them carried 'high' confidence. The claims that the compliance buyer is a Rs 30-35k/month executive under an IR manager, that IT/ITES bench management runs on Oracle Fusion HCM plus Excel, and that hospitals staff a mandatory-training calendar are now unsupported. These shaped the GTM and pricing recommendations. Re-source them through customer interviews before any of it drives a go-to-market decision - a single job advertisement was never adequate evidence of market structure in the first place.
- PRICING: the manufacturing wedge still implies pricing on total bodies at the gate (own employees plus contract workers) rather than HRMS seats, since the contract workforce often exceeds the permanent workforce in a 20-200 plant and is where the liability sits. HROne's 'billing for active employees only' (verified) shows headcount-volatility-aware pricing is already a competitive dimension. But the evidence for who the economic buyer is no longer exists - treat the buyer persona as an open research question, not a finding.
- STILL VALID AS DELIBERATE REJECTIONS: do not lead with STAFFING/CONTRACT LABOUR (buyer does not fit the band; category occupied by Aparajitha/Simpliance, LabourWorks, Groniva and FaceIT with hardware integration and enterprise sales cycles; reach it instead by inverting the principal-employer data model later). Do not lead with RETAIL/QSR (low end commoditised by SalaryBox and similar, which cover more than the original report credited). Do not lead with BFSI on buyer-size grounds - though note the interesting inversion that banking and insurance are central-sphere, so they are the one vertical where this entire researched corpus applies directly. Do not lead with HEALTHCARE: the credentialing evidence base is thin and got thinner on re-verification.

## Open questions

- State-sphere rules are now THE critical unknown and they replace the original report's central-rules question. For each target state, someone must obtain: the notified rules under all four Codes, their commencement date, the form and register numbering (which will NOT match the central Form I/IV/IX), and which legacy state Act rules they supersede. Priority order should follow the wedge's target states. The state-status tracker used here is a commercial vendor blog and was not verified against state gazettes - verify before quoting any state's status or date.
- The 144-hours-per-quarter overtime ceiling is reported by KPMG and secondary summaries but was NOT confirmed against the gazette text in this pass, and the PRS review of the Wages Rules does not mention it. It may sit in the OSH Central Rules rather than the Wages Rules. Verify before building it as a hard block, and note the equivalent state-level cap may differ.
- Spread-over and rest-interval limits: the Wages Central Rules explicitly defer these to the OSHWC Code and its rules. The OSH (Central) Rules 2026 were not successfully fetched in this pass (SCC Online and DLA Piper both timed out). Obtain them - they carry the spread-over, interval, contract-labour licence forms and annual return format that matter most to manufacturing.
- Whether any Indian SMB/mid-market HRMS actually ships multi-CLIENT (bureau) payroll - one tenant running payroll for many client entities with per-client statutory segregation and per-client billing. Still unverified, and it determines whether staffing is genuine whitespace. Note that multi-ENTITY is now confirmed as widely available, so the question narrows specifically to the bureau/multi-client case.
- Indian nursing and allied-health credential lifecycle: is there a national nurse register or unique ID, do state nursing council registrations expire and on what cycle, and is there any statutory duty-hour cap for nurses or resident doctors. Still unresolved. Do not build the healthcare credential module to an assumed renewal period.
- The NABH HRM chapter's actual standard codes and objective elements, and which are core versus commitment level. Only the ten chapter names and abbreviations were verified, and those from the NABH INTERNATIONAL page rather than the domestic hospital standard used by most Indian hospitals. The description of the HRM chapter's contents remains from a search summary.
- Whether RBI's Master Direction on IT Governance, Risk, Controls and Assurance Practices contains an explicit maker-checker mandate applicable to an HR/payroll application used by a regulated entity. Unverified across both passes.
- Whether background verification is a hard regulatory requirement for BFSI hires in India (and under which instrument) or industry practice. Still unverified. Note greytHR lists background verification as a shipped module, so it is at minimum a commercially expected feature.
- Canonical count of scheduled employments and state minimum wage schedules. Partially advanced: the VDA revision cadence is now known for the central sphere (twice yearly, before 1 April and before 1 October, on CPI-IW) and the daily/hourly/monthly conversions are fixed (divide by 8, multiply by 26). Still unknown: the per-state schedule counts, zone structures and whether states adopt the same twice-yearly VDA cadence.
- How the minimum wage will actually be CALCULATED. The final Rules deliberately dropped the draft's detailed criteria (three consumption units, 2,700 calories, 10% housing, 20% fuel and miscellaneous) and defer to a future special or general order, and they do not say whether minimum wages will vary by geography. Until that order issues, minimum wage rates are external reference data the product must ingest, not compute.
- Piece-rate and production-incentive payroll. Still unverified - no product page or government source examined describes a piece-rate model, and HROne's manufacturing page omits it. Whether Indian 20-200 manufacturers run piece-rate inside payroll or as a separate production-bonus spreadsheet must be settled by customer interviews before scoping.
- Whether the contract-labour threshold rising from 20 to 50 workers has materially shrunk the addressable compliance market at the low end of the 20-200 band. This directly sizes the recommended wedge's differentiator and remains a data check nobody has run.
- Whether canteen and transport recoveries sit inside payroll as authorised amenity deductions (subject to the 50% cap and the acceptance requirement) or outside it as cash/vendor settlements. Determines whether this is a payroll component or a reimbursement ledger - and it is one of the few verified product gaps, so it is worth settling early.
- Maternity obligations were entirely absent from the original report and are unscoped: 26 weeks' benefit at 80 days' qualifying service, six weeks for miscarriage or MTP, two weeks for tubectomy, Rs 3,500 medical bonus, two nursing breaks until the child is 15 months, dismissal protection during maternity absence, and creche at 50+ employees. These are cross-vertical rather than a vertical delta, but they are material to female-heavy retail, QSR and healthcare workforces and belong in whichever dimension owns leave and benefits.
