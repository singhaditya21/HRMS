### 1. [high] CRITICAL QUESTION, PART 1 — At 5-19 employees a substantial statutory payroll obligation DOES exist; the 'EPF at 20 / ESI at 10' framing understates it. Verified verbatim from the gazette First Schedule: EPF Chapter III = 'Every establishment in which twenty or more employees are employed'; ESI Chapter IV = 'Every establishment in which ten or more persons are employed other than a seasonal factory' with a proviso extending it to notified hazardous occupations 'in which even a single employee is employed'; Gratuity Chapter V = 'every shop or establishment in which ten or more employees are employed, or were employed, on any day of the preceding twelve months'; Maternity Benefit Chapter VI = the same 10-employee / preceding-12-months test. So at 10-19 employees the employer carries ESI + gratuity accrual + maternity benefit. Only EPF is genuinely absent below 20. ### 2. [high] CORRECTION to Part 1 — Employee's Compensation is NOT a blanket sub-10 liability. The report quotes Chapter VII as 'applies to the employers and employees to whom Chapter IV does not apply' and omits the opening qualifier. The Schedule actually reads: 'Subject to the provisions of the Second Schedule, it applies to the employers and employees to whom Chapter IV does not apply.' The Second Schedule is a defined list of persons who are employees for this purpose — largely railway, mechanical-power, loading and other manual or hazardous occupations. A 7-person software or services firm does not straightforwardly carry this liability. The report's framing that Chapter VII 'lands precisely on the sub-10 employer' as a general rule is not supported by the text it cites.

Code on Social Security 2020, First Schedule Chapter VII entry, and THE SECOND SCHEDULE heading: 'LIST OF PERSONS WHO ARE EMPLOYEES WITHIN THE MEANING OF THE THIRD PROVISO TO CLAUSE (26) OF SECTION 2'. Read directly from the extracted gazette PDF.

### 5. [medium] RETRACTED FROM PART 3 — 'Free annual health check-ups for workers aged 40+' is NOT a universal obligation binding all employers with no headcount threshold. A 15-person services company in an office does not acquire this obligation. ## Implications

- ANSWER THE CRITICAL QUESTION DIRECTLY: at 5-19 employees real statutory obligation exists — TDS u/s 192 with quarterly Form 24Q and annual Form 16 (no headcount threshold), Professional Tax registration and monthly deduction in the states that levy it (triggered by liability to deduct, not headcount), Shops & Establishments registration, minimum wages, and since 21 Nov 2025 prescribed-format appointment letters for every worker. From 10 employees the employer carries ESI, gratuity accrual and maternity benefit. Only EPF is genuinely absent below 20 — and 2,29,403 employers opted into it voluntarily in FY24 anyway. Two corrections to the round-two version: Employee's Compensation below 10 is qualified by the Second Schedule and is NOT the general sub-10 liability the report claims, and the annual health check-up for workers 40+ is sector-limited and permissive, not universal. Any prior conclusion resting on 'no statutory obligation below 20, therefore no need' is still wrong on the premise. But it lands accidentally right on the money: willingness to pay is capped at ₹0-2,500/month because Zoho, Kredily and EmpXtrack have set the marginal price to zero. Keep the conclusion, replace the reasoning — the reason is competitive price collapse, not absence of need.
- DO NOT PRICE THE SUB-10 SEGMENT. Any paid-only product is dead on arrival below 10 employees. Either give ≤10 away as a funnel into the paid 20-200 band, or explicitly decline the segment. There is no third option that survives Kredily's verified free-forever-unlimited tier. If a free tier is offered, cap it on complexity (one salary structure, one state, one pay schedule) rather than headcount, so the upgrade trigger is the customer's growing compliance surface.
- MAKE 20-200 THE BEACHHEAD AND STATE IT AS SUCH. The statutory step-function is 10 (ESI + gratuity + maternity) → 20 (EPF + grievance redressal committee) → 50 (crèche) → 100 (works committee) → 300 (retrenchment/closure approval, raised from 100 by the new Codes). The competitive vacuum is 20-50: greytHR floors at 50, ADP at 25, Dayforce at 100, Paysquare's 'small' at 100, all independently verified. Design onboarding, pricing tiers and the compliance calendar around the moment a company crosses 10 and then 20.
- BENCHMARK AGAINST TALLY AND THE CA, NOT AGAINST greytHR OR KEKA. TallyPrime's payroll is statutorily complete — I verified the full form list against the official documentation — and costs ₹0 incremental to anyone already on Tally. A feature-parity pitch loses. Win on what Tally structurally cannot do: browser and mobile access, employee self-service, attendance capture, multi-state PT and LWF automation, audit trail, and the new appointment-letter and wage-slip obligations. Put a Tally data-import path in v1.
- PRICE AGAINST THE BUREAU, NOT AGAINST THE SOFTWARE — but do the price research first. At 20-100 employees the alternative is ₹3,000-15,000/month to a bureau (verified) plus a CA relationship whose true 2026 cost is unknown, because the only schedule available is pre-GST. A product at ₹5,000-8,000/month for 50 employees is defensible on ROI. Landing below ~₹100 per employee per month prices the product as an Excel replacement rather than a bureau replacement. The observable paid floor at 20-25 employees is ₹1,499-2,499/month (HivePayroll, RazorpayX, both verified) — price above it and justify the gap with filings delivered. Do not put the ₹37,500/year CA figure in a pricing model or a pitch deck without primary interviews; it is a lower bound from a schedule at least nine years stale.
- MAKE THE FILING THE UNIT OF DELIVERY AND THE UNIT OF MEASUREMENT. ICAI's schedule has no payroll-processing line item — verified by full-text search — and prices TDS return filing, PT registration, PT returns and TDS compliance review instead. The buyer thinks in filings, not payslips. The product's primary artefacts should be the generated-and-filed ECR, ESI challan, PT return, Form 24Q and issued Form 16, and its headline metric should be filings completed on time. This is also the natural surface for AI: compliance calendar, return preparation, reconciliation and exception explanation, rather than a chatbot over an HR database.
- TREAT THE 50% WAGES ADD-BACK AS A FIRST-CLASS COMPUTED FIELD, NOT A CONFIG TOGGLE — conclusion unchanged, reasoning updated. SS Code s.2(88) requires that where excluded allowances exceed one-half of all remuneration, the excess is deemed remuneration and added back into wages, re-basing PF and gratuity for most Indian CTC structures. The report justified versioned effective-dating on the rules being in draft; they were notified 8 May 2026, so that justification is gone. The real justification is stronger and permanent: the statute itself says 'or such other per cent. as may be notified by the Central Government', so the percentage is a standing variable. Build it as a versioned, effective-dated, re-computable rule with retrospective recalculation and a visible audit trail, never a hard-coded 50%.
- SIZE THE MARKET ON 7.66 LAKH, NOT ON 5.3 CRORE. This is the most valuable and most rigorously sourced conclusion in the report and it held up completely against the primary PDF. Only 7,66,254 establishments contributed to EPFO even once in FY2023-24, out of 24,18,266 registered PF codes and 5,31,08,900 Udyam registrations (98.8% micro, averaging 6.8 employees). greytHR alone holds ~34,000 of those employers, about 4.4%. Any TAM slide built on 6 crore MSMEs is fiction. The serviceable base for a paid India payroll product is single-digit lakhs of employers.
- BUILD A CHEAP 'REGISTRATION AND NIL-RETURN CONTINUITY' SKU AS A LAND MOTION. 2,29,403 establishments took voluntary EPF coverage in FY24 with only 0.85 accounts each — both figures verified verbatim. IndiaFilings already monetises this as 'PF & ESI NIL Compliance' for companies with zero employees. A low-price SKU that obtains and maintains PF/ESI/PT/S&E registrations and files NIL returns captures these employers years before they have a payroll worth running. Validate the motive with interviews first — the credential hypothesis is an inference from the account ratio, not an established finding.
- DESIGN FOR THE CA AS A USER, NOT AS A COMPETITOR. The ICAI schedule shows the CA is paid for filings and certification, work that carries professional liability and is not going to a SaaS vendor. Ship a CA-facing role with multi-client switching, a per-client compliance calendar, one-click export of exactly what the CA needs for Form 24Q and PT returns, and read-only audit access. Making them the antagonist forfeits the highest-leverage distribution channel into a band that has never bought software directly.
- BUDGET FOR PRICE OPACITY AS A GO-TO-MARKET ADVANTAGE. Every quote-only vendor I spot-checked confirmed the pattern: HRStop's pricing page carries no numbers, Spine directs to 'Book a Demo', Paybooks' pricing path 404s, and ADP, Dayforce, Paysquare, Neeyamo and Strada publish nothing at any size. Published, self-serve, headcount-banded pricing is by itself a differentiator, and it is the only way to be evaluable by a 30-person company that will not sit through a demo.
- VERIFY COMPLIANCE CLAIMS BEFORE SHIPPING THEM AS MARKETING. Three of this report's statutory assertions were overstated or stale, all in the direction of making the regulatory opportunity look bigger and more urgent. Any compliance claim on a website or in a pitch should be traced to the gazette or the notified rules, not to an advisory-firm alert — the alerts this report relied on were written in January 2026 and were superseded in May 2026. Build a habit of re-checking the notified-rules position before each release.

## Opportunities

- The 20-50 employee band is simultaneously legally obligated and commercially orphaned. At 20, EPF turns on; ESI, gratuity and maternity benefit are already on from 10. Yet greytHR's cheapest plan has a 50-employee floor, ADP's smallest named segment starts at 25, Dayforce's at 100, Paysquare's 'small' means up to 100, and bureaus impose a monthly minimum. No incumbent is designed for a 25-person company that has just crossed the EPF threshold. This is the wedge, and it is the best-evidenced opportunity here — every floor in that sentence was independently verified.
- CORRECTED FRAMING — the labour codes are a real migration event, but a settled one, not an unsettled one. The Codes came into force 21 November 2025 (gazette notification S.O. 5319(E)) and the final Central Rules under all four were notified 8 May 2026, prescribing the format and particulars of the mandatory appointment letter. The report's 'rules still in draft, still unsettled' urgency framing is four months out of date. The practical implication actually improves: a vendor can now build against notified rules rather than guessing, while state rules continue to roll out unevenly. The universal obligations that survive scrutiny are prescribed-format appointment letters and minimum wage for all workers, plus the 50% wages add-back that re-bases PF and gratuity. Do NOT market the annual health check-up as a universal obligation — it is sector-limited and permissive.
- Multi-state Professional Tax and Labour Welfare Fund is a genuine capability gap in the incumbent. TallyPrime's payroll documentation covers PT but contains no reference to LWF anywhere — I verified the absence directly. Saral covers LWF but costs ₹53,700/year minimum. A company with employees in three states is doing PT by hand today. Caveat: whether Tally's TDL partner-extension ecosystem already closes this was not established.
- Sell the filed return, not the payslip. ICAI's fee schedule prices Form 24Q, PT registration and PT returns and contains no payroll-processing line item at all — I searched the full document text and confirmed it. The buyer's mental model of 'what payroll costs' is denominated in filings. A product whose unit of delivery is a completed ECR, ESI challan, PT return, Form 24Q and issued Form 16 speaks the buyer's existing language. Price the comparison carefully, though: the ₹37,500/year anchor comes from a pre-GST schedule and the true current number is unknown and higher.
- Channel is the asymmetry. 28,000+ Tally partners already sit inside the accounting workflow of 2.5 million businesses, and the CA already opens the client's file. A Tally-import path plus CA/partner-led distribution reaches a band that has never bought SaaS self-serve. Competing on Google against Zoho and greytHR in the same band is the expensive way to reach the same buyer.
- Voluntary EPF registration is a live, high-volume behaviour hiding a real job-to-be-done: 2,29,403 establishments took voluntary coverage under Section 1(4) in FY2023-24, 77.8% of all new EPFO registrations that year, yet they carry only 0.85 accounts each. Both numbers are confirmed verbatim in the EPFO annual report. These employers appear to want the compliance credential without the payroll machinery — though note the motive is an inference, not a finding. IndiaFilings already monetises the nearest thing as 'PF & ESI NIL Compliance'.
- Bureau displacement at 20-100 is better economics than Excel displacement at 5-19. The incumbent spend there is ₹3,000-15,000/month to a bureau (IndiaFilings' published bands, verified) plus a CA relationship. That is a real budget line already being paid, to a service whose minimum billing punishes small headcount. Replacing it is a switch of supplier, not the creation of a new budget line — a much shorter sale. Caveat: the ₹10,000-25,000 bureau monthly minimum that makes this argument sharpest rests on a single vendor blog and should be tested with primary interviews before it anchors a pricing model.

- Any specific per-employee price for ADP India, Neeyamo, Paysquare, Dayforce, Strada, Spine, Sensys, Wallet HR, Beehive, HRMantra, HRStop, OpportuneHR or uKnowva — NOT STATED. - **positioning**: Sells statutory filings and certification, not payroll computation.
