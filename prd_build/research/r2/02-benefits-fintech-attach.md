### 7. [medium] The loadable FBP wallet rises roughly 3.8x, from about Rs 2,600 to about Rs 10,050 per month — but the absolute level rests on a market convention, not statute, and swings Rs 5,650-14,450 on that assumption. The RATIO is robust; the LEVEL is not.

My arithmetic: (200 x 2 meals x 22 days) + (15,000/12) = Rs 10,050/month vs prior (50 x 2 x 22) + (5,000/12) = Rs 2,617 = 3.84x. The 2-meals x 22-days convention is now corroborated as professional standard by Taxmann, which computes the maximum annual tax-free meal benefit as 'Rs 1,05,600 (Rs 200 x 2 meals x 22 working days x 12 months)'. But the Rules fix a per-meal cap, not a meals-per-day count. SENSITIVITY I RAN: at 1 meal/day the wallet is Rs 5,650/month (ratio 3.73x); at 2, Rs 10,050 (3.84x); at 3, Rs 14,450 (3.89x). Any TAM or attach model multiplying the absolute wallet must carry this sensitivity; conclusions that depend only on the ~3.8x expansion are safe.

Source: https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/opinion-meal-voucher-tax-benefit-under-new-tax-regime/

### 8. [medium] RESOLVED (was the prior draft's biggest open question): the meal-voucher exemption IS available under the new tax regime from FY2026-27. The proviso that previously blocked it was not carried forward into the Income-tax Rules 2026.

The prior draft could only cite self-interested vendor commentary and flagged this as load-bearing and unverified. ### 15. [high] CITATION CORRECTED: the hard regulatory ceiling is 30% of gross written premium for general insurers and 35% for standalone health insurers — but the operative instrument is the 2024 Regulations, not the 2023 ones the prior draft cited, which were repealed.

## Implications

- ANSWER TO THE DIMENSION QUESTION: yes, an Indian HRMS positioned around benefits is a distribution business wearing software clothing — at the company level. The only listed pure-play earns 5.3% of net revenue and 2.4% of gross revenue from software, and says so itself ('Platform fees 2%'). The PRD must state this as a premise. But state it at the level the evidence supports: it is a fact about Zaggle's whole revenue mix, NOT a measured per-employee benefits attach rate.
- DO NOT CARRY ANY PEPM ATTACH NUMBER FROM THE PRIOR DRAFT INTO A MODEL. Two of them were unsound. (a) The Rs 161/user/month 'money movement per employee' is blended across corporate vendor payments, branch opex and channel-partner incentives — the CEO attributes interchange growth to Zoyer and BROME ('branch recurring operating monthly expenses'), and the implied 6.1% rate on the pre-Apr-2026 wallet is 3-4x plausible prepaid interchange. (b) The 'Rs 150-500 PEPM at a 1.5-5% take rate' band was built on a take rate the same report explicitly said it had not computed and must not borrow from Pluxee. Both are removed.
- THE PROFIT POINT SURVIVES AND IS THE MOST DECISION-RELEVANT FINDING. Zaggle gives back 67% of program-fee revenue as incentives and cashback; whole-company Adjusted EBITDA is 9.9% of gross revenue and approx Rs 39 per user per month. Attach REVENUE is large; attach PROFIT is comparable to a healthy Rs 50-150 PEPM software line, and is far more competed, more capital-hungry and more regulated. The correct conclusion is 'attach is how you defend and expand ARPU', not 'software should be free'.
- Reject the free-HRMS-monetised-on-interchange model. It requires winning an incentive war against a listed incumbent with 19 bank partners, 50mn cards and a 51% 3-year CAGR, using your own balance sheet for float, at 9-10% margins and 10% ROE. Zaggle is buying software at approx 95% gross margin precisely to escape that trap.
- Model revenue in two separate lines and do not blend them into one ARPU: (1) software PEPM, forecastable; (2) attach per employee per month — which this research CANNOT currently size. Carry it as a named-assumption range with an explicit 'unverified' label, not a point estimate. The only defensible anchors available are: EWA = Rs 0 to the employer (verified); group health commission bounded above by the non-life industry average of 15.3% of premium (IRDAI) and, on IRDAI's market-wide group premium of Rs 2,233 per life per year, worth roughly Rs 9-28 PEPM at the low end, with corporate GMC somewhere above that and unmeasured.
- DO NOT SEQUENCE GROUP HEALTH BEFORE CARDS ON REVENUE GROUNDS — that recommendation is withdrawn. It rested on group health being 'plausibly the largest per-employee attach' at Rs 62-312 PEPM, a figure sourced to a competitor's marketing blog implying premium 4.4-11.2x the IRDAI market-wide average. There may still be good PRODUCT reasons to build group health workflow first (it is annual and renewal-anchored, needs no float or card issuance, and leans on joiner/leaver/dependant data the HRMS already owns) — make that argument on product and capital-intensity grounds, and get real broker economics before making it on revenue.
- Build the benefits data model into v1 even if no benefit is monetised at launch. Retrofitting FBP declarations, wallet configuration, proof-of-spend, dependant records and endorsement history into a shipped payroll engine is expensive. The data model is the option value on the entire attach business; licences and partnerships can come later. This is the one attach recommendation that holds regardless of how the unit economics resolve.
- Assume attach requires regulated partners. Insurance distribution sits under an IRDAI EOM ceiling of 30% of gross written premium (35% for standalone health insurers) — cite the Expenses of Management, including Commission, of Insurers Regulations 2024, Regulation 6, NOT the repealed 2023 regulations. Commission is set by board-approved policy inside that envelope, so rates are negotiated and compressible, not tariffed. Card issuance requires a bank/PPI partner. The PRD needs a named partner strategy and a build-vs-partner decision per attach product.
- Treat the distribution logic as a competitive threat, not just an opportunity. Jupiter bought sumHR outright to source salary accounts — reportedly for about Rs 7.5 crore. Fintechs can subsidise HRMS software to near-zero and recover it downstream, and the software asset alone may be valued cheaply. The competitive section must model price pressure from fintech-subsidised HRMS, and the moat argument has to be product depth and AI, not price.
- The Apr-2026 regulatory tailwind is now better-supported than the prior draft could show — the new-regime availability point is independently confirmed by Taxmann (Rule 15(5)(a)), not just asserted by a vendor. Still run the sensitivity: it is a rules change and is reversible, and the absolute wallet figure depends on a meals-per-day convention rather than statute. Have tax counsel confirm before it anchors a TAM.
- Fix the sourcing standard before the next research pass. The two findings that failed here both failed the same way: a specific rupee figure taken from a vendor's own marketing page and then compounded through arithmetic into a decision-grade number. Every attach figure entering the PRD should trace to a filing, a gazette, or a regulator's data table — all of which existed and were reachable in this domain.

## Opportunities

- The Apr-2026 perquisite reset is a narrow, dated timing window, and it is now confirmed to apply under the NEW tax regime as well as the old — which is what makes it large. Meal moved Rs 50 to Rs 200/meal and gifts Rs 5,000 to Rs 15,000/yr effective 1 April 2026, expanding the loadable wallet roughly 3.8x. Zaggle reports corporates re-papering wallets right now, with 73% of new users coming from upsell into existing accounts. Every Indian employer is revisiting its FBP configuration in FY27 — a reason for an HRMS to enter the benefits conversation this year rather than later. Use the 3.8x expansion ratio, which is robust; do not build on the absolute Rs 10,050 wallet without carrying the meals-per-day sensitivity.
- Own the FBP configuration layer without issuing anything. The scarce asset is the employee-level declaration, proof-of-spend and payroll-tax computation — HRMS-native data. Card issuance, KYC and float are the commodity, and the part that consumes capital and regulatory licence. Being the system of record that decides where the wallet load goes is the leverage point.
- Multi-issuer marketplace rather than exclusivity. Zaggle runs 19 bank partners; an HRMS that lets an employer keep Pluxee or Zaggle while the HRMS owns configuration and reconciliation removes the switching objection that kills bundled deals.
- Group health endorsement workflow is a real product gap independent of its revenue value. An HRMS already holds joiner/leaver/dependant data, which is exactly the endorsement pain Plum advertises solving. Note this is now framed as a PRODUCT wedge, not a revenue thesis — the prior draft's claim that group health is the richest per-employee attach is retracted as unsupported.
- Float and reconciliation as a second layer. Pluxee earns 12.4% of revenue as float income. Any prefunded benefits flow generates balances; even without issuing, an HRMS can negotiate revenue share on float with an issuing partner. Note this cuts both ways — Zaggle's own float is a live analyst concern on cash flow and ROCE.
- EWA as free retention surface. It costs the employer nothing and the employee pays, so it can be bundled at zero cost to lift stickiness and blue-collar app engagement without touching software price — provided it is never modelled as revenue.
- Zaggle's own strategy points at the gap: it is acquiring software (DICE, TaxSpanner, ZatiX) at approx 95% gross margin to fix a 9.9% blended margin, and it bought ~100 AI engineers with DICE. A credible AI-first HRMS is the asset that side of the market needs and cannot easily build — both a partnership opening and an eventual acquisition path. The Jupiter/sumHR precedent suggests acquirers may value such an asset cheaply, so this is an option, not an exit plan.

## Open questions

- Zaggle's take rate remains uncomputable — it reports program fees but no TPV/GTV (I confirmed 'GTV' appears in the deck only as a business-model label with no figure). The FY26 annual report MD&A or a direct IR question is the path.
- Zaggle does not disclose the revenue split between its Save (employee benefits) business and its Zoyer/BROME corporate-procurement business. This is now the single most valuable missing number in this dimension: without it, no employee-benefits attach rate can be derived from the only legible player in the Indian market. Ask IR directly for the Save-vs-Zoyer revenue split.
- Zaggle's 'aggregate users' explicitly includes channel partners and customers-of-customers alongside employees, and the mix is never disclosed. Every per-user figure is therefore an artifact of a contaminated denominator as well as a blended numerator.
- What is the actual premium per employee for employer-sponsored corporate group medical in India? IRDAI's market-wide group average is Rs 2,233 per life per year; vendor marketing claims Rs 10,000-25,000 per employee per year. The gap is roughly 10x and it drives the entire group-health attach case. IRDAI's public disclosures (NL-form segment reporting by line of business) or a broker's filings would settle it.
- What commission do brokers actually earn on corporate group health specifically? The IRDAI non-life industry average is 15.3% of premium, but that is dominated by retail motor and retail health; negotiated corporate group business is typically lower. A listed broker's or corporate agent's filings would give the real rate.
- No MCA/Tofler/Zauba filings were obtained for any private player. Plum, Onsurity, Nova Benefits, Loop Health, Zeta, Happay and Refyne all file annual accounts; broker entities' revenue IS commission, which would give real take rates instead of the vendor estimates this dimension had to fall back on. This remains the highest-value unexplored source.
- Onsurity's pricing and scale are still completely unknown — the entire domain is blocked by an SSL-inspection interstitial across three URLs I tried. It is the most explicitly PEPM-priced player in the segment and must be checked manually or from a different network.
- Zeta and Happay were not researched. Zeta is a banking-tech issuer processor (Sodexo/Pluxee relationship); Happay was acquired by CRED in 2021. Both bear on whether issuance is commoditising and what CRED intends in corporate spend.
- Bank salary-account referral economics are unquantified. Zaggle confirms the mechanism ('share of interchange and referral fee from the banks') but no rupee value per account sourced was found. Bank annual reports or a direct channel conversation would be needed.
- NPS and retirement distribution was not covered at all — no PFRDA point-of-presence economics, no corporate NPS attach rates. PFRDA publishes POP commission structures and would be a clean government source. This is a genuine gap, not a dead end.
- Did Jupiter's sumHR acquisition actually deliver salary-account volume, and was the consideration really Rs 7.5 crore? Both need MCA confirmation. A post-mortem would say a great deal about whether HRMS-as-distribution works in practice or only in theory.
- Zaggle's float/prepaid balance size and any float income are undisclosed, though analysts pressed on cash flow and ROCE (ROE 10%, ROCE 14%). This determines how much working capital an issuing attach actually consumes.

## Retracted

- RETRACTED (new, not caught by the prior draft): the per-employee reading of Zaggle's program fees. The prior draft's headline that 'money-movement revenue per platform user is roughly 17x software revenue per user — approx Rs 161 vs approx Rs 10 per user per month' is arithmetically correct as a revenue-mix ratio but cannot be read as benefits/FBP attach economics. Zaggle's own deck scopes its spend base to include vendor payments (raw material, tax, rent, utilities) and channel-partner incentives; BROME is 'branch recurring operating monthly expenses'; the CEO attributes interchange growth 'largely driven by performance of our Zoyer and BROME solutions'. Decisive check: Rs 160.7/user/month against the pre-Apr-2026 wallet of Rs 2,617/month implies a 6.1% take rate versus 1-2% plausible prepaid interchange. The 16.8x mix ratio stands; the per-employee benefits interpretation is withdrawn.
- RETRACTED (new): the prior draft's PRD guidance to model 'FBP/card approx Rs 150-500 PEPM gross at 1.5-5% take rate on a post-Apr-2026 Rs 10,050 wallet'. This directly contradicted the same report's own retracted_claims, which stated no take rate had been computed and that Pluxee's ~5% 'must not be quoted as an India take rate'. The band is arithmetic on a disowned input and is removed entirely.
- RETRACTED (new): 'Group health insurance distribution is plausibly a LARGER per-employee attach than card interchange' at Rs 62-312 PEPM. Back-solving that band implies premium of Rs 9,920-24,960 per employee per year against IRDAI's market-wide commercial group average of Rs 2,233 per life per year (Rs 61,434.68 crore over 275.1mn lives, FY25) — 4.4x to 11.2x. On the government anchor at industry commission rates the figure is Rs 9-28 PEPM. The true corporate-GMC number lies between and is unmeasured, but the comparative ranking against card interchange is unsupported, and the consequent recommendation to sequence group health before cards on revenue grounds is withdrawn.
- CORRECTED (new): the prior draft cited the IRDAI (Expenses of Management of Insurers transacting General or Health Insurance business) Regulations, 2023 as the operative gazette source for the 30%/35% ceiling. Those regulations were repealed by Regulation 24 of the IRDAI (Expenses of Management, including Commission, of Insurers) Regulations, 2024 (notified 23 Jan 2024, effective 1 Apr 2024), and the 2023 text carried its own three-year sunset. The limits carried over unchanged at Regulation 6(1)/(2). The substantive claim survives at high confidence; the citation was to dead law.
- CORRECTED (new): the '2.2% of our overall revenue' and 'core monetization is driven majorly by transaction-based revenues' quotes were attributed to Zaggle's CFO. Both are from CEO Avinash Godkhindi. Also: Pluxee's 5.0% take rate is Employee Benefits operating revenue over Employee Benefits BVI (EUR 500mn/EUR 10.1bn), not group revenue over group BVI as the prior evidence implied. And 'Propel margins fell from 10% to 4%' was an analyst's assertion inside a question, not a management disclosure.
- UPGRADED, not retracted: the prior draft flagged as unverified whether the meal/gift perquisite exemption was extended to the new tax regime, calling it load-bearing. It was. Taxmann (CA Sagar Gambhir, 16 Apr 2026) confirms the denial proviso was not carried forward into the Income-tax Rules 2026 and the benefit is available under the new regime, under Rule 15(5)(a) replacing old Rule 3(7)(iii), with a maximum of Rs 1,05,600/year. Still short of a gazette reading (incometaxindia.gov.in and pib.gov.in return HTTP 403), so tax counsel should confirm, but this is no longer resting on vendor assertion.
- SCOPED (retained from prior draft, still valid): the '67% of program fees given back as incentives' ratio is factual (Rs 5,052.6mn against Rs 7,523mn, FY26) but the incentive line also supports the Propel rewards business, so it overstates the give-back attributable to program fees alone.
- NOT CLAIMED: no Onsurity PEPM price appears in this report. Search snippets suggest Rs 145-699 PEPM, but the entire onsurity.com domain was unreachable across three URLs and no primary figure was captured. It would have been easy to assert a plausible number; I have not.
- NOT CLAIMED: the derived wallet of Rs 10,050/month is presented as convention-dependent, not statutory. The Rules cap per meal, not meals per day. At 1 meal/day the wallet is Rs 5,650; at 3, Rs 14,450. Only the ~3.8x expansion ratio is robust across that range.
