# [r2] critic

## assessment

VERDICT: round two turned an advocacy document into an evidence base. It is now sufficient to write ARCHITECTURE, COMPLIANCE SCOPE, COMPETITIVE POSITION and NON-GOALS with real confidence. It is not sufficient for PRICING, BUSINESS CASE, GTM/CHANNEL, or the competitive strategy for the very band it recommends.

CAVEAT: dimension 6 (attendance) is truncated mid-sentence in what I received. I judged it on partial visibility.

THE MOST IMPORTANT SIGNAL IS NOT ANY FINDING, IT IS THE SHAPE OF THE RETRACTIONS. Roughly sixty retractions across six dimensions, and every single one moves the same direction: smaller, later, more contested, already occupied. Not one found the opportunity larger than round one claimed. That is systematic optimism bias in the research generator, not random error. Consequence: haircut every claim still at medium or low confidence — it came from the same generator that produced sixty one-directional errors. Do not treat surviving unverified claims as directionally neutral.

WHAT IS NOW DECISION-GRADE. (1) The denominator: 7,66,254 EPFO-contributing establishments, verified verbatim, against 24.18 lakh registered PF codes and 5.31 crore Udyam registrations. Kills the 6-crore-MSME TAM permanently. (2) The statutory step function from the gazette: 10, 20, 50, 100, 300, Codes in force 21 Nov 2025, final Central Rules notified 8 May 2026. (3) The price floor, captured in a rendering browser: Kredily free-forever-unlimited, Zoho Payroll free to 10, Zoho People free to 5 then ₹48/user, Frappe free and per-site from ₹410/month, Tally payroll at zero incremental cost inside a base licence held by 2.5m businesses. (4) The AI cost ratio: model choice spans 52.7x on identical workload and is FX-invariant, so it survived every input error.

WHERE ROUND TWO STILL FAILS — the part nobody has said yet.

A. THE RECOMMENDED BEACHHEAD IS THE ONE BAND WHOSE COMPETITIVE SET WAS NEVER RESEARCHED. Dimension 1 calls 20-50 a commercial vacuum. That vacuum is evidenced against exactly four vendor floors, three of which are global BPOs (ADP 25, Dayforce 100, Paysquare 100) that a 25-person Indian company would never evaluate, plus greytHR at 50. Meanwhile Zoho Payroll serves 25 at ₹1,000/month, HivePayroll 25 at ₹1,499, RazorpayX 20 at ₹2,499, Kredily serves it free — and Keka, Zimyo, HROne, Qandle, ZingHR and Pocket HRMS were never checked at all. The band is not empty. It is the most crowded band in the market. The defensible restatement: it is over-served by cheap product and under-served by product that actually files. That changes the wedge from availability to quality-of-delivery, and the PRD must not repeat the vacuum claim as written.

B. THE DEMAND-SIDE CRITIQUE WAS ONLY HALF-ANSWERED. Round one was criticised for no demand-side research. Round two answered with regulatory archaeology and competitor price scraping — both supply-side. Willingness to pay is still inferred from competitor list prices, which is inferring demand from supply. There is zero churn, zero CAC, zero win/loss, zero sales-cycle and zero buyer-conversation evidence in the entire corpus. Nobody has talked to a buyer.

C. THE ARPU DENOMINATOR IS ASSERTED, NEVER SOURCED. The ₹50-150 PEPM band sits under every AI-economics conclusion in dimension 3 and is triangulated from list prices. List is not realised. Indian mid-market discounting is unmeasured anywhere in six dimensions. If realised ARPU is 30-40% below list, every margin conclusion compresses.

D. THE ABSOLUTE AI COSTS REST ON AN INVENTED NUMBER. 23,675 input / 2,045 output tokens per employee-month is an engineering guess with no empirical basis, and every headline figure from ₹0.71 to ₹37.60 derives from it. RATIO conclusions survive; ABSOLUTE ones do not. The PRD may state that model choice dominates margin. It may not state a per-employee inference cost as fact.

E. INTERNAL CONTRADICTION, UNRESOLVED. Dimension 1 calls multi-state PT and LWF a genuine capability gap and differentiator. Dimension 2 says table stakes because Frappe v16 ships PT across 15+ states and LWF across 14, free. Both true against different comparators. Resolution the PRD must state: it is a gap against Tally only, and carries zero differentiation credit against the software field.

F. SCOPE WAS STILL NEVER INTERROGATED, THOUGH THE EVIDENCE NOW MAKES THE ANSWER OBVIOUS. SMB-to-enterprise in one product is indefensible on this evidence. Below 10 the price is zero and three vendors set it there. 10-50 is crowded and monetisation-hostile at ₹18,000-30,000 ACV. 50-200 is where money exists — greytHR averages ~103 employees per customer, the bureau alternative is a real ₹3,000-15,000/month budget line, Tally and CA channels are live. 500+ is quote-only, sales-led, 12-18 month cycles, MCP/governance arguments, and M365 Copilot at $30/seat becomes plausible. Pricing shape, caching policy, channel and competitive set all differ across these. One PRD covering all will be incoherent. Pick 20-200 with 50-200 carrying revenue; defer the rest with named entry criteria.

G. AI-FIRST HAS NO WILLINGNESS-TO-PAY EVIDENCE ANYWHERE. Seven independent data points say the market price for HR AI is zero: greytHR NAVOS bundled, Zoho Zia in the entry paid tier, Oracle bundling with a zero-rated Basic tier, Microsoft zero-rating for Copilot seats, Workday's complimentary allotment, 15Five bundling into its $11 tier, Culture Amp bundling into every band. Dimension 3 states outright there is no evidence an MCP server changed a single buying decision. The headline positioning therefore cannot be the revenue thesis. AI is COGS and an acquisition weapon; payroll accuracy, statutory compliance and filings are the revenue thesis. That is a real repositioning and it is well supported.

H. THE LARGEST COST LINE IS UNSIZED. Reaching statutory parity with a free Frappe v16 and a free-to-existing-users Tally is a multi-year compliance-engineering effort with no estimate anywhere. Dimension 2 correctly inverts this into a product (sell the maintenance SLA against free alternatives) — but that means the company must be structured as a compliance-maintenance operation with a permanent statutory team, not a feature-velocity software startup. That is a founding-team and org-design implication the PRD must state.

SECTIONS THAT WOULD BE PURE SPECULATION IF WRITTEN TODAY: pricing and packaging numbers; revenue model and business case; TAM/SAM/SOM below the EPFO denominator; GTM and channel (CA-as-channel and Tally-partner-as-channel are entirely unvalidated — no CA has been asked a single question); AI monetisation; benefits attach revenue (explicitly retracted, no derivable PEPM); competitive win strategy in 20-200; enterprise 500+ strategy; any absolute per-employee AI cost; device integration effort sizing (failure rate retracted with no replacement).

## sufficient_to_write_prd

Partially. Sufficient for the product spine: scope decision, market sizing, compliance architecture, filings-as-unit-of-delivery positioning, AI cost architecture, attendance and device architecture, data model, and non-goals. Insufficient for pricing, business case, GTM/channel, AI and benefits monetisation, and competitive strategy in the recommended 20-200 band. Recommendation: write the PRD now with those areas explicitly labelled as hypotheses carrying named validation plans and kill criteria, and gate any funding or headcount decision on the field-research programme rather than on the document.

## strategic_conclusions_now_supported

- Market sizing on 7,66,254 EPFO-contributing establishments, not 5.3 crore Udyam or 6 crore MSMEs. Primary-verified, columns reconcile, and greytHR's own 34,000 customers are ~4.4% of that base. Strongest evidence in the corpus.
- The statutory step function is gazette-verified: 10 (ESI, gratuity accrual, maternity), 20 (EPF, grievance redressal committee), 50 (creche), 100 (works committee), 300 (retrenchment/closure approval, raised from 100). Only EPF is genuinely absent below 20.
- Headcount-independent obligations bind from employee one: TDS u/s 192 with quarterly 24Q and annual Form 16, Shops and Establishments registration, minimum wages, and since 21 Nov 2025 prescribed-format appointment letters. The premise 'no obligation below 20' is dead.
- Willingness to pay below 10 employees is capped near zero because Kredily (free forever, unlimited headcount, with PF/ESI/PT/TDS calculation), Zoho Payroll (free to 10) and EmpXtrack (free to 10) set the marginal price to zero. Any paid-only product is dead on arrival there. The conclusion is right; round one's reasoning for it was wrong.
- Tally is the incumbent to benchmark against, not greytHR or Keka. TallyPrime payroll is statutorily complete (PF 3A/5/6A/10/12A plus ECR, ESI 3/5/6, PT statement, Form 16, 24Q with Annexures I-II, 27A, 12BA, NPS, gratuity), sits in the base licence, and costs zero incremental to 2.5m businesses. A feature-parity pitch loses.
- The buyer's mental model is denominated in filings, not payslips. ICAI's schedule has no payroll-processing line item on full-text search and prices 24Q filing, PT registration, PT returns and TDS review instead. Filings as unit of delivery and headline metric is well-founded.
- The 50% wages add-back in SS Code s.2(88) is a genuine computation change, not a config toggle, and must be a versioned, effective-dated, retrospectively recomputable rule with audit trail. The statute itself says 'or such other per cent as may be notified', so the percentage is a standing variable.
- Model choice is the entire AI margin decision: 52.7x spread on identical workload, and being a ratio of two USD prices it survived the FX correction untouched. A model-routing layer is P0 architecture, not an optimisation.
- Rules-first, LLM-last is a correctness decision before a cost decision. No statutory or monetary figure may ever be model-generated, because a wrong payroll number is a legal problem, not a UX problem.
- The market price for HR AI is zero, established across seven independent vendors (greytHR NAVOS, Zoho Zia, Oracle, Microsoft, Workday, 15Five, Culture Amp). Bundle the assistant; monetise only usage-linked surfaces. greytHR already concedes the pattern by pricing Recruit per recruiter rather than per employee.
- Recruiting cost does not track headcount and must be metered separately from PEPM: a 15x spread between a 3%/month and a 15%/month hiring tenant on identical pricing.
- Per-tenant and per-user rate limits are P0. HRMS is priced per employee but consumed per user, and one heavy user can exceed the entire ARPU for that seat. Even Microsoft hard-disables agents at 125% of prepaid capacity.
- ADMS/WDMS push receiver in v1 is the best-evidenced engineering call in the corpus: signed per-tenant HTTP endpoint, no on-premise software, covers eSSL and ZKTeco, corroborated across four independent sources and unshaken by hostile re-checking.
- Worker-initiated WhatsApp architecture, forced by a verified constraint: a new business portfolio can reach only 250 unique users per rolling 24 hours, so a 5,000-worker site cannot be onboarded to employer-push on day one.
- Design for shared devices, not personal devices. 85% of adults used a mobile phone and nine in ten non-users live in a phone-owning household, so worker identity cannot be assumed to equal device identity. This is precisely why terminal integration is not optional for frontline segments.
- Benefits attach at company level is a distribution business wearing software clothing: Zaggle, the only listed Indian pure-play, earns 5.3% of net revenue from software and says so itself. Reject free-HRMS-monetised-on-interchange.
- Published, self-serve, headcount-banded INR pricing is itself a differentiator. Every quote-only vendor spot-checked confirmed the pattern, and it is the only way to be evaluable by a 30-person company that will not sit through a demo.
- Aparajitha/Simpliance is a verified negative-space finding: India's largest labour-compliance organisation, owner of a software suite including payroll, SOC 2 and ISO 27001 certified, makes no AI or ML claim anywhere. The deepest statutory dataset in India has no intelligence layer on it.

## strategic_conclusions_contradicted_by_round2

- KILLED, MOST CONSEQUENTIAL: 'AI is open whitespace against Zoho.' Zia ships in Zoho People's cheapest paid tier at Rs 48/user/month and PeoplePlus names an AI agent as an entry-tier feature. Round one recommended AI as 'the one axis on which the zero-cost alternatives cannot follow'. Zoho already followed, at the bottom of its ladder.
- KILLED: 'The filing layer is the universal paywall boundary and a P0 differentiator.' Zoho Payroll's free tier already includes investment-declaration and proof workflow plus bank salary payouts. The filing wedge is real against Frappe and Kredily only. Against Zoho it is table stakes.
- KILLED: the Rs 96/user price anchor. The real entry price for a Zoho HR product is Rs 48. Round one was anchored one full tier too high, understating Zoho's aggression by 2x at the entry point.
- KILLED: 'Multi-state PT and LWF is a differentiator.' Frappe HR India Payroll v16 ships PT across 15+ states with individual slabs and LWF across 14 states, free and open-source, plus one-click EPF/ESIC/LWF registers, ECR and ESI returns. Any 'more complete India compliance' claim will be falsified in a bake-off.
- KILLED: 'The Labour Code rules are still draft, therefore unsettled, therefore urgent.' Final Central Rules under all four Codes were notified 8 May 2026. Round one's urgency framing was four months stale. This inverts the story from 'be the guide through chaos' to 'build against a settled spec', and removes the justification round one gave for effective-dating (a better, permanent one exists in the statute's own 'such other per cent as may be notified').
- KILLED: 'Free annual health check-ups for workers 40+ apply to all employers with no headcount threshold.' The OSH Code delegates scope entirely to rules, and the notified Central Rules confine it to docks, mines and construction in permissive language ('may arrange'). This was one of only three planks under the universal-obligation claim.
- KILLED: 'Employee's Compensation lands precisely on the sub-10 employer as a general rule.' Round one omitted the opening qualifier; the Second Schedule limits it to largely railway, mechanical-power, loading and hazardous occupations. A 7-person services firm does not carry it.
- KILLED, AND IT WOULD HAVE CAUSED REAL OPERATIONAL DAMAGE: 'cache everything.' On Gemini, hourly context-cache storage makes explicit caching strictly WORSE than no caching for any tenant below ~1,000 employees. A 50k-token per-tenant cache held continuously on Flash-Lite costs ~Rs 34.47 per employee at a 100-employee tenant versus Rs 0.27 for paying full input price — roughly 100x that tenant's entire inference bill.
- KILLED: FX at Rs 83.3/USD. The correct September 2026 rate is ~Rs 94.43. Every USD-derived INR cost in round one was understated by 13.4%, and the rupee fell ~7% in twelve months, so USD-priced COGS against INR revenue silently gave up margin with no usage change.
- KILLED: 'Meta begins charging for service messages in India from 1 October 2026.' Contradicted by Meta's own documentation and two independent BSP sources — that change applies to nine other markets with no service-message change. Round one was letting a false deadline drive architecture.
- KILLED ARITHMETIC, CLAIMED TWICE: that a daily marketing-category WhatsApp template at ~Rs 25.89 per worker per month EXCEEDS greytHR's Rs 45 Essential marginal seat rate. It is ~58% of it, and stays below Rs 45 under every scenario tested including the higher BSP rate with GST. The guardrail is still justified; the comparison was false.
- KILLED: the device integration figure '85-98% success, roughly 1 in 7 devices will not integrate.' It is marketing from the vendor selling the integration fix, and round one applied the floor of a range as the mixed-fleet average. No replacement exists, so device integration effort is currently unsized.
- KILLED: 'TeamLease is roughly 100x any India HR software vendor.' The true multiple is 12-24x. Arithmetically false as stated.
- KILLED BY OMISSION, THE LARGEST HOLE: Keka was entirely absent from the competitive set. It is the best-capitalised India HRMS pure-play ($57M WestBridge Series A, ~Rs 78 Cr FY24 revenue, payroll for 1.5M+ employees monthly) and sits squarely in the recommended beachhead. Its pricing and AI posture are still unverified.
- KILLED: the Multiplier EOR price of $400. Fabricated — not present in its own cited source. The correct 2022 figure is $300 and current pricing is unobtainable. Skuad's $199 is the only current vendor-primary EOR price safe to quote.
- KILLED: 'Group health distribution is plausibly a larger per-employee attach than card interchange, at Rs 62-312 PEPM.' IRDAI government data puts the defensible floor at ~Rs 9-28 PEPM, an order of magnitude lower. The figure came from a competitor's marketing blog implying premium 4.4-11.2x the market-wide average.
- KILLED: the Rs 161/user/month 'money movement per employee' figure and the 'Rs 150-500 PEPM at a 1.5-5% take rate' band. Both had contaminated denominators (Zaggle's user count includes channel partners and customers-of-customers) and contaminated numerators (largely corporate procurement, not employee wallets), and the take rate was borrowed from a different company. No benefits attach rate is currently derivable from anything.
- KILLED: 'Alight is a competitor.' It sold its payroll business to H.I.G. Capital in July 2024 and now positions solely around benefits administration. Strada is the successor. Remove Alight from the set.
- KILLED: 'Ascent HR may have exited.' The inference rested on a DNS lookup of a mistyped domain. The company trades normally at ascent-hr.com and publishes current content on the Central Rules 2026.
- KILLED: the MCP adoption statistics (~28% of Fortune 500, Forrester's 30% by end-2026). Unverifiable and must not be used. Shipping an MCP server is table stakes, and there is still no evidence one has changed a buying decision.
- KILLED: 'Peoplebox funding is a two-source dispute.' Its Tracxn record is self-contradictory on the same page ($1.6M total against a listed $7,743,026 seed). No Peoplebox funding figure may be quoted at all.
- KILLED: 'ComplyHR is a competitor.' Could not be verified as an operating company. Drop it until a legal name is supplied.
- DOWNGRADED TO NEAR-UNUSABLE: the ICAI fee schedule and the Rs 37,500/year CA anchor. Pre-GST, carrying VAT, Service Tax and Wealth Tax heads, at least nine years stale. A lower bound of unknown looseness that must not enter a pricing model or a pitch deck. A pricing recommendation was resting on it.
- DOWNGRADED: the bureau minimum-billing argument. The 5-13x multiple recomputes to 4.4-20.8x, and the underlying Rs 10,000-25,000 monthly minimum is a single vendor's unsourced blog assertion on which a structural market argument was built.
- CORRECTED: self-hosting economics got worse, not better. The H100 list price is $5.49/hr, not the promotional $3.99 expiring 30 Sep 2026, so serverless wins by ~74x rather than ~50x. Also, Llama 3.3 70B at $1.04 flat is NOT the cheap open-weight option; Qwen and DeepSeek Flash tiers at $0.14-0.15 are.
- CORRECTED IN THE FAVOURABLE DIRECTION — the only one in the corpus: OpenAI does offer India data-at-rest residency and its models run on Bedrock for in-country inference. Anthropic offers neither, with a US-only immutable workspace geo. Residency is a per-provider matrix, not a uniform blocker.
- CORRECTED: ESI's threshold is not uniformly 10. ESIC's own coverage page specifies certain establishment categories at 20 or more. The flat 10 is correct for the SS Code First Schedule but must not be stated as today's operative threshold in every state and category.
- METHOD FAILURES THAT INVALIDATED CONCLUSIONS, NOT JUST DATA: esic.gov.in and tgct.gov.in were reported unreachable including by curl; curl returns HTTP 200 for both. Zoho's per-user prices exist only in client-injected DOM, and two rounds produced contradictory pricing until a rendering browser resolved everything in minutes. Every future price and statute capture must use a rendering browser with recorded URL and render date.

## recommended_prd_structure

- 0. EVIDENCE REGISTER AND CONFIDENCE LEDGER (front matter, not appendix). Every load-bearing number with source, capture date, capture method, confidence and a named owner for re-verification. After ~60 retractions across two rounds, the document's credibility depends on this being the first thing a reader sees. Include a standing rule: no compliance or competitive claim ships to a website, deck or contract without a gazette, notified-rule, filing or regulator citation captured in a rendering browser with URL and date.
- 1. SCOPE DECISION AND EXPLICIT SEGMENT EXCLUSIONS. The interrogation round one never did. Commit to 20-200 employees with 50-200 carrying revenue. State why below 10 is declined commercially (price collapsed to zero by three vendors), why 10-20 is acquisition-only (Rs 18,000-30,000 ACV), and why 500+ is deferred (different pricing shape, channel, competitive set and assistant strategy). Give each deferred band a named, measurable entry criterion.
- 2. MARKET SIZING ON THE EPFO DENOMINATOR. 7,66,254 contributing establishments, the size distribution with its cumulative-UAN caveat stated in-line, greytHR's ~34,000 as a 4.4% share benchmark, and an explicit prohibition on any 6-crore-MSME framing. Derive serviceable base from the target band only.
- 3. THE STATUTORY STEP FUNCTION AS THE PRODUCT SPINE. What turns on at 10, 20, 50, 100 and 300; what the product does at each crossing; headcount-independent obligations from employee one; the 50% wages add-back as a versioned, effective-dated, retrospectively recomputable rule; and the corrections round two forced (Employee's Compensation is Second-Schedule-qualified; the 40+ health check-up is sector-limited and permissive; ESI is not uniformly 10 in every category).
- 4. COMPETITIVE POSITION AND THE PRICE FLOOR, WITH A WRITTEN 'WHY NOT X' FOR EACH FREE ALTERNATIVE. Kredily free-unlimited, Zoho Payroll free to 10, Zoho People free to 5 then Rs 48, Frappe v16 free with PT across 15+ states and LWF across 14, Tally at zero incremental inside 2.5m businesses, bureau at Rs 3,000-15,000/month. Include a feature-by-feature Frappe v16 parity table, because that is the bake-off that will actually happen. Mark this section INCOMPLETE pending Keka and the mid-market tier, on the page.
- 5. POSITIONING: THE FILING IS THE UNIT OF DELIVERY AND THE UNIT OF MEASUREMENT. Product artefacts are the generated and filed ECR, ESI challan, PT return, Form 24Q, issued Form 16, prescribed-format appointment letter, wage slip and the four statutory registers. Headline metric is filings completed on time. State plainly that this is parity with Zoho and differentiation only against Frappe, Kredily and Tally.
- 6. PRICING ARCHITECTURE (LABELLED HYPOTHESIS). Size gate not feature gate, copying Zoho's shape. A continuous ramp with no cliffs, since Zoho's 5-user, 10-employee and 25-employee discontinuities are the attack surface. A taper above 200 because Frappe's per-site and Zoho's base-plus-overage both beat flat per-employee at scale. Published self-serve INR pricing as a differentiator. Carry an explicit banner that no number here is validated until the bureau and CA quote programme reports.
- 7. AI ARCHITECTURE AND UNIT ECONOMICS. Rules-first, LLM-last stated as a correctness requirement before a cost one, with a hard rule that no statutory or monetary figure is model-generated. Model router as P0 with per-task cost ceilings and logged escalation. Caching as a per-provider, per-tenant-size policy with the Gemini hourly-storage anti-pattern named explicitly. Per-user and per-tenant token budgets with graceful degradation. Cost modelled in INR with FX sensitivity at 90/95/100 and Gemini at 2x as the BASE case. State that absolute per-employee costs are placeholders pending prototype instrumentation, and only the 52.7x model-choice ratio is load-bearing.
- 8. AI MONETISATION AND THE ZERO ANCHOR. Bundle the assistant into the base plan as an acquisition weapon. Monetise only where the unit of value is legibly incremental: recruiting per requisition or per hire (metered separately from PEPM because its cost does not track headcount, and greytHR already prices Recruit per recruiter), bulk document generation, and an HR-analyst copilot per admin seat. Build full token, cache and cost attribution per tenant, user, agent and model from v1 even while price is zero.
- 9. DATA RESIDENCY, DPDP AND PROVIDER ABSTRACTION. A per-provider matrix distinguishing data-at-rest residency from in-country inference, because buyers ask about both and they are different guarantees. At least three interchangeable backends with residency selectable per tenant. Anthropic has neither for India; OpenAI has at-rest plus Bedrock in-country; Vertex has an India region; Sarvam's hosting is unverified and must not be relied on until it is.
- 10. ATTENDANCE, DEVICES AND THE DESKLESS WORKER. ADMS/WDMS push receiver in v1 with signed per-tenant endpoints. Mixed-fleet normalisation (device registry, per-tenant device identity, punch de-duplication, clock-drift correction, firmware-generation tagging), sized from your own hardware spike rather than the retracted vendor failure rate. A published model-level compatibility matrix and self-serve verification tool as a sales artefact no incumbent offers. Worker-initiated WhatsApp with template-category guardrails. Shared-device identity with a supervisor or kiosk enrolment path. The labour-code attendance engine: 8-hour day, 48-hour week, overtime at 2x, 144 OT hours per quarter, four registers in electronic form with five-year retention.
- 11. CHANNEL, INTEGRATIONS AND MIGRATION (LABELLED HYPOTHESIS). Tally data-import path as P0. Importers from Zoho Payroll, Kredily and Frappe treated as acquisition infrastructure, not integrations, with mid-year joiner handling and prior-employer YTD. A CA-facing console with multi-client switching, per-client compliance calendar, one-click 24Q and PT export, and read-only audit access. An MCP server split into ACL-inheriting read tools, idempotent audited approval-gated write tools, and per-tenant admin controls to disable specific tools per assistant. State that the CA and Tally-partner channels are entirely unvalidated and no revenue forecast may depend on the MCP server.
- 12. BENEFITS AND FINTECH ATTACH: DATA MODEL NOW, MONETISATION LATER. Build FBP declarations, wallet configuration, proof-of-spend, dependant records and endorsement history in v1, because retrofitting them into a shipped payroll engine is expensive and they are the option value on the whole attach business. Model software PEPM and attach revenue as two separate lines that are never blended. State explicitly that no PEPM attach rate is currently derivable and the prior Rs 62-312 and Rs 150-500 bands are retracted. Note the April 2026 perquisite reset (meal Rs 50 to 200, gift Rs 5,000 to 15,000, ~3.8x wallet expansion, available under the new regime) as a dated FY27 timing window carrying the meals-per-day sensitivity.
- 13. STATUTORY MAINTENANCE AS AN OPERATING MODEL, NOT A BACKLOG. The largest unsized cost in the plan. Annual Budget changes, wage-ceiling revisions, state PT slab updates, LWF schedules, and state rules under four Codes rolling out unevenly. Sell the guaranteed-compliance SLA as the product against free alternatives whose feature depth you cannot beat cheaply. State the org implication honestly: this is a compliance-maintenance operation with a permanent statutory team, not a feature-velocity startup, and that shapes hiring, release cadence and gross margin.
- 14. NON-GOALS AND EXPLICIT ANTI-RECOMMENDATIONS. No hardware. No free-HRMS-monetised-on-interchange. No self-hosting for cost (only as a priced compliance SKU for named regulated accounts). No premium AI SKU. No cache-everything default. No paid-only product below 10 employees. No TAM on 6 crore MSMEs. No use of the Rs 37,500 CA figure, the $400 Multiplier price, the Rs 161 PEPM Zaggle figure, the 85-98% device success rate, or the MCP adoption statistics. Each entry carries the evidence that killed it, so nobody resurrects them.
- 15. ASSUMPTION REGISTER AND VALIDATION PLAN. Every field-research question with method, owner, cost, timeline, the specific decision it gates, and a kill criterion stating what result would invalidate the strategy. Minimum set before funding: CA and bureau quotes, Tally payroll enablement rate, realised versus list ARPU, AI willingness-to-pay, CA channel viability, and the Keka plus mid-market competitive sweep.

Fintech-subsidised HRMS on the Jupiter/sumHR precedent, where the software asset itself was valued at ~Rs 7.5 crore. Falsification in a Frappe v16 bake-off. Uneven state-rule notification under the Codes. Each with an observable trigger and a pre-agreed response, not a severity rating.
