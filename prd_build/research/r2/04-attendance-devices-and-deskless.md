# [r2] attendance-devices-and-deskless

## Verification notes

WHAT I DID: independently re-fetched 22 sources, including every primary the report leaned on. I did not accept the original's unknowns — I made genuine fresh attempts on UKG India, Star Link, Realtime, the labour-code primaries, and the WhatsApp India rate card, resolving two of them.

THE MOST IMPORTANT CORRECTIONS, in order of consequence:

1. ARITHMETIC ERROR. The report claims twice that ~₹25.89/worker/month (daily marketing-categorised WhatsApp message) is "more than the entire greytHR Essential marginal seat rate of ₹45." It is not: 25.89 < 45. I recomputed under every scenario — with 18% GST (₹30.55), at the higher BSP marketing rate (₹28.50), and both (₹33.63) — and it stays below ₹45 in all four. The correct figure is ~58% of the Essential rate. The guardrail recommendation survives; the alarming comparison does not.

2. A CENTRAL WHATSAPP CLAIM IS CONTRADICTED BY META'S OWN DOCS. The report treats "service messages become chargeable 1 October 2026" as directional-but-likely. Meta's updates-to-pricing page shows the 1 October 2026 change is standalone rate cards for Bangladesh, Iraq, Nepal, Sri Lanka, Kazakhstan, Kuwait, Morocco, Oman and Ukraine — India is not among them and no service-message change is described. Two further BSP sources I fetched say service messaging remains free with no cap. Moved from low-confidence finding to explicit retraction.

3. SYSTEMATIC SOURCE MISATTRIBUTION. essl.co.in — used as source_url on FIVE findings including eSSL manufacturer claims — is CAMS Biometrics (Dheeram Innovations Pvt. Ltd.), whose site states "Not affiliated with eSSL Security." The report's competitor entry identified CAMS correctly but its finding-level citations propagated the confusion. All corrected. Separately, the data-cost figures were attributed to DataReportal, which contains no cost or consumption data at all.

4. FABRICATED PRECISION IN THE "1 IN 7 DEVICES" CLAIM. It takes the 85% floor of a middleware vendor's 85-98% marketing range and presents it as the mixed-fleet average, while every per-model rate that vendor publishes is 96-98%. No distribution exists, and the source sells the remedy.

5. NUMBERS THAT DID NOT MATCH THEIR CITED SOURCES. Gender internet access: source says roughly two-thirds of men vs half of women; report said 64%/81%. Device prices: source lists ZKTeco K40 and eSSL MB160 at ₹4,000-8,000; report said ₹6,000-8,000. eSSL partners: site names six, report said four. Three device prices (iFace 880, F22, generic eSSL) had no traceable source and are removed.

WHAT I UPGRADED: I found the labour-code final rules notification date of 8 May 2026 via KPMG, which the original could not obtain — this raises the OT-engine requirements (8h daily wage period, 48h week, 2x overtime, 144h/quarter) to high confidence, though I withdrew the unconfirmed "12 hours daily" figure and corrected the wage-period nuance. I also found new register requirements: a register of fines and deductions, permission to keep registers electronically (which is what makes software-generated registers viable), and 5-year retention. And I resolved Star Link from "unknown" to a real competitor that ships its own leave-management software — changing it from a non-entity into a displacement target.

WHAT HELD UP UNDER HOSTILE RE-CHECKING: greytHR pricing (verbatim exact, every ratio recomputed correct); Meta's messaging-limit tiers (verbatim exact, unaltered); the ADMS/WDMS push architecture (four independent sources, the report's best work); DataReportal's connectivity figures (exact); the CAMS and Odoo connector prices (exact); the shared-device statistic (exact); Matrix's opacity (re-confirmed and actually strengthened); and the UKG retraction (correct, and upheld after independent re-testing).

DID THE DIMENSION ANSWER ITS QUESTION? Mostly yes. On devices it is genuinely strong — the build/partner/never-build split is well-evidenced and actionable. On deskless workers it is thinner than it appears: the WhatsApp section is the weakest part (one confirmed arithmetic error, one contradicted claim, unresolved rates), and the report never obtained the rate card that its own unit economics depend on. It also does not address the deskless roster question for QSR, logistics or healthcare at all, which it honestly flags.

HOW MUCH SHOULD A PRD AUTHOR TRUST THIS? The device-integration architecture and the build/partner recommendations: high — act on them. greytHR and Kredily pricing and the labour-code engine requirements: high — act on them. Every vendor scale claim (eSSL's 4M customers, ZKTeco's "No.1", Star Link's 8,000+, Mantra's 6.5M): zero — never repeat. The protocol mapping beyond eSSL and ZKTeco: low — spike it. The entire WhatsApp cost model: do not commit a per-worker price until someone downloads Meta's actual INR rate card. Roughly 70% of this report is sound; the WhatsApp economics and any sentence containing a vendor-supplied number are where a PRD author will get hurt.

## Implications

- BUILD the ADMS/WDMS push receiver in-house, in v1. This is the report's best-evidenced finding, corroborated across four independent sources and unshaken by hostile re-checking. Owning this endpoint means owning the punch stream.
- BUILD the mixed-fleet normalisation layer (device registry, per-tenant device identity, punch de-duplication, clock-drift correction, firmware-generation tagging).
