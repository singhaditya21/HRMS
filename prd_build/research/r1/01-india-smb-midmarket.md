# [r1] india-smb-midmarket

## Verification notes

METHOD: Re-fetched every vendor pricing page directly; where the vendor blocked WebFetch (keka.com TLS cert error; razorpay.com and qandle.com client-render prices; outlookbusiness.com and inc42.com behind SSL-inspection interstitials) I re-retrieved via a text-extraction proxy and said so in the evidence. WebSearch budget was exhausted early (200/200 calls consumed before this pass), so verification was WebFetch-only — that is the single biggest limit on this pass and it is why several funding/market-size items could not be re-checked against a second source.

WHAT I CONFIRMED (unchanged, high confidence): greytHR, HROne, Pocket HRMS, Zimyo, factoHR, Kredily, sumHR and Zoho Payroll India pricing all reproduced exactly as reported from the vendors' own live pages. Keka's pricing page genuinely publishes zero numeric prices (8 tier names, setup-fee language, no currency figures). RazorpayX PRIME ₹2,499/≤20 and ELITE ₹5,499 (struck from ₹8,499)/50 emp/100 cap/₹150 additional/18% GST confirmed. Qandle's USD figures confirmed. Zoho People's USD ladder ($1.25/$2/$3/$4.50) confirmed. Keka FY24 financials confirmed against Entrackr (18 Dec 2024). MYND–Qandle (17 Apr 2025), greytHR NAVOS (3 Jun 2026), RazorpayX Payroll Engine 2.0 (published 4 Mar 2026), and the four Labour Codes (effective 21 Nov 2025, 29 laws subsumed) all confirmed verbatim.

WHAT I CORRECTED — 8 material fixes:
1. sumHR acquisition DATE WAS WRONG. Inc42 is dated 17 Feb 2023 but says Jupiter "acquired the HRtech startup last year" — the acquisition was 2022, not February 2023. The report mistook the publication date for the deal date.
2. HROne "no disclosed funding found" WAS WRONG. Tracxn shows $8.36M over 2 rounds, latest Series A 24 Aug 2024. This inverted a stated weakness.
3. Keka's ₹90/₹120/₹150 per-additional-employee rates DO NOT come from "several aggregators" — they trace to HROne's own competitor-attack blog. Techjockey and SoftwareSuggest carry no such rates. Retracted.
4. factoHR international per-additional-employee is $2/$3/$4 by tier, not a flat $2.
5. HROne's Uneecops parentage is softer than stated — Tracxn lists an associated legal entity but explicitly indicates no parent company. Downgraded from fact to association.
6. Qandle's priced tiers are Regular/Plus/Premium only; "Foundation" appears as a name without a published price.
7. greytHR's add-on list was incomplete — GPS Live Tracking ₹140/user/mo and Recruit ₹2,500/recruiter/mo are the expensive ones the report missed, and Premium's base is unlimited employees.
8. Keka total-raised "~$59.2M" is unsupported; only the $57M March 2022 WestBridge Series A is verifiable.

WHAT I RETRACTED OR DOWNGRADED FOR SOURCE WEAKNESS: the Zoho Zia "agentic, from Essential HR tier upward" claim (sole source was an unreachable third-party SEO blog; Zoho's own People page does not use "agentic" and states no tier); Keka FY25 ~₹134 Cr (Tracxn-only, unreproducible); Kredily's $750K/Fosun/named-angels funding detail (YourStory 403, Tracxn 404, Kredily's own about page discloses no funding at all); Zimyo's "~10% fully diluted" IndiaMART stake; and the market-size figures (both IMARC and ResearchAndMarkets URLs 404).

BIGGEST METHODOLOGICAL FLAW I FOUND: the "review-site centre of gravity" finding. SoftwareAdvice renders a company-size distribution for only the ~19–25 reviews visible on page one, not for the full 90 (Keka) / 297 (greytHR) / 359 (Zoho People) review bases. The report presented a first-page sample as "a clean map of where each incumbent actually lives." Downgraded to low and reframed as directional.

TRUST: High for all vendor-published INR pricing (re-read from source this pass, September 2026). High for the four dated events. Medium for anything about Keka's India pricing (vendor publishes nothing; two review sites agree, which is better than the report implied, so I raised that one to medium). Low for all funding totals, all customer/user counts (every one is self-reported and unaudited, and greytHR's own numbers contradict each other across four of its properties), and all market sizing. Do not use this report for market sizing or for competitive claims sourced to any vendor's blog.

## Opportunities

- The sub-50-employee band is structurally underserved by design. Six of ten vendors (greytHR, HROne, Pocket HRMS, Zimyo, Qandle, factoHR) enforce a 50-employee floor, so a 15-person company pays the 50-person price — an effective PEPM of ₹166 at greytHR Essential and ₹330 at HROne Basic. Only Kredily (free), Zoho People (5-user min), sumHR (no floor) and RazorpayX PRIME (≤20) price honestly here, and of those only Kredily and RazorpayX run Indian statutory payroll. A product that runs compliant India payroll with true per-seat pricing from 5 employees has an uncontested lane.
- Support responsiveness is the segment-wide failure mode and nobody is competing on it. Complaints about unanswered tickets, months-long implementations and four-month support waits recur independently across Keka, greytHR and Zoho People review bases. Every vendor competes on modules and AI; none competes on a contractual support SLA. A published, refund-backed response-time guarantee would be differentiating precisely because the incumbents' own reviewers say it is the thing that breaks.
- Price transparency is collapsing at exactly the maturity point where buyers most need it. Keka publishes no numbers at all; HROne, Pocket HRMS, greytHR, Zimyo and Qandle all go quote-only at their top tier; Pocket HRMS additionally requires 100+ employees for Premium; Qandle hides its real prices on a second URL most buyers never find; RazorpayX's pricing page doesn't even render to crawlers. A vendor that publishes every tier and every add-on in INR, including enterprise, converts the entire segment's opacity into a trust wedge.
- Zoho's split-SKU structure is the largest exploitable cost gap in the segment. Zoho People's headline ~₹60/user/month does not include Indian statutory payroll — that requires Zoho Payroll as a separate purchase (₹1,250–₹5,000/month plus per-employee overage). For a 50-person Indian company the real Zoho stack lands well above its advertised price, while greytHR Essential runs compliant payroll at ₹49.90 PEPM all-in. Any India-native vendor should be selling against the all-in number, not the per-seat one.
- Talent modules are the universal upsell trap, and unbundling them honestly is a wedge. HROne puts Recruitment, Performance, Engagement, Expense, Asset and Helpdesk behind a quote-only Enterprise tier; factoHR gates Performance to Ultimate; greytHR sells PMS as a ₹35–45 add-on or Premium-only; Zimyo charges ₹4,000/recruiter and ₹20,000/license for analytics; Pocket HRMS gates analytics and audit trail to Premium at 100+ employees. A mid-market platform that includes performance and basic analytics in its mainline tier removes the single most predictable source of bill shock in this market.
- The 100–500 employee band is a genuine no-man's-land. RazorpayX caps ELITE at 100 and then jumps to a quote while now targeting 500–5,000 with Payroll Engine 2.0 — it has no published answer for the band it must cross. Pocket HRMS requires 100+ for Premium but hides the price. Keka's published ladder tops out at 100 users. This is the band where India SMBs actually professionalise HR, and it is priced by conversation rather than by page almost everywhere.
- The Labour Codes commencement (21 November 2025, 29 laws subsumed, Central and State rules still settling through 2026) is a live re-implementation event with no visible vendor leadership. None of the ten vendors' pages examined this pass market a Labour Codes readiness position — appointment letters for all workers, gig and platform worker social security, the single-registration/single-return regime. Compliance is the one thing India SMB buyers will switch systems for, and the window is open now.
- AI branding has commoditised while AI governance has not. Eight of ten vendors market an assistant or agent, but only Kredily publishes governance specifics — permission scoping, human approval gates for irreversible actions, and an audit log. For a system that touches payroll, 'what stops the agent from doing something wrong, and can I prove what it did' is the buying question, and almost nobody is answering it in writing.
