# [r1] ai-in-hr-2026

## Verification notes

SCOPE AND METHOD. The WebSearch budget for this session was exhausted before I began (200/200 used), so every verification was done by fetching primary and cited URLs directly - roughly 40 fetches. This constrained me: I could re-read sources the report cited and guess at primary URLs, but I could not go hunting for alternative corroboration when a cited source was unreachable. Where that happened I downgraded rather than assumed, which is the conservative direction but means some claims marked "not verified" may well be true.

WHAT I VERIFIED CLEANLY AT PRIMARY SOURCE (11 findings, now high confidence): the EU AI Act Annex III delay to 2 Dec 2027 (EC's own page); Workday's Q2 FY27 AI numbers (both the press release and the earnings transcript, with every quote and speaker attribution matching); the Null Compliance LL144 study (4.6%, 391, 155, 18, 13); DPDP s.7(i) verbatim; the DPDP Rules 2025 phasing and SDF algorithmic due-diligence duty; Darwinbox Cortex's pilot status and its two named pilot customers; greytHR's pricing and free NAVOS bundling; the Greenhouse candidate survey (17 of 18 figures exact); the SHRPA India maturity percentages; the arXiv resume-bias paper 2606.28978; and the ADP agent announcement, which matched verbatim including the "under human oversight" language.

THE MAIN FAILURE MODE WAS AS PREDICTED - unsourced precision. I retracted 18 claims. The most serious: HireVue's "0.25%" facial-analysis figure, which the report called "the most important published number" in the category and which exists in no reachable source; the Betterworks 90%/42% statistics, which are simply not on the page they were attributed to; and the Nature attrition decimals (98.72%, 97.29%, 0.994), behind an auth wall and untraceable. Also stripped: Eightfold's "$7-10 PEPM" and "$2.1B valuation", Darwinbox's "$60.7M revenue" and "130 countries", Mercor's "$20B July 2026" round, micro1's "$500M run rate" and "300,000-interview dataset", ZingHR's imported logo headcounts, and the entire block of interview-fraud statistics.

TWO STALENESS ERRORS MATTERED STRATEGICALLY. First, the Dayforce "still describes limited availability" claim rests on a page saying "available to a select number of Ceridian customers... in 2024" - a two-year-old artifact predating the rebrand, presented as current evidence. Second, the report's Eightfold profile was stale in a way that inverted its conclusion: Eightfold now states "it never decides. Your recruiters do." and has shipped an AI Interviewer with named customers and attributed outcomes, none of which the report captured while attacking it for a "potential scoring" framing not present on the current site. I also caught the SAP Joule agents being listed as "Expected GA: May 2026" in a forward-looking article that the report read as confirmation of shipping - and that misreading was load-bearing, since it was used to rank SAP ahead of Darwinbox.

ONE NUMERIC CORRECTION WORTH FLAGGING: the arXiv 2505.12114 disparate-impact ratios were exactly right (0.290 to 3.306, etc.), but all six group score means were wrong by 0.003-0.011 in the report. Small, but it indicates the numbers were transcribed loosely rather than read, which is a reason for mild systematic caution about other transcribed figures I could not check.

BIAS CORRECTIONS BEYOND FACT-CHECKING. I removed the "verifiable" framing from unaudited vendor volume claims (Alex's 1M candidates was called "the largest verifiable" claim - it is the largest claimed). I added the Null Compliance authors' own caveat that a null result cannot be read as non-compliance, which the report omitted and which materially softens the "dead letter" framing. I noted that Greenhouse has a commercial interest in AI-interview scepticism. I flagged that the report's Mercor "net revenue" band was its own arithmetic. And I noted that the report's "everyone in India bundles AI free" thesis rests on one verified vendor, not two.

WHAT I ADDED. Only three things, all directly verified: Eightfold's repositioning and AI interviewer; the Dayforce staleness; and the SAP GA correction. I did not add findings to appear productive.

HOW MUCH TO TRUST THIS. High confidence in the 11 cleanly verified findings and in all 18 retractions - a retraction only requires showing the source does not support the claim, which I did in each case. Medium confidence in the regulatory findings I could not re-reach (US state law, EEOC/Colorado, MeitY): these are mostly pre-cutoff facts I have independent reason to believe, but they are marked source-unreachable rather than confirmed. Low confidence, and explicitly labelled as such, on the interview-fraud statistics, the Indian IT services deployment figures, the deflection benchmarks, the attrition literature and the Bersin projection. Net: the cleaned report is substantially more defensible than the original, but roughly a third of it now rests on "could not re-verify" rather than "confirmed false", and the open_questions list is where the remaining risk is concentrated.

### 12. [high] Bias in LLM resume screening is real, measurable, and unstable across model versions - the direction of bias flipped between model generations, so a one-time bias audit certifies nothing after a model upgrade.

## Opportunities

- Publish the metrics nobody else publishes. Across 18 vendor sites read at primary source in this pass, not one published a model-versus-human agreement rate, a hallucination rate, or an adverse-impact ratio. Alex holds ISO 42001 and still says nothing about bias auditing; HireVue, the most scrutinised vendor in the category, makes no LL144, EU AI Act or ISO 42001 claim at all. A single honest metrics page is a defensible differentiator that costs engineering time rather than R&D breakthrough.
- Treat model upgrades as a compliance event and ship continuous fairness monitoring rather than annual audits. This is now evidence-backed rather than speculative: arXiv 2606.28978 verified that the direction of resume-screening bias reversed between model generations across 14 LLMs, which means a point-in-time bias audit certifies nothing after the next upgrade. No vendor in this set offers monitoring on that cadence.
- Compete on scope discipline, not scope breadth. The two vendors with the strongest verified production evidence - Paradox and Workday - both win on logistics automation (scheduling, messaging, orchestration), and Workday's own flagship number is 8 million auto-scheduled interviews. Meanwhile Eightfold has moved to explicit non-decisional positioning. Scoping AI to logistics and drafting, with human adjudication, is where the evidence actually is.
- Design for the disclosure specification the candidate research already gives you. Greenhouse's verified numbers are effectively a product spec: 44% want upfront disclosure, 46% want a human option, 38% want human review before decisions, and 81% accept the same or more AI given safeguards. Betterworks' verified 75% who accept AI-generated reviews provided a manager reviews and adjusts them points the same direction. Building disclosure, opt-out and human review as defaults converts the largest objection into a feature.
- Exploit the India consent advantage deliberately. DPDP s.7(i) was verified verbatim and removes the employee-consent friction that GDPR-bound competitors carry, while the SDF 'algorithmic due diligence' duty in the DPDP Rules 2025 is the one hook that bites AI. Building to the SDF standard early - DPIA, independent audit, algorithmic risk documentation - is cheap now and becomes a procurement differentiator by the May 2027 compliance date.
- Solve payroll anomaly detection with an explicit human approval gate and publish false-positive rates. ADP's language ('under human oversight') was verified and is the honest design; Dayforce's 'Autonomous Payroll' evidence turned out to be a two-year-old page. Nobody in the category publishes a false-positive rate, which is the number a payroll manager actually cares about.
- Assume AI is bundled, not upsold, in India SMB. greytHR's free NAVOS bundling across all tiers from Rs 2,495/month is the one pricing fact fully verified at primary source in this report. Note the correction: the Zoho half of the original 'everyone bundles free' thesis could not be verified, so the anchor rests on one vendor - worth confirming with a second before treating it as a market-wide ceiling.
- Attach every outcome claim to a named customer. PeopleStrong publishes five outcome percentages with zero customer attribution; ZingHR publishes 70%, 90% and '0 manual payroll interventions' against a logo wall with no linkage; Leena AI's 70%+ auto-resolution touches none of its thirty named logos. Paradox does the opposite and it is why its evidence reads as credible even though the numbers are equally self-reported.
