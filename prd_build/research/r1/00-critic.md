# [r1] critic

## assessment

The sweep is strong on one axis and structurally incomplete on three. What it does well: vendor-primary price cards, an unusually disciplined retraction log, and a statutory-payroll dimension that is genuinely build-grade. What it is not: a market study. It is a study of eleven vendors' websites plus one PSU tender. Almost every conclusion is derived from what vendors publish about themselves, and the sweep repeatedly mistakes "no vendor publishes X" for "X is a defensible opportunity." Seven of the eight SMB opportunities and five of the seven enterprise ones are publishing/positioning moves (publish prices, publish metrics, publish an SLA, publish a Labour Codes stance) that any incumbent can copy in a sprint. That is a marketing plan, not a product thesis, and a PRD built on it will have no moat section.

Three structural gaps matter more than any individual missing fact.

First, the demand side was never researched. Zero buyer interviews, zero RFP corpus beyond one bank tender, zero job-posting or implementation-failure analysis, no data on who actually runs payroll in an Indian 30-person company today. The sweep's headline SMB wedge — "compliant India payroll with true per-seat pricing from 5 employees has an uncontested lane" — is asserted without ever naming the actual sub-50 incumbent, which is Excel plus TallyPrime plus a ₹1,500–5,000/month chartered accountant, not greytHR. Worse, the sweep's own statutory dimension contradicts the wedge: EPF applies at 20+ employees and ESI at 10+, so a 5–19 person company has almost no statutory payroll obligation beyond TDS, PT and payslips. The lane may be uncontested because it is not a market.

Second, the AI layer was researched as positioning, never as engineering economics. Nobody costed inference. Oracle charges $5/employee/month for AI agents alone; the sweep's own India ARPU band is ₹50–140 PEPM (~$0.60–1.70) for the entire product, and its pricing recommendation is to bundle AI free on greytHR's precedent. Those two facts are irreconcilable and the sweep never notices. The single most PRD-determining question for an "AI-first India HRMS" — what token budget per employee per month does Indian ARPU actually buy, and what does that force architecturally — is asked nowhere across two full AI dimensions.

Third, scope was accepted rather than interrogated. The brief says SMB + mid-market + enterprise; the sweep dutifully covers all three and never says the obvious thing its own evidence proves: a new entrant cannot clear Indian enterprise procurement for years. The PSU tender requires ₹500 crore average turnover, ₹40 lakh EMD, and three years of proven HRMS implementation in a PSU or 7,000+ employee private bank, with POCs explicitly disqualified. Meanwhile the SMB lane requires a self-serve, sub-₹100 PEPM, zero-touch product. These are not one product and arguably not one company. A completeness critic's first duty is to say so.

Two methodology failures compound this. (a) The sweep says "revenue unverifiable" for greytHR, Zimyo, HROne, Pocket HRMS, factoHR, Qandle and Darwinbox while every one of them files annual returns with the MCA, retrievable from Tofler/Zauba/MCA21 for a few hundred rupees each. That is a purchasing decision, not an information gap, and it leaves the entire competitive set unsized. (b) All market sizing was retracted or downgraded to low, so the PRD has no TAM — yet nobody attempted the bottom-up substitute (MCA-registered active companies by headcount band × realistic PEPM × attach rate), which is fully doable from public data and would be more credible than any analyst report.

Finally, the sweep's own rule is violated inside itself: it correctly quarantines hrone.cloud as competitor marketing in the SMB dimension, then retains HROne's synthesized PEPM bands as the only pricing data in existence for the entire India enterprise segment. And keka.com is simultaneously "blocks all fetches, the single most consequential research gap" in two dimensions and fully quoted (pricing tiers, per-employee rates, AI itemisation, governance copy) in a third. Until that is reconciled, the price anchor for the market leader is unknown.

## missing_dimensions

- ATTENDANCE HARDWARE AND THE DESKLESS WORKFORCE — entirely absent, and the product scope explicitly includes attendance. No research on the biometric/face device installed base (eSSL, Matrix Comsec, ZKTeco, Realtime, Mantra, Secureye, Star Link), device integration protocols (ADMS/push, vendor SDKs, on-prem connectors, offline sync), GPS/geofencing for field staff, or shift/roster engines. In Indian manufacturing, retail, logistics and healthcare, HRMS deals are routinely won or lost on whether you talk to the customer's existing device fleet. This is a build-vs-partner decision with real engineering cost (an on-prem agent, a device-protocol layer) that the PRD currently has no basis to make. Note the only datapoint in the whole sweep is greytHR pricing GPS tracking at ₹140/user/month — 3x its base rate per head — which is a loud signal that this is monetisable and nobody looked.
- WHO ACTUALLY RUNS PAYROLL TODAY (the demand-side baseline) — no primary research on the real incumbent in each band: Excel, TallyPrime's payroll module, Busy/Marg, a legacy desktop tool (Saral PayPack, Spine, Sensys, Relyon), the company's CA or a payroll bureau. The SMB thesis rests on an assumption about the status quo that was never tested. Also missing: what the HR ops person's month actually looks like (payroll input cutoff, attendance reconciliation, arrears/retro, mid-year joiners with previous-employer income, investment-proof season), which is where the product either earns its place or does not.
- AI UNIT ECONOMICS AT INDIAN ARPU — no dimension costed inference against a ₹50–150 PEPM price point. This determines everything downstream: whether AI can be bundled (as the sweep recommends on a single greytHR datapoint), what fraction of the stack must be deterministic rules rather than LLM calls, whether to run small/local models, how aggressively to cache and RAG, and whether the AI layer is a feature or a separate SKU. Oracle's $5/employee/month AI SKU is 3–4x the entire India ARPU and the sweep quotes it without drawing the inference.
- EMPLOYEE-FINANCIAL-SERVICES MONETISATION — the actual business model of several Indian HRMS players, and it was never examined. Corporate salary accounts (the sumHR/Jupiter case is noted but its economics are not researched), FBP and meal-card issuance (Zaggle is a LISTED company with audited financials showing exactly how large this attach is; Pluxee/Sodexo, Zeta, Happay), group health insurance distribution (Plum, Onsurity, Nova Benefits, Loop), earned wage access (Refyne, Jify, KarmaLife), NPS. At ₹100 PEPM SaaS revenue, many of these vendors are not software businesses. If the attach is worth 3–10x the licence, the entire pricing strategy in the PRD is wrong and 'publish a transparent price card' is the wrong wedge.
- CONTRACT LABOUR AND PRINCIPAL-EMPLOYER COMPLIANCE — not mentioned once, despite being the largest headcount pool in Indian mid-market and enterprise (manufacturing, retail, logistics, BFSI field forces) and despite the OSHWC Code having just restructured it. Missing entirely: contractor onboarding, licence and registration tracking, principal-employer liability for contractor PF/ESI, vendor compliance scorecards, and the specialist vendors who own this (Aparajitha, Simpliance, Ascent HR, TeamLease, Quess). This is both a competitive category and a module decision.
- CHANNEL, GTM MOTION AND UNIT ECONOMICS OF DISTRIBUTION — the sweep produced price cards with no idea how anything is sold. Nothing on: Techjockey/SoftwareSuggest as paid lead-gen channels (they appear only as price sources), regional reseller and implementation-partner networks, CA/consultant referral, field sales cost per deal, sales-cycle length by band, average contract value, discount norms, or whether self-serve conversion works at all in India. A product spanning SMB to enterprise needs at least two motions and the PRD has no evidence for either. Related unexplored tell: Keka's published renewal rates are LOWER than its list rates (₹6,999 vs ₹9,999), implying heavy first-year loading — a pricing-architecture insight the sweep records and never interprets.
- INTEGRATION SURFACE — no research on what an India HRMS must connect to. GL/accounting posting to Tally, Zoho Books, SAP, Oracle, NetSuite; bank bulk-payment file formats and APIs (ICICI Corporate Connect, HDFC ENet, Axis, SBI, plus RazorpayX Payouts/Cashfree); NPCI rails; identity (AD, Entra, Okta, Google Workspace); Slack/Teams; expense (Happay, Fyle, Zoho Expense); insurance brokers; and above all Naukri's resume database for the recruiting module. Also nothing on API/webhook expectations — noteworthy given the sweep itself found Pocket HRMS gates API access behind a quote-only 100+ employee tier.
- MIGRATION AND SWITCHING COSTS — the single biggest practical blocker to displacing an incumbent, and it appears nowhere. Mid-year cutover requires opening balances, YTD earnings and TDS already deducted, previous-employer income under Form 12B/12BB, investment declarations mid-cycle, Form 16 Part A/B continuity across two systems, EPF UAN and ESI IP continuity, leave balances and encashment liability, and gratuity accrual. The sweep says implementation friction is the segment's dominant churn trigger, then never researches the mechanics that cause it. This is directly a product spec (a migration engine is a first-class feature, not a services task).
- SECURITY AND PROCUREMENT GATES AS A COST AND TIMELINE — partially covered for enterprise (SOC 2, ISO 27001, RBI outsourcing, the tender's controls) but never costed or sequenced, and India-specific gates are missing outright: CERT-In empanelled VAPT, the CERT-In 6-hour incident reporting directive, MeitY empanelment, GeM (Government e-Marketplace) listing — which is how you sell to any Indian PSU or government buyer and is not mentioned once. ISO 42001 is mentioned only in passing (Alex holds it) and never treated as an AI-governance procurement gate the product should target.
- VERTICAL SEGMENTATION — the entire sweep is horizontal. Indian HRMS buying is strongly vertical: manufacturing (shift, contract labour, factory compliance registers under OSHWC), IT/ITES (bench management, multi-country deputation, Ramco/PeopleStrong's home turf), retail/QSR (high churn, hourly, franchise multi-entity), BFSI (RBI, maker-checker, row-level security — the one vertical actually evidenced), healthcare (rostering, statutory duty hours), education, staffing. Which vertical the PRD picks changes the module priority order completely, and there is no evidence base to pick.
- COMPETITIVE-RESPONSE MODELLING, ESPECIALLY ZOHO — nobody asked what happens when incumbents react. Zoho is the existential case: effectively infinite runway, a suite pull-through motive, and a demonstrated willingness to price at $1.25/user. If Zoho bundles People + Payroll at ₹50 PEPM all-in to defend its India base, the sweep's entire pricing wedge evaporates. Similarly unmodelled: Keka post-₹57M with FY24 losses of ₹80 Cr (a company that must grow into its burn and may buy the low end), and Darwinbox with KKR/Microsoft/Salesforce capital moving down-market.
- OPEN-SOURCE AND ERP-EMBEDDED ALTERNATIVES — Frappe HR / ERPNext is a free, open-source, India-origin HRMS with a working Indian payroll module and a substantial SMB following; Odoo has an India payroll localisation; SAP B1 and Dynamics have HR add-ons. These set the price floor in exactly the band the sweep calls uncontested, and none is mentioned.
- REGIONAL LANGUAGE, WHATSAPP AND LOW-END ANDROID CONSTRAINTS — one passing mention (HROne leads with voice). No research on WhatsApp Business API pricing and template limits (which materially constrain any AI-agent design for deskless workers), regional language coverage expectations, offline-first behaviour, or device/data constraints for frontline India. For an AI-first product targeting Indian workforces this is a first-order design input.
- LABOUR WELFARE FUND — the statutory dimension covers PF, ESI, PT, TDS and gratuity in real depth but LWF does not appear in the visible output. It is a separate ~10–16 state variant with its own rates, periodicities (mostly half-yearly/annual), employer/employee splits and returns. If it is genuinely absent, that is a hole in an otherwise excellent dimension.
- MULTI-ENTITY / MULTI-REGISTRATION DATA MODEL — the sweep captures per-state PT variation but never frames the structural requirement: multiple GSTINs, multiple PF establishment codes, multiple ESI sub-codes, multiple shops-and-establishment registrations, inter-entity employee transfers with continuity of service for gratuity, and consolidated group reporting. This is a data-model decision made once and never cheaply reversed, and it is the actual reason Indian mid-market buyers churn out of tools built for single-entity companies.
- THE 'AI LAYER' MAY NOT BE A PRODUCT SURFACE AT ALL — no dimension treats Microsoft (Copilot/Viva), Google, Slack or Glean as competitors for the assistant layer, even though the sweep records Darwinbox shipping Cortex INTO Teams/Copilot/Slack/Glean and both Darwinbox and Keka building MCP servers. If the enterprise buyer's assistant is Copilot, the HRMS becomes an MCP data source and the AI differentiation collapses to data quality and tool design. That is a strategic scenario the PRD must address and the sweep never raises.

## competitors_not_covered

- Tally Solutions (TallyPrime payroll) — the real incumbent in Indian sub-50 payroll, installed in millions of SMBs and operated by the company's accountant/CA. Its absence invalidates the sweep's sub-50 'uncontested lane' claim until tested.
- Frappe HR / ERPNext — free, open-source, India-origin, with an Indian payroll module (PF/ESI/PT/gratuity). Sets the price floor in the SMB band.
- Odoo (India payroll localisation), Busy Accounting, Marg ERP — accounting/ERP suites that own the SMB relationship and bolt on payroll.
- Legacy desktop/on-prem India payroll installed base: Saral PayPack, Spine HRMS/Payroll, Sensys, Relyon, HRMantra, Wallet HR, Beehive HRMS, EmpXtrack, Paybooks, HRStop, Opportune HR, uKnowva. Low-visibility online but large in aggregate and the actual displacement target.
- Akrivia HCM — India-origin enterprise HCM competing directly with Darwinbox/PeopleStrong; a clear omission from the india-enterprise dimension.
- Hono (hono.ai) — India AI-first HRMS positioned exactly where the PRD wants to be; absent from both AI dimensions.
- inFeedo (Amber) — India's best-known conversational HR AI with named enterprise logos; the most significant omission in ai-in-hr-2026 given the dimension's own complaint that no India vendor attaches AI claims to named customers.
- Peoplebox — India-origin performance/OKR platform that has repositioned to AI recruiting; a live wedge-from-a-point-solution case study and a direct threat to the performance module.
- Mesh, Leapsome, 15Five, Culture Amp — performance/engagement point solutions; only Lattice and Betterworks were covered, so the 'performance-first vendors can't retrofit an HRIS' thesis rests on a single data point.
- Vantage Circle, Xoxoday/Empuls, Advantage Club — India-origin rewards/recognition, often the first HR SaaS a mid-market Indian company buys and a plausible wedge upward.
- Zaggle (LISTED, audited financials), Pluxee/Sodexo, Zeta, Happay — FBP/benefits card issuers. Zaggle in particular is the only public-market read available on how large the India benefits/FBP attach actually is.
- Plum, Onsurity, Nova Benefits, Loop Health — group health insurance platforms that own the HR relationship in Indian startups and mid-market and are moving into HR workflows.
- Refyne, Jify, KarmaLife — earned wage access; a monetisation and retention layer several HRMS players are attaching.
- Naukri / Info Edge (Naukri RMS, Zwayam, Naukri 360, iimjobs, hirist), Foundit, LinkedIn Talent Solutions — Info Edge owns the Indian candidate graph. Any recruiting module without Naukri resume-database integration is unsellable, and Naukri's own ATS competes. Completely absent.
- India/APAC ATS and assessment vendors: Turbohire, Talview, InCruiter, Hyreo, Sense (Skillate), Ceipal, HackerEarth, HackerRank, Mettl/SHL, iMocha — the recruiting competitive set was effectively unresearched outside AI-interviewing novelties.
- Payroll and compliance outsourcing: ADP India, Excelity (Dayforce), Neeyamo, Paysquare, Alight, Aon, Accenture/Genpact payroll BPO, Ascent HR. A large share of Indian mid-market and enterprise payroll is BPO, not SaaS — this determines whether the product sells software or software-plus-service.
- Statutory compliance specialists: Aparajitha, Simpliance, Lawrbit, ComplyHR — they own the Labour Codes narrative the sweep calls the top GTM wedge, and none was checked.
- Staffing / contract-labour majors: TeamLease, Quess, Randstad India, Adecco, Gi Group — several sell payroll and compliance software/services and control large captive headcount.
- Attendance hardware and workforce-management: eSSL, Matrix Comsec, ZKTeco, Realtime, Mantra, Secureye, Star Link (most bundle their own attendance software), plus Truein (India face-attendance for contractual/distributed staff) and UKG/Kronos, which is the incumbent time-and-attendance system in a lot of Indian manufacturing and is missing from the enterprise dimension entirely.
- L&D/LMS: Disprz (India), upGrad for Business, Skillsoft, Docebo, Cornerstone, Zoho Learn — flagged only as a gap in RazorpayX's stack, never researched as a category or an AI/skills adjacency.
- India-founded EOR/global-employment players: Multiplier, Skuad, Wisemonk, plus Papaya Global and Velocity Global. The sweep covered Deel/Remote/Rippling as foreign reference but not the India-origin cohort that competes for the same Indian engineering talent and understands the same statutory stack.
- SAP SuccessFactors and Workday as India competitors were only partially covered (both key claims sit at low confidence), and Infor, ADP Vantage/Celergo and Oracle's India regions were not resolved. Microsoft (Copilot/Viva) and Glean are not treated as competitors for the AI layer despite Darwinbox shipping into them.

## unverified_critical_claims

- Keka's actual India price and per-employee rates. The sweep simultaneously holds (a) 'Keka publishes NO numeric prices' [high], (b) ₹90/₹120/₹150 PEPM retracted as a competitor blog's laundered fabrication, and (c) 'Keka's pricing page exposes exactly three per-employee rates — ₹90, ₹120, ₹150' [medium]. This is the price anchor for the market leader and the foundation of the 'price is not a differentiator' thesis. Currently unknown.
- The ₹50–140 PEPM convergence. Every figure is list price at exactly 50 employees, excluding 18% GST, excluding undisclosed setup fees at Keka/sumHR/Qandle, and excluding unquantified annual and half-yearly discounts. There is not one real quote, invoice, won-deal or renewal figure anywhere in the sweep. The most load-bearing commercial conclusion rests entirely on published rate cards.
- Zoho People's India INR pricing and whether Zia is genuinely included from the entry tier. Two dimensions report the pricing page 404s; a third quotes it in detail. Zoho is the most dangerous competitor in the SMB band because of its ability to price to zero, and its actual India price is unresolved.
- Income-tax Act 2025 salary-TDS renumbering — s.392 for salary TDS, Form 138 replacing 24Q, Form 130 replacing Form 16, Form 131 replacing 16A, Form 133 replacing 27D. Self-flagged as [low]: sourced only to uncited vendor content, no CBDT notification located, no government page showing any renumbered form. This is a direct engineering spec for the TDS module. If wrong, the module is built against fiction.
- The claim that the TDS return file format changes and 'every FVU code has been reassigned' [low, explicitly a positioning claim]. Determines whether quarterly filing works at all under the new Act, and whether an existing e-TDS pipeline can be reused.
- Professional Tax slabs. The sweep says PT data is 'MATERIALLY DISPUTED between reputable secondary sources' and that at least one widely cited 2026 table reproduces a superseded structure. There is no verified statewide PT dataset anywhere in the sweep — only the observation that one is needed. PT is named as the largest combinatorial complexity driver and it is entirely unsourced.
- Odisha PT repeal [low] — do not act on it, but it illustrates that even a binary 'does this state levy' question is unresolved for at least one state.
- The EPF/ESI parallel-regime cutover under CoSS s.164(2)(b), expiring on or about 21 November 2026. The saving date is verified; what replaces it is not. No dimension asks whether the saving will be extended, whether ESI schemes under the Code have been notified, or what the engine must do on 22 November 2026 — roughly ten weeks after the sweep's own 'today'.
- ESI district notification coverage — 668 districts, 565 full / 103 partial — carries a 'last reviewed 2025-04-04' stamp. Coverage is determined at district and sometimes sub-district granularity, so this is a live data dependency with no live source and no update mechanism identified.
- 'Support responsiveness and implementation friction is the dominant SMB churn trigger' [high]. This is the #2 opportunity in the sweep and it is sourced from qualitative reading of review-site first pages. No churn rate, no NPS, no ticket-volume data, no time-series. Before a contractual support SLA becomes a core differentiator, this needs a number.
- Every self-reported scale figure: greytHR's four different customer counts across its own properties, Kredily 25,000+, HROne 2,500+ brands / 1,500+ elsewhere, Keka 12,500+, Zimyo 2,500+, Pocket HRMS 2,000+, factoHR 4,000+, Darwinbox 1,400+/4.5M, ZingHR 1,200+/2.8M, PeopleStrong 500+/2M. None audited or filed. Any share-of-market or segment-size reasoning built on these is built on marketing copy — including the reviewer-distribution 'map' of where each incumbent lives, which the sweep itself rates [low] on a 19–25 review page-one sample.
- Kredily's funding and capital base. A free-forever-unlimited competitor with an agentic AI claim and an unknown balance sheet is either a strategic weapon or a company about to reprice. The sub-50 lane thesis depends on which.
- Darwinbox's revenue and growth (all figures [low] or retracted) and Cortex's GA date. You cannot size the India enterprise opportunity or judge whether 'AI-native' converts to ACV without either.
- The entire India enterprise pricing picture. The only surviving PEPM data is HROne's self-labelled 'synthesized estimates' from a competitor blog the sweep elsewhere instructs the reader to quarantine. There is no verified enterprise price point for Darwinbox, PeopleStrong, ZingHR or Ramco.
- Mobley v. Workday's 2026 rulings, particularly the 28 May 2026 ruling on privilege over bias-testing data [medium/unconfirmed]. This determines whether shipping AI screening creates direct vendor liability and whether internal fairness testing is discoverable — a product-design and legal-posture decision, not a compliance footnote.
- Workday's India data centre and SAP EC Payroll's India locale coverage [both low]. These determine whether the global suites are a real threat in India enterprise or structurally handicapped, which changes the enterprise positioning entirely.
- DPDP: whether any HRMS vendor has been designated a Significant Data Fiduciary and which categories the Government has actually notified as localisation-restricted [low]. Determines whether algorithmic due diligence and DPIA obligations are real or theoretical by the ~May 2027 date.
- greytHR NAVOS free-bundling as the India AI pricing anchor [high, but a single vendor]. The ai-in-hr dimension explicitly notes the Zoho half of the 'everyone bundles AI free' thesis was retracted, so the entire pricing recommendation for the AI layer rests on one vendor whose own published customer counts the sweep says are internally inconsistent.

## contradictions

- KEKA PRICE, DIRECT CONTRADICTION. india-smb-midmarket retracts ₹90/₹120/₹150 PEPM as a competitor's number 'laundered into the report as independent fact' and states [high] that Keka publishes no prices at all. incumbent-ai-roadmaps then states [medium] 'PARTIALLY RESOLVED: Keka's pricing page exposes exactly three per-employee-per-month rates — Rs 90, Rs 120 and Rs 150' with ₹90 attached to FOUNDATION. Same three numbers, opposite provenance. Note also that the smb dimension's own ₹9,999/month for 100 users implies ~₹100 PEPM, uncomfortably close to ₹90 — so the retracted figure may in fact be right, which would mean a correct number was destroyed by a sourcing objection.
- KEKA.COM REACHABILITY. india-smb-midmarket ('keka.com blocks direct WebFetch with a TLS error') and ai-in-hr-2026 ('three separate fetch attempts failed on a reproducible TLS certificate error... the single most consequential research gap') both treat the site as unreachable. incumbent-ai-roadmaps quotes the pricing page, the keka-ai page CTAs, the COMING SOON tags, the LLM-information page's April 2026 update date, and verbatim governance copy ('No citation means no answer'). One dimension clearly got in. The 'Keka is unknowable' framing that shapes two dimensions' open questions should have been retired.
- ZOHO PEOPLE PRICING PAGE, SAME SHAPE. ai-in-hr-2026 retracts Zoho AI bundling because 'zoho.com/people/pricing.html returns 404' and india-smb-midmarket says all India-path URLs 404. incumbent-ai-roadmaps states [medium] that Zia is bundled from the ESSENTIAL HR tier, that People Plus tiers list 'AI agent' as included, and that 'no separate AI price and no AI-credit mechanism is published on the Zoho People pricing page' — a claim that requires having read it.
- DATA RESIDENCY, OPPOSITE INSTRUCTIONS. india-enterprise: 'India data residency is the sharpest verified wedge against Darwinbox' and recommends building a contractually committed India region. global-modern-reference: 'DATA RESIDENCY IS NOT THE MOAT IT IS COMMONLY ASSUMED TO BE' and recommends explicitly to 'stop treating data residency as the moat.' Both are half-right — RBI outsourcing rules are a hard BFSI contractual gate, DPDP is a blacklist mechanism not a localisation mandate — but the sweep never reconciles them and a PRD reader will pick one at random. The correct synthesis (residency is a segment-specific procurement gate for BFSI/PSU/insurance, not a general-market differentiator) appears nowhere. Neither dimension mentions SEBI's CSCRF or IRDAI localisation requirements, which would sharpen the same point.
- AI: TABLE STAKES VS UNSHIPPED. india-smb-midmarket: 'an agentic or assistant-branded AI is table stakes across most of the India SMB/mid-market incumbent set.' incumbent-ai-roadmaps: Keka's flagship is on a WAITLIST with no announced date, Darwinbox Cortex is early-access with no GA date and two non-Indian design partners, PeopleStrong and ZingHR publish no availability labels at all, and 'the two most prominent India HRMS AI stories are both unshipped.' The reconciliation — AI branding is table stakes, AI shipping is an open field — is the actual finding, and neither dimension states it as the headline.
- LABOUR CODES RULEMAKING: ANSWERED IN ONE DIMENSION, OPEN IN TWO. india-smb-midmarket lists 'Where did the Labour Codes rulemaking actually land through 2026?' as its most consequential open question and downgrades the December 2025 draft rules as unverifiable. india-enterprise treats commencement as the whole story. india-statutory-payroll has final Central Rules under all four Codes notified 8 May 2026, with the wage conversion formula and re-skilling fund mechanics. The sweep answered its own top compliance question and never propagated it. Same failure on the 50% wages rule: india-enterprise carries a correction note about basic-pay-at-50%, while the statutory dimension has the precise s.2(y)/s.2(88) add-back mechanism and the two-different-wage-bases consequence.
- SUB-50 WEDGE VS STATUTORY THRESHOLDS. The sweep's flagship SMB opportunity is 'a product that runs compliant India payroll with true per-seat pricing from 5 employees has an uncontested lane.' Its own statutory dimension establishes EPF applicability at 20+ employees and ESI at 10+ (Central sphere 20+). At 5–19 employees the compliant-payroll value proposition is largely TDS, PT and payslips. The wedge and the statutory finding are in the same sweep and were never cross-checked. The lane may be empty because the need is thin, not because incumbents are lazy.
- 'PRICE IS NOT A DIFFERENTIATOR' VS TWO PRICE-SHAPED WEDGES. india-smb-midmarket concludes [high] that effective all-in cost clusters at ₹50–140 PEPM so 'price is not a differentiator inside the mid-band', then lists sub-50 pricing honesty and full price transparency as its #1 and #3 opportunities. Both cannot be true. The sweep also admits every figure is list price at exactly 50 employees ex-GST ex-setup ex-discount, which means the convergence finding itself is unproven.
- HRONE QUARANTINE, SELECTIVELY APPLIED. india-smb-midmarket correctly establishes that hrone.cloud's competitor content is marketing and retracts multiple Keka claims sourced to it. india-enterprise then retains HROne's five-band Darwinbox PEPM ladder (₹450/380/340/220/180, bundles ₹480–620, implementation ~1.5x ARR) as the only pricing data for the entire India enterprise segment — from a page the sweep says labels its own numbers as synthesized estimates, written by a direct competitor. It is labelled, but it is still the only enterprise price evidence that exists, which makes the enterprise commercial model unfounded.
- DARWINBOX GARTNER STATUS. india-enterprise retracts the Talent Acquisition MQ Leader claim outright ('the specific newsroom URL returns 404... no 2026 Gartner MQ release of any kind'). incumbent-ai-roadmaps states [high] from Darwinbox's own boilerplate that it is 'a Leader with the top rating for AI capabilities in the Gartner Magic Quadrant for Talent Acquisition' while being a Challenger in HCM. Not strictly incompatible (boilerplate vs press release) but one dimension's retraction is another's verified fact, and no reader can tell which to use.
- SUMHR INTERNAL. india-smb-midmarket states sumHR 'markets no AI capability at all as of September 2026' and, in the same dimension, that agentic AI is table stakes across the incumbent set. Small, but it is the same list contradicting itself.
- GREYTHR AS BOTH UNRELIABLE AND THE ANCHOR. The sweep flags [high] that greytHR's own customer, employee and country counts are inconsistent across four of its own properties — and then makes greytHR the single verified anchor for the entire 'bundle AI free' pricing thesis after the Zoho half was retracted. A vendor whose published facts contradict each other is carrying the load for a market-wide pricing conclusion.
- EOR/GLOBAL VENDORS AS COMPETITORS VS REFERENCE. global-modern-reference concludes EOR economics 'invert in India and cannot be a domestic business model' and treats Deel/Rippling/Remote as reference architecture — correctly. But no dimension establishes whether these vendors compete for Indian-HQ buyers at all (they mostly serve foreign companies employing in India, a different buyer with a different budget). The dimension's conclusions are therefore applied to a competitive set whose overlap with the PRD's target buyer was never established.

Every commercial conclusion in the sweep is list price at exactly 50 employees. This single workstream determines pricing strategy, gross margin, and whether 'price is not a differentiator' survives. Also resolves the Keka ₹90/120/150 contradiction definitively.
- 2. Field-research the actual sub-200 status quo: interview 20–30 Indian companies at 10, 30, 80 and 180 employees plus 5 CAs/payroll consultants. What runs payroll today, what does it cost, what breaks, what would make them switch, and who signs. If the sub-50 incumbent is Excel plus a ₹3,000/month CA, the SMB wedge is a category-creation problem, not a share-shift problem — and the beachhead segment in the PRD is wrong. Nothing else in the sweep tells you whether the demand exists.
- 3. Model AI cost per employee per month against Indian ARPU. Estimate token/inference cost for the intended agent set (helpdesk, payroll anomaly, letter drafting, policy Q&A, screening) at realistic Indian usage, against ₹80–150 PEPM total revenue. Derive the gross-margin envelope and the architecture it forces: deterministic rules engine for statutory, aggressive caching and RAG, small/local models, per-tenant rate limits, or a paid AI SKU. Two full AI dimensions and nobody costed inference. This is the defining constraint on an 'AI-first, India-first' product.
- 4. Pull MCA filings for the whole competitive set — Greytip, Zimyo, Uneecops/HROne, Sage Software Solutions/Pocket HRMS, factoHR, Qandle/MYND, Kredily (PeopleProsper), Darwinbox, ZingHR, Akrivia. Revenue, growth, losses, headcount, auditor notes. The sweep says 'unverifiable' about a dozen times for filings that cost a few hundred rupees each. Settles market sizing, competitor health, who is winnable and who is about to reprice — in about a day.
- 5. Verify the Income-tax Act 2025 salary-TDS spec at primary source: CBDT notifications and circulars, Protean/TIN FVU release notes, TRACES form availability. Section numbers, form numbers, file format, correction-statement enablement date, and Form 16 equivalence. Currently sourced to uncited vendor content at [low]. This is a build spec, and the sweep's own finding that TDS correction statements for TY 2026-27 were still 'enabled shortly' as of 1 Sept 2026 means the government itself may not have shipped what you are building against.
- 6. Build a verified, gazette-sourced Professional Tax and Labour Welfare Fund dataset for all states/UTs: levy yes/no, slabs, gender variants, periodicity, PTRC/PTEC registration, filing frequency, effective dates, and the Maharashtra February top-up class of quirks. Then decide build vs buy vs partner for ongoing maintenance. The sweep proves aggregator PT data is materially disputed and never produced a source of truth. LWF appears to be missing entirely.
- 7. Resolve what happens when the CoSS s.164(2)(b) one-year saving lapses on/about 21 November 2026: are EPF/ESI schemes under the Code notified, is the saving extended, what does the engine run after that date? The sweep dates the cutover and then asks nothing about it. This is the most time-critical unknown in the whole sweep and it lands within weeks of the research date.
- 8. Map the attendance-device ecosystem and decide build-vs-partner: installed base by segment, integration protocols (ADMS/push, SDKs), whether an on-prem connector is required, whether device vendors' bundled software competes, and what share of mid-market deals gate on device compatibility. Attendance is in scope and was not researched at all.
- 9. Quantify the fintech/benefits attach. Read Zaggle's public filings for FBP-card economics; get indicative terms for salary-account referral, group-insurance distribution and EWA partnerships. Determine whether Indian HRMS is a software business or a distribution business. If attach revenue is a multiple of licence, the PRD's pricing chapter inverts — cheap or free software, monetise the money movement — and 'publish a transparent price card' stops being the wedge.
- 10. Establish the Naukri/Info Edge dependency for the recruiting module: resume-database API terms and cost, whether integration is available to competing ATSs, and how Naukri's own products (Zwayam, Naukri 360) compete. A recruiting module that cannot source from Naukri is not a product in India, and this is a hard external dependency that must be settled before the recruiting scope is written.
- 11. Obtain 3–5 more real Indian RFPs/tenders across segments — GeM listings, PSU, large private, one manufacturing, one BFSI — and extract the union of mandatory requirements. The single PSU bank tender was the highest-yield artefact in the entire sweep; the marginal value of a fifth vendor website is near zero and the marginal value of a second tender is very high.
- 12. Answer the scope question the sweep dodged: can one product serve 15 employees and 30,000? Model the enterprise entry path explicitly against the tender gates (₹500 Cr turnover, ₹40 lakh EMD, three years of prior PSU/large-bank implementations, POCs disqualified, maker-checker, row-level security, AD SSO, 99.9% uptime with cash penalties, SI bench). Then decide the sequence and say it in the PRD. On current evidence enterprise is a 3–5 year path that cannot be a launch segment.
- 13. Model Zoho's response. What is the all-in India price of Zoho People + Zoho Payroll today, what is Zoho's strategic incentive to price India HR to near-zero as suite pull-through, and what happens to the business case if it does? Zoho is the only competitor with no capital constraint and the sweep never asks the question.
- 14. Get real retention data instead of review sentiment: full G2/Capterra review corpora with time-series and version tagging, any disclosed renewal or NRR figures, churn interviews with 10+ companies that left an incumbent. Quantify the 'support is the churn trigger' claim before making a refund-backed support SLA a core differentiator — an SLA is expensive and is being justified by qualitative reading of first-page reviews.
- 15. Specify the migration engine. Interview 5–10 companies that switched HRMS mid-year and document exactly what broke: opening balances, YTD TDS, previous-employer income, Form 16 continuity across two systems, investment declarations, EPF UAN and ESI IP continuity, leave and gratuity accruals. The sweep names implementation friction as the dominant churn trigger and never researched its mechanics. Migration is a first-class product surface, not a services problem.
- 16. Research contract-labour and principal-employer compliance as both a module and a competitive category under the OSHWC Code: what mid-market and enterprise employers must track, who serves it today (Aparajitha, Simpliance, Ascent, TeamLease, Quess), and whether it is a wedge or a distraction. Largest headcount pool in Indian mid/large employers, zero coverage.
- 17. Pick a vertical and research it properly — manufacturing, retail/QSR, IT services, BFSI or healthcare — and derive the workflow deltas (shift/roster engines, contract labour, factory registers, bench management, RBI controls). The sweep is entirely horizontal and the module priority order cannot be set without this.
- 18. Test whether the AI layer is defensible at all: if enterprise buyers converge on Copilot/Glean as the assistant and the HRMS becomes an MCP data source, what remains proprietary? Darwinbox already ships Cortex into Teams/Copilot/Slack/Glean and both Darwinbox and Keka are building MCP servers. Decide now whether the strategy is 'own the assistant' or 'own the data and tools the assistant calls' — they produce very different products.
- 19. Assess Frappe HR/ERPNext and other free or open-source India payroll: statutory completeness, adoption, and whether they cap the price floor in the sub-50 band the sweep calls uncontested.
- 20. Build a bottom-up market size to replace the retracted analyst numbers: MCA active-company counts by headcount band, cross-checked against EPFO establishment counts and ESIC coverage, times realistic PEPM and attach rate. All published sizing in the sweep is retracted or [low]; a defensible bottom-up model is achievable from public data and is what the PRD's business case actually needs.
