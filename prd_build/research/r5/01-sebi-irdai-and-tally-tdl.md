### 2. [high] CORRECTED — THE PRIOR ROUND'S HEADLINE RETRACTION IS OVERBROAD AND DANGEROUS. There are TWO distinct compliance lanes, and which one an HR SaaS falls into depends on whether it owns its infrastructure or rides a public cloud. Lane A (own data centres): CSCRF 'Hosted Services' spec, offshore hosting permitted with an India readable copy — this is the lane FAQ Q51 describes. Lane B (riding a third-party cloud, i.e. AWS/Azure/GCP): cloud-circular clause 2(ii) applies and requires MeitY-empanelled underlying infrastructure and MeitY-empanelled + STQC-audited data centres. A typical HR SaaS on public cloud is in Lane B for the 8 cloud-circular RE categories.

The prior report cited FAQ Q51 in isolation. Q47 and Q50 sit immediately BEFORE Q51 in the same FAQ and say the opposite for cloud-riding providers. Q50 verbatim: 'REs must ensure that the CSP uses only MeitY-empanelled infrastructure for all services. Please refer clause 2(ii).3 of SEBI Cloud Adoption Framework.' Q47 verbatim: 'REs must ensure that the CSP uses only MeitY-empaneled infrastructure for all applicable cloud services.' Clause 2(ii) of the circular is headed 'For selection of CSPs offering PaaS and SaaS services in India' — a SaaS vendor IS a 'CSP' for that clause. The two lanes reconcile against the Hosted Service definition's own wording: 'hosted on IT infrastructure either owned or controlled and managed by the service provider'. Satisfiable in Lane B (the major hyperscalers' India regions are MeitY-empanelled) but it is a hard region-pinning constraint, not a soft one.
