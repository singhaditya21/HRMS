# [r5] review-counsel

## lens

Indian technology and employment counsel — pre-release review for statements creating legal exposure, misstatements of law, not-yet-in-force obligations in the present tense, citation/proposition mismatch, reliance risk, and under-warning

## verdict

No — not in this form to customers or investors. Internally this is the most disciplined evidence document of its kind I have reviewed, and I would sign off on roughly 85% of it as written. But there are eleven specific statements I would have to strike or rewrite first, and two of them (§11's treatment of DPDP s.7(i), and §12.3's instruction to state an unverified tax proposition with the citation deliberately suppressed) are the kind that get read aloud back to you. The single structural problem is that the document's credibility rests entirely on its VERIFIED badge, and the badge does not mean what §00 says it means — §12.1 and §13.3 both carry VERIFIED on findings the same paragraphs admit came from third-party mirrors. One opposing counsel spot-check on that collapses the provenance apparatus that is the document's main asset. Fix the eleven, re-badge honestly, add a document-level disclaimer, and I would clear it.

## internal_contradictions

- §11 lists DPDP s.7(i) under 'What binds from day one'; §12.1 establishes that ss.3–17 are not in force until on or about 13 May 2027. s.7 is inside that range.
- §11 says the RBI outsourcing obligations attach 'with no turnover threshold'; §13.3 says they apply 'only where the arrangement is "material" — an entity-by-entity call'. Both carry VERIFIED.
- §00 defines VERIFIED as 'Primary source, survived hostile re-check ... Safe to build on and safe to say out loud.' §12.1 marks VERIFIED a finding whose own footnote says 'The commencement notification was read from a third-party mirror because meity.gov.in and egazette.gov.in return 403'. §13.3 marks the RBI row 'Verified, but from a mirror.' The badge does not mean what its own definition says.
- §00 standing rule: 'No compliance or competitive claim ships to a website, deck or contract without a gazette, notified-rule, filing, regulator or source-repository citation.' §12.3 instructs the opposite: 'State the proposition, omit the section number — it could not be verified at primary source.'
- §07 marks the Income-tax Act 2025 renumbering a HYPOTHESIS and says 'Verify against notified Income-tax Rules before it drives schema.' §05's parity table already uses the renumbered mapping as settled row labels — '24Q / 138', '16 / 130', '12BB / 124' — in a table declared to be from source inspection and official documentation.
- §07 says the Codes are 'a settled central spec'; §04 says 'no state's notified rules were obtained ... we can describe the shape of an obligation but not state one operative rule for one target state'; §20 bans 'Any state Labour Code commencement date'. §04's threshold table nonetheless states operative thresholds flatly, several of which (crèche, canteen, appointment-letter form) are appropriate-Government prescribed.
- §02's band table triggers the Grievance Redressal Committee at '20 employees'; §04's gap list correctly says '20+ workers'. The IR Code threshold counts workers. The same conflation runs through the '10' row (ESI counts persons employed; gratuity and maternity count employees).
- §12.4 instructs 'Do not state the statutory retention period for that record — it could not be verified at gazette level', and §12.6 says retention was 'flagged as needed in three separate dimensions and produced in none'. §8.10 nonetheless states 'Statutory register retention — 5 years, electronic — Central Rules 2026'. Partly reconciled by the 'central sphere' qualifier, but the two read as opposed.
- §12.1 sets the rule 'Write "on or about" and never a single date ... false precision in a document a lawyer reads is a needless own goal.' §07 pairs 'expiring on or about 21 November 2026' with a headline 'APPROXIMATELY 78 DAYS FROM THE RESEARCH DATE' — a day-count off an undefined baseline, which is false precision by another route (from the document's own 5 September 2026 date the figure is 77).
- §09's opening sentence states '₹0.15–3.27 per employee per month ... a 93–99% gross margin on inference' and the box immediately below says 'The absolute per-employee figures are not [load-bearing]. They derive from an engineering estimate ... with no empirical basis.' The headline number is what a reader retains; a 22× range presented as a costing is not a costing.
- §05 asserts 'The incumbents' own published compliance data is wrong' and §19 builds the moat narrative on it, while §05's own 'FOUR CELLS DELIBERATELY LEFT UNRESOLVED' states 'neither product was run, so every finding is declared capability, not verified correctness.'

## missing_sections

- POSH — the Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal) Act 2013. Internal Committee constitution at 10 employees, case register, and the annual report to the District Officer. Absent from §04's threshold table, absent from the 'eight genuine gaps', absent from the entire document — while the Grievance Redressal Committee at 20 is listed as one of the eight differentiating gaps. This is the most conspicuous omission in the file for an Indian HR product, and it sits squarely inside the 'welfare-threshold monitoring' feature §04 proposes to sell.
- Employee monitoring and geolocation. §05 prices a competitor's GPS add-on and §14 designs mobile attendance for deskless workers, but location tracking receives none of the proportionality analysis §12.4 correctly applies to biometrics. The legal question is identical — least intrusive means for an employment purpose — and the deskless module cannot ship without it.
- The MeitY clarification of 24 August 2011 on the SPDI Rules, which addresses whether rules 5 and 6 obligations rest with the contracting entity or with a service provider processing under contract. This materially determines whether the employer or the SaaS owes the written-consent duty for biometrics — which is exactly the question §13.5 lists as open ('Does the SaaS bear direct penalty exposure as a processor?') without noting that the most relevant instrument on it was never consulted.
- Contract architecture implied by the §06 positioning: liability allocation for a rejected or wrong assisted filing (the statutory liability is the employer's and non-delegable), the DPA position while DPDP is not in force, sub-processor list and change-notice mechanics for AI vendors, and the indemnity/cap consequences of §13.5's unresolved processor-exposure question. §13.5 says 'Being wrong is immediately expensive' and then does not scope it.
- Private-employer-binding anti-discrimination statutes beyond RPwD 2016 — Code on Wages s.3, Transgender Persons (Protection of Rights) Act 2019 s.3, HIV and AIDS (Prevention and Control) Act 2017 s.3. §13.1 names only RPwD and frames the rest as constitutional provisions that bind the State, which under-states the position for a product doing shortlisting and performance scoring.
- Contract labour and principal-employer liability. §04 notes the threshold rose from 20 to 50 and §04's whitespace argument turns partly on it, but the product will hold contract-worker records and the principal employer's liability for contractor wage and PF defaults is a real feature and a real exposure. Not addressed.
- A named owner and clearance gate for the document itself. §12.2 proposes exactly this control for marketing copy ('Treat customer-facing legal claims as product surface with a named owner, not as collateral'). Nothing applies it to this document, which is the artefact most likely to be forwarded.

## things_done_well_keep

- §12.2's standing citation rule — 'no foreign decision may be cited without naming its jurisdiction in the same sentence' — and its correct application to the Turkish KVKK 2026/921 biometric decision, including naming the failure mode. This is the single best control in the document and I would lift it into the company's general drafting standards. I looked specifically for unnamed foreign law elsewhere and found none.
- §12.2's REPRESENTATION CONTROL box, which separates misrepresentation risk from compliance risk and forbids describing anything as sensitive 'under DPDP'. Correct analysis and correctly actioned. Extend its scope; do not weaken it.
- §22's pre-agreed response 'Never warrant compliance with provisions not in force. Cite the live regimes instead — CERT-In and the SPDI Rules.' Exactly right, and it is the answer to §11's own defect.
- §12.1's KILLED box on the November 2026 Board-fine claim. That claim does circulate and it is wrong; the s.6(9)/s.27(1)(d) reasoning confining twelve-month commencement to Consent Manager registration is sound, and stating it affirmatively because 'a customer's lawyer may arrive believing the contrary' is the right instinct.
- §12.2 correctly identifying the withdrawn May 2022 UIDAI photocopy advisory as never citable as law while predicting it will appear in customer security reviews. Both halves are right.
- §12.3's precision note — that a face template captured by an employer's own terminal is outside s.29, but a photograph is 'biometric information' under s.2(g), so 'a face template is not biometric information under the Aadhaar Act' must not be written. That is the level of care the rest of the document should match.
- §12.4 and §12.5's product-shape calls: no hard Aadhaar block, a genuine non-biometric attendance path selectable without manager override or penalty flagging, and the reasoning from s.142 being drafted as an obligation on the employee. This is the right answer and it is reasoned rather than asserted.
- §12.4's OSH Code s.33(a) citation for 'labour law requires an attendance record and is expressly device-neutral'. I pulled the Code text: s.33(a) requires a register 'electronically or otherwise' containing 'leave, leave wages, overtime work, attendance'. The citation supports the proposition precisely. Note only that the particulars are 'as may be prescribed by appropriate Government', which reinforces the state-sphere caveat.
- §13.2's mandatory redaction and tokenisation chokepoint on outbound model calls, framed as a structural constraint in the call graph rather than a library developers must remember — with the correct observation that the failure mode is silent. Right control, right justification, right place.
- §20's instruction to forbid candidate-database scraping in architecture and in collateral, naming exposure to the customer as well as to the company.
- Naming the live six-hour CERT-In clock against the imported 72-hour GDPR assumption, and treating a 72-hour runbook as non-compliant today. Correct, and it is the kind of error that otherwise ships in a security whitepaper.
- §12.1's insistence on 'on or about' rather than a single commencement date, and its instruction to pull G.S.R. 843(E) from the e-Gazette before it goes in front of a customer's counsel. Follow that instruction before release — it is the fix for the VERIFIED-badge problem.
