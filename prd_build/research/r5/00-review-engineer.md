# [r5] review-engineer

## lens

Implementation readiness — read as a staff engineer who has shipped payroll, asking whether a team could be pointed at this on Monday and start building the thing the document says is the product.

## verdict

No. Not the product this document is about. I could build a competent gross-to-net engine and a multi-entity core from §8 at maybe 60% confidence on the schema — and that is precisely the commodity §01 and §05 tell me not to compete on. Every part that constitutes the differentiator (the ECR/24Q/ESI/PT artefacts, the state rule set, the compliance data set, the attended filing operation) is either explicitly forbidden to be built by §21 or specified only as a principle. This is an outstanding market and legal brief — genuinely better evidence discipline than most PRDs I have been handed — and it is not yet a product requirements document. It states what must be true about the system; it almost never states what the system does. There are no acceptance criteria anywhere in 982 lines, no release sequence, no scope cut for v1, no screen, no API, no field, no rounding rule. "P0" appears six times attached to six unrelated things with no ordering between them. My honest estimate: 4–6 weeks of specification work plus items 9, 10 and 11 of §21 closed before a team can start, and the §21 items are calendar-bound on other people (EPFO's 404s, state gazettes, counsel), not on us.

## internal_contradictions

- §8.5 says the payroll month is 'immutable after LOCK' with a terminal FILED state; §06 says a filed PF return is now correctable via Revised and Supplementary returns, and §8.7 gives Filing a 'corrected' state. There is no post-FILED correction transition on the month machine.
- §06 places the hard verification gate 'immediately before payment initiation'; neither the §8.5 month machine nor the §8.7 filing machine contains a payment-initiation state. The gate the document identifies as the key REVERSAL has nowhere to sit.
- §01 says portal rejection rate is a headline metric 'measured off the filing state machine in section 8'; the §8.7 filing state machine has no rejected state.
- §8.3 requires universal bitemporality ('every... employee attribute') and identical replay; §12.3 requires a retention ceiling tied to consented purpose and §12.6 requires erase-on-exit for biometric templates and 'no image column, no image bucket'. Never-forget versus must-forget, unresolved, and §12.6 calls its side one-way doors.
- §8.4 requires the engine be pure with 'no I/O, no clock'; §8.6's central money-deciding rule keys PF liability to the disbursal date, an external and later-known event. No injection mechanism is specified.
- §8.4 mandates a DAG evaluated in topological order; §07's s.2(y) add-back re-bases components that feed the add-back test, and gross-up is fixed-point. The DAG assertion is untested against the document's own hardest calculation.
- §12.5 requires Aadhaar optional everywhere including EPF and ESI flows; §06/§8.7 require portal-accepted-first-time artefacts for which UAN/Aadhaar seeding is a precondition. No behaviour specified where they meet.
- §12.6 says consent 'cannot be backfilled'; §15 makes mid-year migration of existing 200-person employers a P0 commercial requirement. Every migrated employee arrives without a consent artefact.
- §02 defers 2,000+ and §17 puts 200+ out of v1 scope; §8.10's availability NFR and §8.8's SAML/SCIM, row-level security and maker-checker requirements are sourced only from the PSU tender for that excluded segment.
- §02 says start ISO 27001 immediately and hold it by month 6; §21 says gate any funding or headcount decision on the validation programme. ISO 27001 by month 6 requires a funded ISMS and staff from month 0.
- §13.3 defaults AI off for regulated tenants; §10 bundles the assistant into the base plan and the architecture is AI-native throughout. No non-AI fallback is specified for any flow.
- §09 rests the margin case on a 52.7× model price spread; §11/§13.2 require residency-selectable backends and establish that residency support varies by provider. The cheap end of the spread is never checked for residency eligibility.
- §09 concedes the gross-margin model is incomplete and must be rebuilt before restating any AI margin conclusion, then retains the 93–99% margin claim in its own header and §16 restates the ₹45–50 PEPM ceiling as though it were unaffected.
- §8.2 introduces Client as a first-class dimension for bureau/CA multi-client use; §8.11 says the pooled-versus-siloed multi-tenancy model is undecided and that 'the CA and bureau multi-client console pushes hard on this'. A schema dimension has been added ahead of the decision that determines its shape.
- §15 line 738 calls Tally import P0 as 'the wedge' (importing customer accounting data), while the §15 migration paragraph treats migration from Tally as the P0. Two different features under one label.
- §8.8 calls the payroll engine 'a library, not a service' and §8.4 makes it pure; §8.8 also puts Compliance data in a separate domain the engine must read. No interface for injecting the rule set into the pure library is defined.
