## Open questions

- Whether payroll registers constitute 'books of account' for Income-tax Act 2025 s.62 and Rule 46. This is now the single most consequential unresolved question, because it gates BOTH the seven-tax-year floor (r.46(9)) AND the India data-residency duty (r.46(8)). I confirmed s.62(1)(b) reaches ordinary businesses above a low threshold, so the only remaining uncertainty is artefact classification, not entity scope. No provision in the Act or Rules classifies payroll registers one way or the other. Salary is a deductible expense, so wage registers are at minimum supporting documents; a conservative engine should treat them as in scope.
- Whether Companies Act 2013 s.128(5) has been amended since enactment. I verified the as-enacted 2013 gazette text (eight financial years, indefinite on an ordered Chapter XIV investigation) but did NOT check it against the Companies (Amendment) Acts of 2015, 2017, 2019 and 2020. Because eight financial years is now the LONGEST floor in the stack for a company employer, this should be confirmed against a consolidation before the number is hard-coded.
- EPF Scheme 1952 retention remains unresolved at primary level (see the low-confidence finding). Compounding this, s.164(1) item 3 of the SS Code — the repeal of the EPF & Miscellaneous Provisions Act 1952 — was NOT commenced on 21.11.2025, so the 1952 Act and its Scheme remain in force in parallel with the Code. A second attempt should target the EPFO's own scheme PDF or the original 1952 gazette rather than the site's HTML.
- The exact commencement date of the OSH (Central) Rules 2026. The gazette masthead and the notification are dated 8 May 2026, but the e-gazette stamp is 09052026 while the Wages and Social Security rules carry 08052026. Since all three commence 'on the date of their publication in the Official Gazette', an employer relying on a same-day cutover has a one-day ambiguity for OSH specifically.
- Whether 'five years' (Code on Wages r.51(4)) and 'five calendar years' (OSH r.72(1)(vii), SS r.53(1)(e)) are intended to differ. The divergence is real, verified and unexplained. Treat all three as five calendar years.
- Two undefined retention cross-references remain unresolved in the notified rules themselves: OSH r.72(7)(ii) requires true copies of lost or destroyed manual originals to be preserved 'for a specified period' with no definition anywhere in the 318-page rule-set (verified by exhaustive search), and IR r.47(2) requires records to 'comply with the requirement of retention of records' with no period or cross-reference at all.
- STATE-LEVEL RULES REMAIN ENTIRELY OUT OF SCOPE. Only Central rules were examined, across both rounds. Employers of 20-200 people are typically also covered by state Shops and Establishments legislation and state rules under the four Codes, each with its own registers and retention periods. The retention engine cannot ship or be sold as complete without a state layer.
- Whether any instrument has been issued since 21 November 2025 repealing the ESI (General) Regulations 1950 under 'the corresponding provisions of this Code' as s.164(2)(a) contemplates. My conclusion that they survive rests on s.164(2)(b) not having been commenced; a later notification commencing clause (b), or expressly repealing the Regulations, would restart a one-year sunset. Worth a standing watch.
