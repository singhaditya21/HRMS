# [r5] review-cfo

## lens

Seed investor / CFO evaluating a funding decision — looking for the point where the business case is asserted rather than demonstrated.

## verdict

No. I could not make a funding decision from this, and the document tells me so itself in §21: "Gate any funding or headcount decision on this programme, not on this document." I take it at its word. What I have here is an outstanding technical, regulatory and competitive due-diligence memo — genuinely better than most Series A data rooms on the compliance and architecture surfaces — attached to no investment case at all. There is no revenue model, no ARPU I am allowed to use (§16 forbids it explicitly), no customer count at any horizon, no cost structure, no burn, no runway, no headcount plan, no team section, no milestones, and no ask. I cannot tell what I am being asked to fund, for how much, to reach what. The one gross-margin number in the document (§9, 93–99%) is computed on the smallest cost line, against an ARPU that the document's own §16 contradicts. Separately, the document surfaces — and then walks past — the fact that market leadership here looks like ₹150–300 Cr of revenue and negative EBITDA (§3). That is the single most important number in the file and it is never confronted. What IS fundable is the §21 validation programme. It has real questions, real methods and real kill criteria. But it arrives with no budget, no duration, no owner and no headcount, so even that cannot be priced. Bring me §21 as a costed, dated, staffed programme with a named team and I will look again.

## internal_contradictions

- §9 vs §16: the AI margin is computed against '₹50–150 ARPU' while §16 states, VERIFIED, that the effective price ceiling at 50 employees is '₹45–50 per employee per month.' The margin claim's ARPU range begins above the document's own ceiling and runs to three times it.
- §2 vs §16: the 10–20 band is declined because 'ACV lands near ₹18,000–30,000,' yet the beachhead's own revenue-carrying floor of 50 employees produces ₹27,000–30,000 ACV at the stated ceiling — inside the range declared uneconomic.
- §21 vs §2/§9/§12.6/§13.2/§15: 'Gate any funding or headcount decision on this programme' sits alongside instructions to commit at month zero to ISO 27001, a CERT-In six-hour pipeline, India log residency, three model backends, the redaction chokepoint, the Aadhaar keyspace and P0 migration importers — several explicitly labelled one-way doors.
- §3 internally: ₹2,100–3,900 Cr of measured real spend against 7.37 crore contributing employees implies roughly ₹2.4–4.4 realised PEPM, an order of magnitude below §16's ₹45–50 list ceiling. Either penetration is ~10% or realised price is ~10% of list; the document uses the list ceiling as its pricing anchor without reconciling the two.
- §10 vs §21 item 7: AI is bundled free and all incremental revenue is pushed into three metered SKUs, while item 7's kill criterion ('If zero everywhere, drop the metered AI SKUs') means every non-base revenue line in the document is unvalidated and disposable.
- §19 vs §8.11: the compliance data set is 'the actual moat' and simultaneously 'nobody has designed the pipeline that maintains it' — a moat with no mechanism, no cost and no owner.
- §2 vs §17: the beachhead is '20–200, with 50–200 carrying revenue,' but §17's motion table prescribes a real assisted motion for 20–50, leaving unclear whether CAC is being spent on a band that by §2's own logic does not carry revenue.
- §0 vs §3/§5/§6: the 70–75% reliability discount on unreviewed round-one and round-two claims is declared and then never applied to any specific commercial number, including the market size that anchors the whole capture argument.

## things_done_well_keep

- §20's banned-numbers register is the most valuable page in the document and I have never seen its equivalent in a fundraising file. Naming the 6-crore-MSME TAM, the $23.32bn press figure, the ₹161 PEPM attach number, the fabricated $400 EOR price and the ₹83.3 FX rate as permanently banned means diligence does not have to go find the inflated numbers — they have already been found and killed. That is real de-risking.
- §3 replaces every retracted analyst figure with 7,66,254 EPFO-contributing establishments and, critically, flags the 3.2× registered-vs-contributing gap. That is a better denominator than most Series A decks carry, and the honesty about the 3× overstatement is exactly right.
- §5's REVERSAL on Frappe HR, from source-code inspection — 549 lines, three overridden functions, zero matches for ECR/EDLI/EPS/UAN/LWF/24Q/12BB/12BA/27A, no Indian state name in the repository — is genuine primary work that reversed a prior conclusion against the author's earlier position. Reversals against interest are the strongest credibility signal in the file.
- §16's refusal to let any of its own numbers enter a financial model is discipline, not weakness. Most PRDs would have modelled off list prices and called it a plan.
- §21's kill criteria are properly specified — '£Under ₹2,000/month collapses the pricing anchor', 'At ~5% Tally is not the incumbent and section 5 is rebuilt', 'If 5× the estimate, free AI bundling stops working at the low end'. Falsifiable, with named methods. This is a fundable programme once it is costed.
- §17's self-indictment — 'no CA has been asked a single question across four rounds. This is the most consequential GTM recommendation in the document and the least evidenced' — is the sentence that most raises my confidence in everything else.
- §2's ISO 27001 seasoning-clock insight (acquisition 3–6 months plus twelve months of seasoning, so month-6 acquisition for month-18 usefulness) is a genuinely non-obvious operational finding.
- §12 and §13's separation of what actually binds today (CERT-In six hours, SPDI Rules, Aadhaar s.38/s.43 criminal exposure) from the ₹250 crore DPDP headline that is not yet enforceable is better than most external legal memos, and the Turkish KVKK citation warning is the kind of detail that prevents a real error.
- §8.10's load-shape observation — near-idle for three weeks, saturated for three days, 'never size on averages' — is the right instinct and has direct cost implications, even though those implications are never costed.
- The four deliberately unresolved cells in §5's parity table, and the refusal to assert TallyPrime ESS in either direction, is the correct handling of genuine uncertainty.
